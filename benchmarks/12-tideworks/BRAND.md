# BRAND — what is fixed, what is open

`EVIDENCE.md` section 7: **nothing is owned.** There is no logo file, no palette, no typeface
licence, no photography and no existing site. So every line below is "open", and the honest
consequence is that almost nothing should be invented — an internal board with an invented
brand is a branding exercise, which the brief names as a fault.

| element | fixed by the client | open | what was decided, and why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| name | **Tideworks** — given in the brief's title | — | used as a wordmark, nothing more |
| mark | — | open | a wordmark in the board's own face plus one drawn glyph: the board's own instrument in miniature — a thin elapsed line, a heavy "now" rule, a solid remaining bar. No roundel, no droplet, no wave. A first attempt drew a lock chamber in plan; at 24px it read as a ladder and said nothing that a competitor's mark could not, so it was replaced by the device the board is actually built from. |
| colour | — | open | taken from the lock's own traffic signal — green, amber, red — because it is the one code every keeper on this cut already reads. See `DIRECTION.md`. |
| type | — | open | one monospace, because every column on this board is a time, a depth or a set of initials |
| photography | — | open, and deliberately not taken | see `ASSET-PLAN.md` |
| voice | — | open | the standing order's voice: imperative, unhedged, no explanation of things an expert knows |

**Scale of the identity.** The mark is 24 px of glyph and one word in the chrome bar, and it
appears exactly once. The brief bans a masthead that takes a third of the first screen; the
answer here is a chrome bar of 56 px — 6 % of a 900 px screen — and no masthead at all.
