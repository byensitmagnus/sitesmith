Sound design & field recording — Oslo

Most of a film’s sound is decided before anyone opens a mixer.

I record on location, usually alone, usually earlier than the schedule wants me to. What comes back is a library specific to the film rather than a folder of stock atmospheres that could belong to anything.

How a job usually goes

Three phases, roughly

Before the shoot. I read the script for sound, not for story, and come back with a list of what has to be recorded on location and what can be built. That list is usually shorter than directors expect and more specific.

During. I record wild tracks in the actual spaces, at the actual times of day. A room recorded at six in the morning does not sound like the same room at two in the afternoon, and the difference survives all the way to the mix.

After. Design and mix, with the director in the room for the first pass and out of it for the second. The library goes to the production at the end, labelled, whether or not I stay on the project.
