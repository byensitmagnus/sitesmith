Incident review, for teams who stopped writing them

The postmortem gets written. It just doesn’t get read.

Aftermath reads the incident channel, the deploy log and the alert history, and drafts the review from what actually happened — timeline first, then contributing factors, then the three actions worth arguing about.

Connect a channel See a finished review

Reads history only. Never posts, never resolves, never pages anyone.

One — the timeline

Ordering the mess is most of the work

Every incident leaves a trail across four or five systems, each with its own clock. Aftermath merges them into one ordered sequence and marks the moments where the shape of the incident changed — first signal, first human, first mitigation, recovery.

You correct what it got wrong. That correction is the review.

Two — the argument

Contributing factors, not a root cause

Incidents rarely have one cause, and the search for one usually stops at the person who pressed the button. Aftermath lists the conditions that had to hold simultaneously, and says which ones you can change.

Action items come out ranked by how much of the failure surface they remove — not by how easy they are to close.
