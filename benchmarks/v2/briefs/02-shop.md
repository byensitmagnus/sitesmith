# Brief 02 — e-commerce

> Handed to the agent verbatim, in both arms. Do not add context to one and not the other.

---

Build the shop for **Fenwick Rope**, a rope and cordage supplier in Bristol. They have sold
to trade for forty years and are opening online to reach smaller buyers: arborists, riggers,
theatre technicians, boatyards and people restoring things.

**What they sell.** Rope by the metre and by the reel, in around 140 lines: polyester
three-strand, nylon anchor warp, dyneema, arborist climbing line, natural manila, and a small
range of hardware — thimbles, shackles, splicing tools.

**Who reads the site.** People who know exactly what they need and want to check a
specification, and people who half know and are afraid of buying the wrong thing. Both care
about breaking strain, diameter and construction. Neither wants a lifestyle photograph of a
sailing boat.

**What they want to happen.** An order. Cut lengths are the majority of orders and the
awkward part: the buyer picks a diameter, a construction and a length in metres, and the price
is per metre with a minimum of five.

**What matters to them, in their words.** "Every rope has a breaking strain and a working
load limit, and they are not the same number. People get hurt when a website is vague about
that. We would rather lose a sale than round a figure."

**Facts you may use.** Founded 1984, family owned, three staff. Stock held in Bristol,
next-day if ordered by 2pm. Cut lengths are not returnable, reels are within 30 days. Prices
excluding VAT because most buyers are trade, with an inclusive figure shown as well. Real
data exists for every line: diameter, construction, material, breaking strain, working load
limit, colour options, and whether it floats.

**Constraints.** No invented breaking strains, ever — if a number is not supplied, the page
says the figure is pending rather than guessing. Product photography exists for about sixty
lines; the rest have nothing yet.

**Scope.** At minimum: a category listing with filtering that reflects how a rigger thinks,
one full product page, and a basket. Home page optional if you argue for it.

Use whatever stack you think is right. It must run as static files served from a directory.
