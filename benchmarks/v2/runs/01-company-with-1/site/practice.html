01

Who we are

A small practice, deliberately

Four chartered surveyors, all regulated by RICS. The surveyor who inspects the building is the surveyor who writes the report and the one who takes the call about it afterwards. Nothing is delegated to somebody the client has never spoken to, and no report leaves the office without a second surveyor having read it.

Around 180 instructions a year is what four people can do to that standard. It is also why we occasionally say no to a deadline rather than say yes and deliver something thin.

02

Standards

Why the reports read the way they do

In practice that means a report structure that does not change between instructions, conclusions tied to the evidence they rest on, and limitations set out where a reader will find them. It also means the report says when the evidence will not carry the conclusion somebody was hoping for.

We are regulated by RICS and work to its professional standards, including the guidance on dilapidations and the pre-action protocol for terminal claims. That is not a badge; it is the reason a report drafted here does not have to be redone in the right format when it reaches a solicitor.

03

Working with us

How an instruction runs

  1. Stage 1

    A telephone call

    The building, the question and the deadline. Usually ten minutes, and there is no charge for it. If the work is not ours we say so during this call.

  2. Stage 2

    Scope and fee in writing

    What we are inspecting, what we are not, what the fee is and what would change it. Fixed once agreed, so a variation is a conversation rather than an invoice nobody expected.

  3. Stage 3

    Inspection

    On site, with access arranged in advance where a tenant is in occupation. Recorded as we go, with numbered photographs that the report refers to by number.

  4. Stage 4

    Report, then a conversation

    The document, and then a call to go through it. Most of the value of a defect report is in the twenty minutes after somebody has read it.

04

Clients

Why there are no client names on this site

We work for commercial landlords, managing agents and asset managers, and much of the work is contentious. Naming a client on a website is a decision for the client, not for us, and we have not asked. If you want references before instructing us, ask on the telephone and we will ask the relevant client first.

The same applies to case studies. We would rather describe the kind of building and the kind of failure — which is what the buildings page does — than publish something identifiable without permission.

Coverage

Leeds, and the region around it

The office is in Leeds. Most instructions are within an hour or so of it — West and South Yorkshire, York and the East Riding, Teesside and Tyneside — and being able to go back to a building a second time without it becoming an expedition is part of how the work gets done properly.

Tell us the building and the date.

Two facts get us started: what kind of building it is, and when you need the report. A surveyor will call you back — you will not be asked to fill in a form.
