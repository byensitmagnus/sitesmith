Defect diagnosis

Something has gone wrong and the accounts of why do not agree. We establish what happened and on what evidence, so the conversation about cost can start from a fact rather than from three opinions.

Talk to a surveyor

01

When to call

The point at which an instruction is usually worth it

  • Water is entering and two remedial visits have not stopped it.
  • A contractor says the design is at fault and the designer says it is the workmanship.
  • Cracking has been monitored, or has not been monitored, and somebody now needs an opinion on it.
  • An insurer has declined, and the decision turns on the cause rather than on the policy.
  • A tenant is withholding, and the dispute rests on whether the defect is disrepair or original construction.

If the cause is already agreed and only the remedy is in question, say so when you call. That is a shorter and cheaper piece of work, and we would rather scope it that way than sell you an investigation you do not need.

02

Method

How a diagnosis runs

  1. Stage 1

    The call, and an agreed question

    Ten minutes on the telephone establishes the building, the deadline and the question the report has to answer. A defect investigation without a stated question expands until the budget stops it.

  2. Stage 2

    The record before the visit

    Drawings if they exist, the construction and maintenance history, previous reports, and what has already been tried. Half of the diagnoses we write turn on something in a file rather than something on the wall.

  3. Stage 3

    Inspection, and opening up where it is needed

    Diagnosis usually means opening up rather than only looking. Where that is not possible on the day, the report says which conclusions are provisional as a result and what would settle them.

  4. Stage 4

    The report, written for people who disagree

    A chronology, the evidence, the cause, and what follows for repair and for liability. Written on the assumption that somebody hostile will read it closely, because in this kind of work somebody usually does.

03

The report

What you receive, and what it is built to withstand

One document, in a structure that does not change between instructions: the question, the inspection record, the evidence, the conclusion on cause, the limitations, and the remedial options with the reasoning behind each. Photographs are numbered plates and are referred to by number in the text.

Every report is written on the basis that it may be disclosed. That governs the tone: no adjectives doing the work of evidence, no conclusion stated more firmly than the inspection supports, and the limitations set out where a reader will find them rather than in a footnote.

04

Limits

What we will not do

We will not write a conclusion the evidence does not support, however clearly a party would like to read it. Where the honest answer is that the cause cannot be established without further opening up, the report says that and prices the next step.

We do not carry out residential homebuyer reports, and we do not take defect work on houses. This is commercial and mixed-use building work: mill conversions, concrete-frame offices, retail parks, listed industrial.
