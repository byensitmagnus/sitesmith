Dilapidations

Advice at lease end, for either side. Schedules that are quantified rather than asserted, and responses that deal with an over-stated claim line by line instead of arguing with it in general.

Talk to a surveyor

01

What we prepare

Four pieces of work, depending on where you are in the lease

  • 01

    Interim schedules

    Served during the term, where the condition of the building is deteriorating faster than the lease intended. Useful when there is still time for the tenant to put things right, which is usually cheaper for everybody than a claim later.

  • 02

    Terminal schedules and quantified demands

    Prepared at or after lease end, with each item tied to the covenant it arises under and costed. A schedule that cannot show its arithmetic invites a response that ignores it.

  • 03

    Responses for tenants

    A received schedule is a starting position, not a bill. We answer it item by item on liability, on the standard of repair the lease actually requires, and on cost.

  • 04

    Diminution advice and negotiation

    Where the landlord’s loss is smaller than the cost of the works — because the building is being redeveloped, relet in shell condition, or stripped by the incoming tenant — that is the number the claim is capped by, and it needs to be established rather than argued.

02

Method

How we work a schedule

  • The lease first: the repairing covenant, the schedule of condition if there is one, licences for alterations, and any yielding-up provisions.
  • Then the building, item by item, photographed and recorded against the covenant rather than against an ideal.
  • Then the cost, from measured work rather than from a rate applied to a wish.
  • Then the loss, because the two are not the same number and the difference is usually where the case is settled.
  • Throughout, the RICS guidance for dilapidations and the pre-action protocol for terminal claims, so nothing has to be redone in the right format later.

03

Timing

When to bring us in

For a landlord, the useful moment is well before expiry — early enough that an interim schedule is still an option and that the terminal schedule is not being written against the clock.

For a tenant, the useful moment is when the exit is being planned rather than when the schedule arrives. What the tenant does in the last six months of a lease usually decides the size of the claim.

If a schedule has already been served and there is a date on it, say that when you call. We will tell you honestly whether we can do it properly in the time.

04

Position

We act for one side at a time

We advise landlords and we advise tenants, and on any given building we do one or the other. The work is adversarial by nature, and a schedule is only worth what the surveyor’s independence is worth.

The same rule applies to the numbers. A claim inflated to leave room for negotiation is a claim that gets discounted on sight, and a response that denies everything gets read the same way.

Tell us the building and the date.

Two facts get us started: what kind of building it is, and when you need the report. A surveyor will call you back — you will not be asked to fill in a form.
