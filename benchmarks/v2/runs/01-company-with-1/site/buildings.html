The buildings we work on

The question we are asked most often before an instruction is whether we have done this kind of building before. Four types account for most of our work, and each fails in a way that is worth knowing in advance.

Talk to a surveyor

01

Nineteenth century

Mill conversions

Solid stone or brick walls built for a use nobody has any more, then cut about for a use they were never designed for: new openings, inserted floors, roof terraces, and services chased through structure that was doing something.

  • Damp read as rising when it is penetrating, or the other way round.
  • Beam and joist bearings weakened by later alterations.
  • Original cast iron and steel hidden behind fire protection, with no record of its condition.
  • Conversion-era workmanship that is now twenty years old and failing on its own timetable.

02

Post-war

1960s concrete-frame offices

A generation of buildings now sixty years old, most of them refurbished at least twice, and many of them holding a cladding or a curtain wall from a different decade than the frame behind it.

  • Carbonation and chloride attack at the frame, and spalling where cover was thin.
  • Cladding fixings and restraint that nobody has looked at since installation.
  • Movement joints filled, painted over, or omitted.
  • Refurbishments that changed the loading or the thermal behaviour without recording it.

03

Retail and distribution

Retail parks

Portal frames, profiled cladding, flat or shallow-pitch roofs, and a tenant mix that changes the internal fit-out every few years while the shell stays the same age as it was.

  • Roof and gutter detailing that fails quietly until a tenant reports water.
  • Fit-out works cut into the envelope without making good.
  • Service yards, drainage and hard standing, which is where the dilapidations money usually is.
  • Landlord and tenant boundaries that are clear in the lease and blurred on site.

04

Heritage

Listed industrial

Industrial fabric with a listing attached to it, where the repair that would be obvious anywhere else needs consent, an argument, or a different repair altogether.

  • Repairs that are technically correct and not consentable.
  • Earlier interventions carried out without consent, which the current owner inherits.
  • Roof structures and coverings where like-for-like is expensive and anything else is contentious.
  • The gap between what a lease requires and what the listing permits.

05

Everything else

If your building is not on this list

These four are what we see most, not a restriction. Commercial and mixed-use work across Yorkshire and the North East is the practice’s range, and if a building falls outside what we can do properly we will say so on the telephone and, where we can, point you at somebody who can.

The one firm boundary is residential: we do not carry out homebuyer reports or house surveys of any kind.

Tell us the building and the date.

Two facts get us started: what kind of building it is, and when you need the report. A surveyor will call you back — you will not be asked to fill in a form.
