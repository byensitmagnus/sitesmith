Pre-purchase building surveys

Commercial property, surveyed before your client commits. The scope is agreed in writing first, and the report is structured so that the people who have to act on it — the purchaser, the solicitor, sometimes a lender — can find what they each need.

Talk to a surveyor

01

Scope

The scope is agreed before anybody goes to site

A commercial survey can be almost any size, and most disappointment in this work comes from a scope nobody wrote down. We agree in writing what is being inspected, what is not, whether services are being covered and by whom, and what happens if something on site turns out to need opening up.

  • Fabric, structure and the external envelope, inspected and recorded.
  • Deleterious materials and asbestos: what we look for, and where a specialist survey is the right answer instead.
  • Statutory and compliance matters visible on inspection, flagged for the lawyers rather than advised on as law.
  • Services covered by a specialist where the deal warrants it, coordinated by us so the reports arrive together.
  • Reinstatement cost assessment where it is asked for, priced separately.

02

The report

Written to be acted on, not filed

A summary that says what the building is and what the material risks are, in the first two pages, because that is what gets read. Then the detail, section by section, with each defect given a cause where the cause can be established, a likely cost bracket, and a timescale.

Then the limitations: what was covered, what was not accessible, what the weather prevented, and what we recommend is done before exchange rather than after. A report that hides its limitations is the one that causes the argument two years later.

03

Deadlines

Deals move, and we would rather say no than be late

Tell us the date you need it by when you first call. Four surveyors and around 180 instructions a year means the diary is real: some weeks we can be on site within days, and some weeks we cannot.

If the deadline cannot be met properly, we will say so on the telephone rather than accept the instruction and deliver something thin. That is the whole of our policy on turnaround.

04

Not this

We do not do residential homebuyer reports

RICS Home Survey Levels 1 to 3 are a different product, for a different reader, on a different fee scale. We do not offer them — not for a house, not for a flat, and not as a favour.

We say this here because we say it on the telephone about twenty times a week, and it is quicker for everybody if the website says it first. A residential surveyor local to the property will do it better than we would.

Tell us the building and the date.

Two facts get us started: what kind of building it is, and when you need the report. A surveyor will call you back — you will not be asked to fill in a form.
