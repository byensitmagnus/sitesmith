Defect diagnosis

Something has failed, it has been going on for a while, and the parties do not agree whose fault it is. We work out what actually happened and put it in writing in a form that survives being taken apart.

What we get called to

Roughly the same six problems, in different buildings, most years.

  • Water ingress through curtain walling that two or three contractors have already been at, each blaming the last.
  • Cracking after adjacent works — groundworks, a new basement, a service trench — where the timing is suggestive but nobody has established a mechanism.
  • Flat roofs failing early, well inside a guarantee that turns out to exclude the thing that has gone wrong.
  • Condensation and corrosion in composite panel roofs, attributed to the tenant's process when it may be a detailing or ventilation failure.
  • Movement following unapproved alterations, typically a mezzanine, a new opening, or plant put on a roof that was never designed for it.
  • Damp in solid-walled conversions where the wrong mortar, the wrong paint or the wrong insulation has been used, and the last three reports disagreed.

Method

1. Paperwork before ladders

Lease and licences to alter, the O&M manuals, previous reports, warranties and guarantees, maintenance records, and where relevant the local weather data for the days things went wrong. A surprising number of disputes are settled by a document nobody had read.

2. Inspection with proper access

Roof level, at height, and inside the construction where necessary — MEWP, rope access or drone as the building requires. A survey carried out from the ground with binoculars answers very few of the questions that matter, and we would rather not do one.

3. Opening up, only where it will answer something

Targeted, agreed in advance, and wherever possible witnessed by the other side. Opening up that has been watched by everybody is very difficult to argue with later. Opening up that has not can be worth nothing.

4. Testing and monitoring where the evidence needs it

Spray and hose testing to CWCT standards for facades and windows, thermography, moisture profiling, half-cell potential surveys where reinforcement corrosion is suspected, mortar and render analysis, and crack monitoring or level surveys over three to six months where movement has to be shown to be live or historic.

5. Then, and only then, an opinion

We do not form a view on the first site visit and spend the rest of the job defending it. Where the evidence supports one cause, we say so plainly. Where it supports two, we set out both and explain what further work would separate them.

What the report contains

  • What we did and what we saw, dated, with the access we had and the access we did not.
  • The mechanism — how water, load or movement is actually getting from A to B, not a general description of the symptom.
  • Cause and contributory factors, separated. Most failures have more than one, and the proportions matter to whoever is paying.
  • An opinion on responsibility where we can properly reach one, with the reasoning shown so it can be checked.
  • Remedial options and budget costs, including the option of doing less than the obvious answer, where that is defensible.
  • What we could not establish, and what it would take to establish it. This section is never empty and we do not pretend otherwise.
“Every report we write can end up in front of a court, so it has to be defensible.” Why the method looks the way it does

When it goes further

A good proportion of these instructions end in negotiation rather than proceedings, and a well-evidenced report is usually what makes that possible. Where matters do go further, we accept instructions as an expert witness and as a single joint expert.

In that role our duty is to the court and it overrides the duty to whoever is paying. We would rather say that at the start of a job than have it come as a surprise at the end of one. It is also why we write the first report as though the second role is already in prospect.

Straight answers

Things we will not do

We will not diagnose a defect from photographs and a phone call. We will not write a conclusion that was decided before the survey. We will not carry out an inspection so limited that any opinion coming out of it would be worthless — if the scope you need is not enough to answer the question, we will say so and let you decide.

We do not carry out residential homebuyer surveys, including defect reports on houses and flats for private buyers. Our work is commercial property only.
