Pre-purchase building surveys

Commercial pre-acquisition surveys: scoped in writing before we start, costed across the next ten years, and written so that your funder, your solicitor and the person who inherits the asset can all use the same document.

At a glance

For
Investors, asset managers and owner-occupiers buying or taking a long lease on commercial property. Funders by reliance.
Scope
Agreed in writing before instruction, in RICS terms of engagement. You know what is in, what is out, and what it would cost to put the outs back in.
Deal pace
Where a transaction is moving, a short written note of anything deal-critical within 48 hours of the inspection, ahead of the full report.
Report
Normally ten working days after inspection, with a costed schedule over one, five and ten years.
Reliance
Available to named funders and co-purchasers where it is agreed before we start, with the liability cap stated up front rather than negotiated afterwards.
Fee
Fixed, quoted after we have seen the address, the age and the tenure. Access plant, drone survey and specialist sub-consultants priced separately and clearly.

Coverage

What a survey covers

  • Structure and fabric — frame, walls, floors, movement, and the condition and remaining life of the envelope.
  • Roofs and drainage — inspected at level wherever access allows, with the coverings' remaining life stated as a range, not a guess dressed as a fact.
  • Building services — a surveyor's overview, with an M&E specialist brought in and co-ordinated where the plant is significant. We will tell you when our overview is not enough.
  • Statutory and compliance — fire safety and the building safety regime where it applies, means of escape, EPC and MEES exposure, accessibility, planning and listed building consents, and any evidence of unauthorised alteration.
  • Deleterious and hazardous materials — asbestos register review, RAAC where the age and form of construction put it in play, high-alumina cement, wood-wool decking, and cladding build-ups that will need investigation.
  • Site, boundaries and neighbours — access, party structures, rights, and anything next door that is about to become your problem.
  • Tenanted investments — schedules of condition, tenant alterations and licences, and the standing dilapidations position, which is frequently the largest number nobody has looked at.

The costed schedule

Every report ends with a schedule of what needs spending and when, split into immediate, within one year, one to five and five to ten, with budget costs at current rates and an allowance stated for preliminaries, fees and inflation assumptions.

Costs are ranges where a range is honest. A single figure carried to the nearest pound on an item we have not opened up is a false comfort, and it is the sort of thing that gets quoted back at you two years later.

What it will not tell you

A pre-acquisition survey is non-invasive unless we have agreed otherwise, and in an occupied building we see what the occupier lets us see. We record what we could not inspect and what we would recommend investigating before or immediately after completion, and we price that investigation so it can be a condition of the deal.

We do not value. Where the survey affects value, that is a conversation for your valuer, and we will give them what they need.

“We would rather turn down work than write something we cannot stand behind.” Including instructions that arrive too late

On short deadlines

We are used to transactions moving faster than anyone would like, and we will work to a tight programme. What we will not do is inspect a building in half the time it needs and issue a report that reads as though we did not.

If the deadline is genuinely too short for the building, we will say so on the first call, and offer the alternative: an attendance and a written note of the deal-critical risks, clearly described as exactly that, with the full survey to follow.
