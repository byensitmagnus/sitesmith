Services

Three services, all of them on commercial property.

Defect diagnosis is the one we are known for and the one that usually brings the other two. If you are not sure which you need, the short version is below.

Start here

  • Something has failed and there is an argument about it. Water ingress, movement, premature failure, a repair that did not work. → Defect diagnosis
  • A lease is ending, or a tenant wants to alter or exit. Schedules, quantified demands, responses, negotiation. → Dilapidations
  • You are buying, funding or taking on a building. Condition, risk, and what it will cost you over the next ten years. → Pre-purchase survey

Jobs cross over more often than not. A defect investigation frequently turns into a dilapidations argument, and a pre-purchase survey regularly finds the defect that becomes the next instruction. We will tell you if you are asking for the wrong one.

The three services in detail

01
What we are known for

Defect diagnosis

Establishing what caused a building failure, on the evidence, when the parties do not agree. Inspection, opening up where it is justified, monitoring and testing where it is needed, then a written opinion on cause, responsibility and remedy.

Typical clients: managing agents, asset managers, landlords, and occasionally both sides jointly.

Read more →
02

Dilapidations

Interim and terminal schedules for landlords, and responses and Scott Schedules for tenants. Costed properly, endorsed as the Dilapidations Protocol requires, and tested against the section 18(1) cap before anyone spends money arguing.

Typical clients: landlords and managing agents at lease end; tenants planning an exit twelve to eighteen months out.

Read more →
03

Pre-purchase building surveys

Pre-acquisition condition surveys on commercial property. Scope agreed in writing first, a costed schedule of repairs over one, five and ten years, and reliance for funders or co-purchasers where it has been agreed in advance.

Typical clients: asset managers and investors, and their funders.

Common to all three

How we quote and what you get

Fees
Fixed, quoted in writing, after a short conversation about the building. We do not price a survey we have not understood.
Who does it
One of four chartered surveyors, named at the point of instruction. Whoever inspects writes the report and takes the calls about it afterwards.
Specialists
We say up front where a structural engineer, M&E consultant, asbestos surveyor or drainage contractor needs to be involved, and we co-ordinate them.
Drafts
You see a draft and we talk it through before anything is issued. Factual corrections, yes. Changing a conclusion we can evidence, no.
Insurance
Professional indemnity cover in line with RICS requirements; certificate provided on request before instruction.
Not offered
Residential homebuyer reports, valuations, and agency. We are a surveying practice only, which is part of why our reports are usable in a dispute.
