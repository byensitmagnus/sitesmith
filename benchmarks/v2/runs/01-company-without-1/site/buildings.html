Buildings

Buildings we know

The usual first question is whether we have done this kind of building before. These four account for most of our work, and each of them fails in ways that are worth knowing about in advance.

Mill conversions

Stone or brick mills converted to offices, studios and mixed use, usually between 1998 and 2008, often with a second round of fit-out since. Handsome buildings that behave nothing like the modern ones around them.

What goes wrong. Solid walls treated as though they have a cavity. Cement pointing and impermeable coatings trapping moisture in a wall built to let it out. Timber beam ends decaying where they are built into damp masonry. Rainwater goods sized for a different century. Steelwork inserted at conversion without adequate bearing. Damp that has been blamed on rising damp, on the roof and on the tenant, in that order, by three previous reports.

What we look at. Mortar and pointing specification, moisture profiled through the depth of the wall rather than read off the surface, beam ends and bearings, gutter and downpipe capacity, and the junction between original fabric and everything that has been added to it.

1960s and 1970s concrete frames

City-centre and edge-of-centre offices, exposed or clad frames, often refurbished twice and re-let more often than that. The single largest source of the water ingress instructions we take.

What goes wrong. Carbonation reaching the reinforcement, spalling to edge beams, columns and soffits, and patch repairs that have accelerated corrosion in the concrete next to them. Curtain walling, gaskets and sealed units at the end of their lives all at once. Movement joints that were filled in during a refurbishment. Asbestos in the fabric and the services. RAAC where the roof form and date make it a real possibility rather than a talking point.

What we look at. Cover and half-cell surveys, carbonation depth testing through a specialist, the history and pattern of previous repairs, flat roof drainage and outlets, and whether the curtain walling can sensibly be repaired or is simply due.

Often a pre-purchase survey →

Retail parks and trade counters

Single-storey terraces and standalone units, 1990s onwards, with a portfolio owner at one end and a national covenant at the other. The work is rarely complicated and frequently contentious, because several tenants share one building.

What goes wrong. Built-up and profiled roofs with failed laps, fixings and sealants. Internal and syphonic gutters that block, overflow and then get blamed on the weather. Tenant penetrations through the envelope for flues, condensers and extract, almost never made good. Mezzanines installed without approval or structural check. Car park surfacing and drainage, which is somebody's liability and usually a surprise.

What we look at. Gutters, outlets and overflows first, then every penetration through the roof and wall, mezzanine loading and its paperwork, and whether the physical demise boundaries match the ones in the leases. They often do not.

Frequently a dilapidations job →

Listed industrial

Sawtooth north-light sheds, engine houses, warehouses and works buildings, listed or in a conservation area, still in use or waiting for a scheme. The technical problem is usually the easy half.

What goes wrong. Patent and north-light glazing decades past its life. Cast and wrought iron corroding at the bearings, where nobody can see it. Brick and stone erosion made worse by repointing in the wrong mortar. Roof coverings that need like-for-like replacement at several times the cost of the obvious modern answer. And repairs carried out without consent, which then become the client's problem and not the contractor's.

What we look at. The listing and the consent history before anything else, including what is actually listed and what is curtilage. Then ironwork at bearings and connections, glazing and its supports, masonry decay mechanisms, and repair options that stand a real chance of consent rather than options that would be tidier.

Everything else

Not on the list

We also work regularly on 1980s and 1990s business parks, city-centre offices, industrial estates, cold stores, and the odd church hall that has ended up in a commercial portfolio. The four above are simply where most of the year goes.

If your building is not one of them, say what it is when you call. If we have not done enough of that type to be useful, we will tell you, and where we can we will suggest someone who has.
