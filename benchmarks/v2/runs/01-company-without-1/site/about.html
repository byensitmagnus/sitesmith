Why we are like this

Small on purpose

Four surveyors is enough to cover holidays, to have someone to argue a diagnosis with before it goes out, and to take on a portfolio instruction without dropping everything else. It is not enough to have a department that writes reports nobody senior has read. That trade is deliberate.

The person who inspects your building writes the report, signs it, and takes the call about it eighteen months later when it matters. There is no team of graduates behind a partner's name on the cover.

Most of our work comes by recommendation

Nearly all of it, in fact, from managing agents and asset managers who have used us before or been on the other side of one of our reports. We are told the second route happens more often than we would expect, which we take as the better compliment.

We are not the cheapest

We are usually not the cheapest quote on a job and we do not try to be. A cheap survey that cannot support a conclusion costs more than an expensive one that can, and it costs it later, in front of more people.

“We are not the cheapest and we are not trying to be. Every report we write can end up in front of a court, so it has to be defensible. We would rather turn down work than write something we cannot stand behind.” The practice, in its own words

What turning work down looks like

It is less dramatic than it sounds, and it happens perhaps a dozen times a year. Usually it is a scope too narrow to reach a safe view — a diagnosis expected from a two-hour visit to a building that needs opening up, or a survey squeezed into a deadline that does not fit the building.

Occasionally it is an instruction where the conclusion has already been decided and the survey is meant to arrive at it. We say no to those on the phone, politely, and it very rarely damages the relationship. The people who ask usually understand why.

The four of us

Who you will be dealing with

All four of us are chartered building surveyors and members of the RICS, with between twelve and thirty years each in commercial building surveying. Between us we cover defect investigation and expert work, dilapidations for both landlords and tenants, pre-acquisition surveying, and historic and listed fabric.

You will be told at the point of instruction which of us is doing your job, and you will deal with that person throughout. If they are on site or in a hearing, one of the other three will know enough about the file to be useful rather than apologetic.

Regulation and standards

  • An RICS-regulated firm. Our work is carried out to RICS professional standards, and our terms of engagement are issued in writing before we start.
  • Professional indemnity insurance in line with RICS requirements. We will send the certificate before instruction if you need it for your file.
  • Complaints handling procedure. We have one, as RICS requires, with independent redress if we cannot resolve something between us. Ask and we will send it.
  • Expert witness work undertaken in accordance with the RICS practice statement and CPR Part 35, including single joint expert instructions.

Clear limits

What we do not do

We say this on the phone roughly twenty times a week, so it may as well be on the website: we do not carry out residential homebuyer surveys. No RICS Home Survey Levels 1 to 3, no reports for private house or flat purchases, no help with the crack in your kitchen wall. If you call us about one, we will say so in the first minute and try to point you somewhere sensible rather than waste your afternoon.

We also do not value, and we do not do agency. Keeping to surveying is part of what makes our reports usable when there is money in dispute: there is no second commercial interest in the building to explain away.

And we do not act for both sides of the same dispute. If we are already instructed on a building, we will tell you on the first call.
