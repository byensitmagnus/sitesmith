01 How we work

Everyone who inspects also writes.

In a lot of practices the building is inspected by one person, written up by another, and signed by a third who has never been there. It is efficient, and it is where most of the weak reports come from, because the detail that matters is the detail that does not survive the handover.

We do not work that way. One surveyor takes the instruction, goes to the building, writes the report, puts their name on the front, and takes the call about it three months later when somebody disagrees. Four people, roughly 180 instructions a year, is what it takes to keep that promise honestly.

02 What we turn down

The work we decline is part of the service.

A practice that accepts everything is telling you something about the quality of its advice. These are the instructions we say no to, every time.

  • Residential homebuyer reports. We do not do them. Not occasionally, not as a favour. If you are buying a house you want a residential surveyor and we will point you at one.
  • Conclusions the evidence will not carry. If a client wants a report that says the contractor is liable and the evidence does not establish that, we will write what the evidence establishes or we will decline the instruction.
  • Deadlines we cannot meet properly. We would rather lose the job than take it, rush it and issue something thin. We will say on the first call whether your date is realistic.
  • Both sides of the same matter. Conflicts are checked before we accept anything. If we have acted for the other party on that building, we tell you and step away.
  • Work outside our competence. Structural design, valuation, M&E condition, contamination. We know the boundary of what a chartered building surveyor should be signing, and we say when you need somebody else.

03 Clients

We do not publish client names.

Most of our work comes from property managers and asset managers at agencies, and most of it is confidential, commercially sensitive or connected to a dispute. Putting a logo wall on a website is easy; getting genuine permission from every client whose name is on it is not, and we have not asked.

So the honest position is this: we will give you references on a call, from clients who have agreed to give them, on work comparable to yours. If somebody recommended us to you, they can tell you more than a logo would have done.

The same rule governs the photographs on this site. They are our own, from our own instructions, and we keep them generic as to location until a client has told us otherwise.

04 Fees and terms

Fixed fee, quoted before you instruct us.

Basis
A fixed fee for a defined scope, agreed in writing before we start. Where a scope genuinely cannot be defined up front — open-ended defect investigations, mainly — we agree a first stage with a fixed fee and review before going further.
What changes it
Additional buildings, access equipment, specialist testing, opening up, and anything you ask for that was not in the scope. All quoted and agreed before it is incurred, never added afterwards.
Reliance
Agreed at the outset, including any assignment to a funder or purchaser. It is much easier to sort out before the report is written than after.
Regulation
Regulated by RICS. Professional indemnity insurance in place; details on request. Complaints handling procedure available on request, with recourse to an independent redress scheme.
Where we work
Leeds office. Site visits across Yorkshire and the North East as standard, and further where a client has an existing relationship with us and asks.
