01 What we do

Three things, and one of them brings the other two.

Most people find us because something has gone wrong and the explanations so far have not held up. That work leads to the rest.

  1. 01 Defect diagnosis What we are known for Water is coming in, the frame is cracking, the roof has been repaired twice and still leaks — and the contractor, the designer and the tenant each blame one of the others. We establish what is actually happening, on evidence, and set out what would fix it. Answers → What is the mechanism · How bad is it · What repair is proportionate · What the evidence supports on cause →
  2. 02 Pre-purchase building surveys Commercial acquisition surveys for buyers and their funders. Structure, envelope, services, statutory and deleterious materials, with a costed schedule of what the building will need and when — written so the deal team can price it, not just read it. Answers → What am I buying · What will it cost me over ten years · What should change the price or the contract →
  3. 03 Dilapidations Schedules and quantified demands for landlords; responses and exit budgets for tenants. Interim and terminal. We act for one side on any given matter and say clearly which side that is. Answers → What is genuinely recoverable · What will not survive a challenge · What the realistic settlement looks like →

02 The report is the product

Written on the assumption that somebody will try to pull it apart.

A surveyor's opinion is only worth what it can withstand. Ours are structured so that a solicitor, an expert on the other side or a judge can follow every step from what we saw to what we concluded.

  • Fact, inference and opinion are separated. You can see which is which on the page, and so can anybody arguing with it.
  • Every finding is evidenced. Photographs referenced to a plan, readings recorded with the instrument and the date, opening-up described rather than implied.
  • Limitations are stated, not buried. What we could not see, why, and what it would take to see it. A report that pretends to certainty it does not have is the one that fails.
  • One name on the front, and that person went. The surveyor who inspected writes the report and answers the phone about it afterwards.

03 Buildings we know

The question we actually get asked is “have you done one of these before?”

Usually, yes. These are the building types that make up most of our year, and each one fails in its own particular way.

Mill conversions
Solid stone and brick, cast-iron and timber frames, and forty years of well-meant repairs in materials the building cannot cope with.
1960s concrete frames
Carbonation, chloride attack, spalling and corroding reinforcement — plus whatever the last refurbishment hid behind the linings.
Retail parks
Long-span portal frames, single-ply and profiled metal roofs, gutters and outlets sized for a rainfall event that no longer applies.
Listed industrial
Repair that has to satisfy the conservation officer as well as the building, and a record made before anybody touches anything.

04 One thing we don't do

We do not carry out residential homebuyer reports. We say so on the phone roughly twenty times a week, so it may as well be on the site.

If you are buying a house, you want a residential surveyor and you will be better served by one. Ring us anyway and we will point you at somebody sensible. Everything else on this site is commercial work: offices, industrial, retail, mixed-use and the awkward converted things in between.
