Service 03 · Landlord and tenant, at lease end

Dilapidations

Schedules and quantified demands for landlords. Responses and exit budgets for tenants. A realistic number early is worth more than an ambitious one late.

Dilapidations claims fail for two reasons. Either the schedule asks for items the lease does not actually cover, or it asks for a standard of repair the building never had. Both are avoidable, and both are obvious to the surveyor on the other side within about ten minutes.

We prepare schedules on the basis that they will be scrutinised by somebody competent who is being paid to disagree. That produces a lower headline figure than some clients expect and a much higher proportion of it recovered.

Acting for landlords

  • Terminal schedules and quantified demands. Prepared under the Dilapidations Protocol, item by item against the covenant relied on, with costs and — where relevant — loss of rent and fees set out separately.
  • Interim schedules. During the term, where the building is deteriorating and waiting until expiry would make it worse or cost more.
  • Negotiation and settlement. Meeting the tenant's surveyor, narrowing the schedule to what is genuinely defensible, and advising you on where the real settlement range sits rather than the opening one.
  • Section 18(1) exposure. We will flag early where the statutory diminution cap is likely to bite, and when a valuer needs to be instructed to answer it. Pressing on without that advice is how good claims get discounted to nothing.

Acting for tenants

  • Response to a schedule. Line by line: what is covered by the covenant, what is betterment, what is fair wear and tear, what is inherent defect and what is simply wrong.
  • Exit budgeting, twelve to eighteen months out. The single most useful thing a tenant can do. You get a number for the provision, and the option of doing works yourself at your own price rather than paying the landlord's.
  • Break clause compliance. Where a break is conditional on repair or reinstatement, we set out what actually has to be done and by when. Failed breaks are expensive and usually preventable.
  • Schedules of condition at the start. Cheapest work we do and the one that saves tenants the most. It fixes the standard you will be held to five or ten years before anyone argues about it.

We act for one side

On any given matter we act for the landlord or for the tenant, never both, and we check for conflicts before we accept the instruction. If we have acted for the other party on that building, we will tell you and decline.

When to bring us in

  1. At lease commencement

    Schedule of condition. Photographic, dated, agreed and annexed to the lease. A day's work that determines what can be claimed a decade later.

  2. Eighteen to twelve months before expiry

    The point of maximum useful choice. Landlords can decide whether to serve interim or wait; tenants can still price up doing the works themselves.

  3. At or after expiry

    Terminal schedule and quantified demand, or a response to one. Still very workable — it just costs both parties more to get to the same place.

What we need from you

  • The lease, including any licences for alterations and their reinstatement provisions.
  • Any schedule of condition annexed at the start, if one exists.
  • The expiry or break date, and any dates already set by solicitors.
  • Whether you are landlord or tenant, and who is advising you legally.
  • Access, and whether the building is still occupied.

Lease end approaching?

Tell us the expiry date and whether you are landlord or tenant. We will tell you what is worth doing now and what can wait.
