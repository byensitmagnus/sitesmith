What to tell us

  1. The building

    Type, town, roughly how old and roughly how big. “Six-storey concrete frame office in Sheffield, 1968, about 40,000 sq ft” is a complete brief as far as we are concerned.

  2. What you need

    A diagnosis, a survey before purchase, a schedule, or an opinion on somebody else's report. If you do not know, say what has happened and we will tell you which it is.

  3. The deadline

    Exchange date, lease expiry, board meeting, court timetable. This is the thing that decides whether we can help at all, so it is worth saying first.

  4. Whether anything is contested

    If a dispute is live or looks likely, tell us at the outset. It changes how the report is written and it affects the conflict check.

  5. A phone number

    We would rather have five minutes on the phone than five emails.

What happens next

  • A call back the same working day, from a surveyor, not a coordinator.
  • A view on whether the work is worth doing and whether your date is achievable — including if the answer is no.
  • A fixed fee and a written scope, usually within one working day of that call.
  • If it is not our sort of work, a name of somebody whose sort of work it is.

Before you ring about a house

We do not carry out residential homebuyer reports, RICS Home Surveys or valuations for mortgage purposes. We are asked about twenty times a week and the answer has been the same since 2004. Ring anyway if you want a recommendation — we will happily give you one.
