§ 01The problem

What usually brings people here

Three situations account for most of what we are instructed on. They arrive looking different and they end in the same place.

  1. A building has failed and nobody agrees whose fault it is.

    The façade is spalling, the roof is letting water in, the floor has moved. The contractor, the developer and the warranty provider each have a reason it is not theirs. Somebody has to establish the cause from the building and put their name to it.

  2. A lease is ending and the schedule does not look like the building.

    You have been served a terminal schedule that reads like a wish list. Or you are the landlord, and the response you have had back quietly writes off half the claim.

  3. Your client is buying and their solicitor will not exchange on a hedge.

    A mill conversion, a 1960s office, a unit on a retail park. The report has to say what the building is, and be specific enough that somebody can rely on it.

All three end in a document that somebody will argue with. That is the document we write.

§ 02Services

Three services, and one of them brings the other two

Most people find us because of a defect. The dilapidations and the pre-purchase work tends to follow from the same relationship.

  • 01

    Defect diagnosis

    What we are known for

    Something has gone wrong and the parties disagree about why. We establish the cause from the building itself — inspection, opening up where the evidence is buried, monitoring where movement is not yet understood — and write it up so it survives being challenged.

    • The cause established from the building, not from the correspondence file
    • Written for the people who will read it next: a solicitor, an adjudicator, an expert on the other side
    • We say plainly when a cause cannot be determined without opening up, and what it would take
  • 02

    Dilapidations

    Interim and terminal schedules for landlords. Responses and negotiation for tenants. Never both sides of the same lease.

    • Schedules of dilapidations, and Scott Schedules where the claim is heading to a hearing
    • Responses under the dilapidations Pre-Action Protocol
    • Where the section 18(1) cap is likely to bite, we say so at the start, not at the end
  • 03

    Pre-purchase building surveys

    Commercial property, before your client commits. The scope is agreed in writing before we start, so the report answers the questions the purchase actually turns on rather than a standard list.

    • Fabric, structure, and the condition the building is actually in
    • Written to be handed to a solicitor without being rewritten first
    • Commercial property only — not residential homebuyer reports

§ 03Buildings

The building you are holding is probably one of these

Four types account for most of our year. Each fails in its own way, and knowing which way saves a great deal of argument later.

  • Type 01

    Mill conversions

    Cast-iron and timber frames, jack-arch floors, solid stone and brick walls that were never built to be dry in the way a lease now assumes.

  • Type 02

    1960s concrete-frame offices

    Carbonation, chloride, shallow cover to the reinforcement, and curtain walling that has outlived its gaskets by thirty years.

  • Type 03

    Retail parks

    Long-span portal frames, profiled cladding, and valley gutters that account for more terminal schedule value than anything else on the unit.

  • Type 04

    Listed industrial

    Where the repair has to satisfy the conservation officer as well as the building, and like-for-like is not a preference.

§ 05Fees and limits

What we cost, and what we turn down

“We are not the cheapest and we are not trying to be. Every report we write can end up in front of a court, so it has to be defensible. We would rather turn down work than write something we cannot stand behind.”

The practice's own position

There is no price list on this page, because a number published against a building type would be wrong for your building. The fee follows the building, the scope and the date. Tell us those three and you will have a figure and a straight answer about whether we should be doing it.

What we do not do

We do not carry out residential homebuyer reports — RICS Home Survey Level 1, 2 or 3. We are asked about twenty times a week and the answer has not changed since 2004. This practice works on commercial property.

Tell us the building and the deadline.

That is enough to start with. If it is not work we should be taking, you will hear that on the call rather than three weeks later.
