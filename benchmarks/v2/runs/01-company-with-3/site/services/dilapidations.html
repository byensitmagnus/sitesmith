Service 02 — landlord and tenant, at lease end

Dilapidations

Schedules for landlords. Responses and negotiation for tenants. We act for both kinds of client, and never for both sides of the same lease.

Talk to a surveyor

§ 01For landlords

If you are the landlord or the managing agent

The claim is only worth what it can be evidenced to be worth. A schedule that overstates gets discounted as a whole, including the items that were right.

  • Interim schedules during the term, where the condition is deteriorating and waiting until expiry would cost you the remedy
  • Terminal schedules at or near lease end, item by item against the covenants actually in the lease rather than the ones you wish were
  • Scott Schedules where the claim is heading for a hearing and the positions need setting out side by side
  • Negotiation with the tenant's surveyor through to settlement, and a clear account of what was conceded and why

Where the section 18(1) cap under the Landlord and Tenant Act 1927 is likely to bite — because the reversionary value is not diminished by the breaches — we say so at the start. It is a worse conversation at the end.

§ 02For tenants

If you are the tenant, or acting for one

Most terminal schedules arrive containing items that are not repairs, items that are betterment, and items the lease never made yours. They are also frequently right about a few things that matter.

  • A response setting out which items are admitted, which are disputed, and on what basis
  • Costings tested against what the work would actually take, not against the schedule's figures
  • Advice on whether to do the work before expiry or to settle in money, which is usually a question about time rather than cost
  • Where the landlord's loss is capped or the premises are to be redeveloped, the argument made early and in writing

§ 03Method

How the work runs

  1. The lease first

    The repairing covenant, the decorating covenant, the yield-up clause, the licences for alterations and whether reinstatement was ever required. Half the arguments are settled here rather than on site.

  2. The schedule of condition, if there is one

    If one was taken at the start of the term it usually decides the outcome. If one was not, we say what that means for the claim.

  3. Inspection and record

    Item by item, photographed and referenced, so each line can be traced to a position in the building.

  4. The schedule or the response

    Served in the form the Pre-Action Protocol expects, with the surveyor's endorsement it requires.

  5. Negotiation to settlement

    Surveyor to surveyor. Most of these settle. The ones that do not settle are the ones where somebody put a number in they could not evidence.

§ 04Conflicts

Which side we are on

Never both sides of the same lease

We act for landlords and we act for tenants, and we will tell you on the first call if we are already instructed on the other side of your matter or anywhere it touches. Tell us the building and the parties when you ring and we can check before either of us goes further.

When a dilapidations claim turns on why something failed rather than on what it costs to put right, it stops being a schedule and becomes a defect question. We would rather tell you that early than price the wrong piece of work.

Tell us the building and the deadline.

The building, which side you are on, and the date the lease ends. That is enough for us to check for conflicts and tell you what it will cost.
