Service 03 — before your client commits

Pre-purchase building surveys

A commercial building survey written for the decision it is going to inform. The scope is agreed in writing before we start, so the report answers the questions this purchase turns on rather than a standard list.

Talk to a surveyor

§ 01Scope

The scope is agreed before we start

A survey scoped by habit produces a report that is long, expensive and silent on the two things that mattered. We agree in writing what is inspected, what is not, and what the report will and will not comment on.

  • Fabric and structure — the frame, the envelope, the roof, the condition each is actually in and what that implies over your client's hold period
  • Statutory and regulatory — the matters visible to a building surveyor, with the point marked clearly where a specialist's advice is needed
  • Deleterious and hazardous materials — what is visible and what the age and construction of the building would lead us to expect, and a review of the asbestos register if one exists
  • Services — commented on to the limit of a building surveyor's competence, with specialist inspection recommended where it is warranted rather than as a routine disclaimer
  • What was not inspected — recorded as clearly as what was

§ 02Method

How the work runs

  1. The brief

    What your client is buying it for, how long they intend to hold it, and what would make them walk away. A survey for a fifteen-year hold is not the same document as a survey for a two-year trade.

  2. The paperwork

    Drawings, previous reports, warranties, the asbestos register, plant records, and any dilapidations history. What has already been argued about tends to be what fails next.

  3. Inspection

    Roof level where it can be reached safely, plant areas, risers, the parts of the building nobody shows you. Access arrangements agreed in advance so the visit is not wasted.

  4. The report

    Findings, then the implications, then the costs — separately, so your client's adviser can see which figures are ours and which are estimates carrying a range.

  5. The conversation afterwards

    The report is not the end of the instruction. Solicitors ask questions and we answer them.

§ 03Readers

Who the report has to satisfy

Your client will read it once. Their solicitor will read it properly, and if there is debt on the deal so will the lender's adviser. It is written for the second and third readers.

  • Costed items separated from noted items, so nothing is double-counted into a negotiating position that will not survive scrutiny
  • Estimates given as ranges with the basis stated, not as single figures implying a precision we do not have
  • Assumptions listed, so the reader can see what would change the conclusion
  • Reliance and the terms of engagement settled at the start, not negotiated after the report has been issued

§ 04Limits

What this is not

We do not do residential homebuyer reports

This is not an RICS Home Survey Level 1, 2 or 3, and we do not carry those out. We are asked about twenty times a week and the answer has not changed since 2004. If your client is buying a house, a flat or a residential portfolio to live in, this is not the firm and we would rather say so before you take the call any further.

Mixed-use and residential investment property is a different question. Ring and describe it.

Nor is this a valuation. We survey the building; the value is somebody else's discipline, and we will say where the two need to meet.

The building types we see most often

Tell us the building and the deadline.

What it is, what your client wants it for, and the date you need the report. That is enough for a fee and a straight answer about whether we can meet the date.
