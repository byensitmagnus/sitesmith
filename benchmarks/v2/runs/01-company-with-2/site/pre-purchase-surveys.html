Pre-purchase building surveys

Commercial property, before you are committed. What the building is, what it will cost to hold, and what has to be resolved before exchange rather than discovered after it.

01Scope

What this is, and three things it is not

A building survey for a commercial acquisition, written to sit alongside the rest of the due diligence rather than to duplicate it.

  • It is a fabric and structure inspection of the whole building

    What is there, what condition it is in, what is going to need money and when, and what any of that means for the transaction you are in the middle of.

  • It is not a valuation

    We do not give you a figure for the property. We give the acquisition team the costs and the timings to take to whoever does, in a form a valuer can use.

  • It is not the mechanical and electrical report

    Where the plant, the incoming services or the lift need a specialist, we say so and say why, rather than writing a paragraph that reads like an inspection and is not one.

  • It is not a homebuyer report

    We do not do those, at any level. Different standard, different market, different firm.

02Coverage

What we look at

  • Structure

    Frame, floors and foundations so far as the available evidence allows, the movement history, and the alterations somebody began and nobody quite finished.

  • Envelope

    Roof coverings and their age against their realistic remaining life, parapets, gutters and outlets, walls, windows and sealants — and every junction between two of those, because that is where the money goes.

  • Statutory and historic

    Listing and consent history where it applies, compartmentation and means of escape as observed, and deleterious materials. Asbestos registers get read rather than assumed to exist.

  • The site

    Yards, slabs, drainage and boundaries — and how much of the building's problem is actually a water problem arriving from outside it.

03The report

The two pages your deal team will actually read

The report is long, because a building is. The front of it is not. Everything found is ranked, given an order of cost and a timing, and put into one of three positions.

  • Resolve before exchange

    Things that cannot be priced without opening up, and things that change whether you want the building at all. Short list, and it is meant to be.

  • Price into the deal

    Known costs with a known timing, in the form your side can take into the negotiation without having to translate it first.

  • Budget for later

    Real, dated, and belonging in the ten-year plan rather than in the completion statement. This is the section that stops the same building surprising you in year four.

That summary is the part that gets forwarded, and it is written knowing that. The detail behind each item — photographs, references, and what we could not inspect — is in the body of the report where the surveyor on the other side will look for it.

04Dates

Working to an exchange date

Acquisitions run on dates that were fixed before anybody thought about the survey. That is normal, and it is manageable, provided the date is the first thing we hear.

  1. Tell us the date before the building

    It decides whether the work can be done properly. If it cannot, you find that out while there is still time to instruct somebody else, which is more useful to you than a report that arrives on the day after exchange.

    You provideExchange or completion date

  2. Access, in one visit if it can be done

    Vacant and occupied parts, roof access, plant rooms, and any tenants who need notice. The most common cause of a late report is a roof hatch nobody could find the key for.

    You provideAccess arrangements and tenant notice

  3. Ranked summary first, full report behind it

    You get the ranked summary as soon as it is defensible, and the full report behind it. The summary does not change between the two unless something on site changes.

05Questions

Asked before instructing us

The vendor has already had a survey done. Is a second one worth it?

It depends who it was addressed to and what it was asked. A report prepared for the vendor is not one you can rely on unless it is formally readdressed or assigned, and it was scoped for a different question. Send it to us and we will tell you whether it needs replacing or only extending.

Can you turn one round in a week?

Sometimes, depending on the building and on the access. What we will not do is inspect less of the building to hit a date and leave that out of the report.

Do you do the valuation as well?

No. That is a separate instruction and a separate competence. Putting both into one document is how you end up with a report that is not much use for either purpose.

What happens if we do not proceed?

The report is yours. The fee is the same whether the deal completes or collapses, and we will not resell it to the next buyer who comes along.

Tell us the building and the exchange date.

What it is, roughly how big, and when you have to be committed by. If it is not work for us, we will say so on the call rather than take it.
