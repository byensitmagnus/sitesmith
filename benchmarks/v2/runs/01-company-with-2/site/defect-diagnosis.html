Defect diagnosis

What is actually happening, why it is happening, and what it would take to put right — written so that the party who disagrees with you has to argue with the evidence rather than with the surveyor.

Start an enquiry 0113 496 0184

01When we are called

Usually after something has already been tried

A repair that did not hold. A contractor who has been back twice. A tenant who has stopped accepting the explanation. By the time a managing agent reaches for a surveyor, the cheap answers have been used up.

  • Water where it should not be

    Ingress that moves, dries up in a mild week, or appears three bays from where anyone has been working. Roof, parapet, curtain walling, below ground. The stain is rarely above the fault.

  • Movement

    Cracking that one party wants to call subsidence and the other wants to call thermal. The distinction decides who pays and whether an insurer is in the conversation at all, so it is worth establishing properly rather than quickly.

  • Concrete and reinforcement

    Spalling, rust staining, patch repairs that have blown again. Common in the concrete frames of the 1960s and expensive to guess at, because the repair specification depends entirely on whether the cause is carbonation or chloride.

  • Something that is about to become a dispute

    The contractor, the designer and the installer each have a version of events. You need one document a solicitor can work from, written by somebody who has no stake in which version turns out to be right.

02Method

How a diagnosis is actually done

Four steps. The third column is what we need from you, because that is usually the thing that holds an instruction up.

  1. We scope it on the phone

    What has happened, what has already been done about it, who is arguing and what the deadline is. If it is not work we should take, or we cannot meet the date, you find out on that call.

    You provideNothing

  2. We read before we climb

    The lease where it bears on liability, previous reports, the operating and maintenance file, drawings, correspondence and repair records. A good deal of what a defect investigation needs is already written down somewhere.

    You provideDrawings, previous reports, correspondence, repair history

  3. We go and look, and open up where we have to

    Every relevant junction photographed and referenced to a plan. Moisture readings with the instrument and the conditions on the day recorded. Opening up only with the building owner's written agreement, and with the making-good settled first.

    You provideAccess, a key holder, and consent to open up

  4. We write the finding

    Cause and mechanism, the evidence for it, the alternatives we rejected and why, what we could not establish, and the remedial options with an order of cost. Then we send it, rather than presenting a conclusion you have not read.

    You provideNothing

03The report

What is in the document

The document is the product. This is what it contains, in this order, whether the instruction is worth four figures or five.

  • The instruction, and the limits

    What we were asked, what we inspected, what we did not inspect, and why not.

  • What we saw

    Numbered photographs referenced to a plan, so that a second surveyor can stand where we stood.

  • What we measured

    Readings with the instrument named and the conditions on the day recorded beside them.

  • The cause, and what we ruled out

    The mechanism, and the explanations we rejected with the reason for rejecting each one.

  • What we could not establish

    Said plainly, with what it would take — and roughly cost — to establish it.

  • What to do, and what it is likely to cost

    Remedial options with an order of cost, and the consequence of doing nothing for another year.

Where a report is commissioned for proceedings, it is prepared in the form Part 35 of the Civil Procedure Rules requires, including the statement of truth and the declaration that the expert's duty is to the court rather than to whoever is paying. Where the findings are going into a Scott Schedule, we set them out item by item so that they line up.

04Limits

Four things we will not do

  • Guess

    If the access, the records or the weather will not let us reach a conclusion we would defend, the report says so rather than picking the most likely story and presenting it as a finding.

  • Write to a conclusion that has already been reached

    We are told from time to time what a report needs to say. That is the one instruction we always decline, and declining it is the reason the rest of them are worth anything.

  • Act for both sides of the same building

    If we are already instructed by one party we cannot take the other, on that building, on that issue. We will tell you who else in the region does this work.

  • Residential

    No homebuyer reports at any level, and no private house defects. This is commercial work only.

05Questions

Asked before instructing us

Can you act if this ends up in a dispute?

Yes, and it is worth telling us at the outset if it is likely, because it changes how the inspection is recorded. Where a report is prepared for proceedings it is written to CPR Part 35, and the duty is to the court rather than to whoever pays the fee.

Will you open up?

Where it is the only way to answer the question. We need the building owner's written agreement and an agreed arrangement for making good before anything is cut, and the likely cost of the opening up is set out with the fee rather than after it.

How quickly can you be on site?

Tell us the deadline on the first call. If we cannot meet it we will say so then, rather than take the instruction and miss it. We would rather lose the job at the start than lose it at the end.

Do you do residential?

No. Not homebuyer reports at any level, and not private house defects. If that is what you need, the RICS Find a Surveyor service will find someone local who does it properly.

Tell us the building and the deadline.

What it is, what has happened, and when you need the report by. If it is not work for us, we will say so on the call rather than take it.
