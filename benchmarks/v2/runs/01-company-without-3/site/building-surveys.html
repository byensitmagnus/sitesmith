Service 03

Pre-purchase building surveys

Commercial acquisition surveys, written to a transaction timetable. The job is not to list everything that is imperfect about a building. It is to tell you what you are taking on, what it will cost over the next ten years, and which two or three items are worth reopening the price over.

01 — The brief

Tell us what you are buying it for.

A survey for a fifteen-year hold on an institutional purchase is a different document from a survey for a two-year trade-out, and both are different again from a survey where the buyer intends to strip the building back and refit it. The building does not change; the questions do.

So the first conversation is about intention: hold period, whether the asset is let and on what terms, whether there is a rent-free obligation to fit out, what the funder needs to see, and when exchange is expected. Everything after that follows from those answers.

What is inspected

  • Structure and fabric — frame, walls, roofs, floors, external envelope, drainage where accessible.
  • Roof coverings, parapets, rainwater goods and abutments, at roof level where safe access exists, otherwise by drone or from adjoining vantage points.
  • Building services, at a level appropriate to the deal: a visual review and records check as standard, with a specialist M&E survey commissioned where plant is significant or ageing.
  • Statutory and regulatory matters visible on inspection — means of escape, compartmentation where it can be seen, accessibility, EPC and, on newer or altered buildings, the documentation position under the Building Safety Act regime.
  • Deleterious and high-alumina materials, and, on 1950s to 1990s stock, an explicit check for reinforced autoclaved aerated concrete. If it is present or cannot be excluded, we say so in terms and recommend a specialist appraisal.
  • Site, boundaries, hardstanding, retaining structures and anything shared with a neighbour.

What is not

  • Asbestos surveying — we review the register and management plan and tell you what is missing, but the survey itself is done by an accredited specialist.
  • Testing of services installations, contamination and ground conditions, and any formal valuation.
  • Legal title, planning history and lease interpretation, though we flag what we see and pass it to your solicitor.

02 — The report

Written to be used, not filed.

Two people read an acquisition survey properly: the person deciding whether to proceed and the solicitor working out what to ask for. It is written for both.

Executive summary
One page. The three or four things that matter, with numbers against them, and a clear statement of anything that should change your view of the price. If there is nothing serious, it says that too.
Condition by element
Each element rated and evidenced, with photographs referenced to a plan. No adjectives doing work that a number should be doing.
Costed 1 / 5 / 10 year plan
Immediate expenditure, medium term, and lifecycle replacement, at indicative rates and clearly marked as indicative. This is the section most often lifted straight into an appraisal, so it is set out to be lifted.
Matters for your solicitor
Warranties and collateral warranties worth chasing, licences for alterations, party wall matters, missing statutory documentation, service charge exposure on a multi-let. Listed as questions to ask rather than legal advice.
Reinstatement cost assessment
Where instructed, as a separate figure for insurance purposes. It is not a valuation and the report says so.
Reliance
Who may rely on the report is agreed at the outset and stated on its face. Where a funder or a co-investor needs reliance, tell us early — it is straightforward before the report is issued and awkward afterwards.

03 — Deadlines

Deadlines in a transaction are real deadlines.

We take on acquisition surveys on the basis that the date is fixed. If we cannot hit it, we say so when you ring rather than a week before exchange. Where the timetable is very short we will offer a verbal headline within twenty-four hours of inspection, followed by the full report — on the clear understanding that the headline is preliminary and the report governs.

Typical turnaround
Inspection within a week of instruction and access being arranged; report five to ten working days later, depending on size and whether specialists are involved.
Access
The usual cause of delay, not the survey. Occupied multi-let buildings need notice to tenants; tell us early and we will start chasing it while the fee is being agreed.
Fees
Fixed, based on floor area, building type and the scope agreed. Specialist sub-consultants quoted separately and never instructed without your approval.

Commercial property only. We do not carry out RICS Home Surveys at any level, and we do not report on houses or flats being bought to live in.
