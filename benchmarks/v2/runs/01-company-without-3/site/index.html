01 — Services

Three things, done properly, for commercial property.

Most of our work arrives through the third one. A building starts doing something it should not, the parties disagree about why, and somebody needs an answer they can rely on in writing.

What we are known for

01

Defect diagnosis

Water where it should not be, cracking, movement, failing cladding, concrete in poor condition. We establish the cause, the extent, and what it will take to put right — in a form that holds up when the other side's surveyor reads it.

Defect diagnosis

02

Dilapidations

Interim and terminal schedules, quantified demands, responses and Scott Schedules. For landlords and for tenants — never both on the same matter. Best started well before the lease ends.

Dilapidations

03

Pre-purchase building surveys

Commercial acquisition surveys against a transaction deadline. Condition, liabilities, a costed plan for the next one, five and ten years, and a clear view of what is worth negotiating over.

02 — Due diligence

What you are probably checking before you call.

Most people who reach this page were given our name by someone they trust, and are now confirming three things. Here they are, without the search.

Is the firm real?
Founded in 2004 and still four people. All four are chartered surveyors, MRICS, and the practice is regulated by RICS. Around 180 instructions a year, which is roughly 45 each — deliberately, because the partner you speak to is the one who goes to the building.
Have they done this building?
Mill conversions, 1960s and 1970s concrete-frame offices, retail parks and listed industrial buildings make up most of our year. What we look for in each type.
Will the report stand up?
Every report is written on the assumption that it will be read by someone paid to disagree with it. Findings are separated from opinion, opinion is tied to evidence, and the limits of the inspection are stated rather than buried. Where we are instructed as an expert, reports are prepared in the form required by Part 35 of the Civil Procedure Rules.
Turnaround
Tell us the deadline when you call. Most defect inspections happen within five to ten working days and the report follows within a fortnight. If a date cannot be met we will say so on the phone rather than after the instruction.

03 — Position

“We are not the cheapest and we are not trying to be. Every report we write can end up in front of a court, so it has to be defensible. We would rather turn down work than write something we cannot stand behind.”

Kestrel Survey — the practice, on how it works

In practice that means we occasionally decline instructions: where the scope is too narrow to reach a safe conclusion, where the timescale would not allow a proper inspection, or where we already act for the other side. It is a short conversation and it is better had at the start.

04 — Instruction

How an instruction usually starts.

  1. You call, or email, with two facts

    The building type and the deadline. That is enough for us to tell you whether we are the right practice and whether we can meet the date.

  2. We call you back the same working day

    Usually within a couple of hours. The person who calls back is the surveyor who would do the work, not an account manager.

  3. A one-page scope and fee

    What we will inspect, what we will not, what the report will cover, what it costs and when you get it. Fixed fee wherever the scope allows.

  4. Inspection, then the report

    Where opening up, monitoring or testing is needed we will say so before it is done, with the cost. Nothing appears on the invoice that was not agreed first.

Tell us the building and the deadline.

No forms with fifteen fields. Call, or send two lines by email, and a chartered surveyor will ring you back.
