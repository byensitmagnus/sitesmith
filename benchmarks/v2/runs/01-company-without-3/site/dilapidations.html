Service 02

Dilapidations

Lease end, for landlords and for tenants. Schedules, quantified demands, responses and the negotiation that follows. The cases that settle badly are almost always the ones that started too late.

01 — Whose side

We act for either party. Never both on the same building.

Roughly two thirds of our dilapidations work is for landlords and managing agents, the rest for tenants. Doing both is useful: you learn quickly which heads of claim survive contact with a competent surveyor on the other side, and which ones simply annoy everyone and delay settlement.

For landlords & managing agents

A claim that will actually be paid

Interim schedules where there is still time for the tenant to do the work. Terminal schedules and quantified demands at lease end. We would rather serve a shorter schedule that stands up than a long one that invites a line-by-line fight over items we cannot support.

For tenants

A response that removes the padding

Review of the schedule served on you against the actual repairing obligation, the state of the premises at the start of the term, and the landlord's real loss. Advice on doing the works yourself before expiry where that is genuinely cheaper, and on exit strategy where it is not.

02 — Scope

What the work involves.

It begins with the lease, not the building. The repairing covenant, the decorating covenant, the yielding-up and reinstatement provisions, any schedule of condition, any licences for alterations and whether the works they permitted were ever reinstated. A great many claims are argued for months before somebody notices that a licence for alterations dealt with the point.

What we produce

  • Interim schedules during the term, where there is time for the tenant to remedy and a genuine risk to the reversion.
  • Terminal schedules and quantified demands, costed item by item, with the covenant relied on stated against each entry.
  • Responses on behalf of tenants, item by item, distinguishing what is admitted, what is disputed on liability, and what is disputed on cost.
  • Scott Schedules where matters proceed, and the negotiation that usually settles them first.
  • Advice on section 18(1) of the Landlord and Tenant Act 1927 — the statutory cap on damages by reference to the diminution in the value of the reversion. Where a diminution valuation is needed, that is a registered valuer's work; we set out the building case and work alongside them.
  • Expert evidence where a claim does not settle, in the form required by Part 35 of the Civil Procedure Rules.

We work to the Dilapidations Protocol and expect the other side to as well. Endorsements are given honestly: we do not sign a quantified demand we would not be prepared to defend.

03 — Timing

Start earlier than feels necessary.

This is the single piece of advice that changes outcomes most, and the one most often ignored.

18–12 months out
Tenants: get the premises inspected and the likely exposure costed while there is still time to do the works yourself — almost always cheaper than paying damages. Landlords: decide whether an interim schedule is worth serving, and start thinking about what you actually intend to do with the building.
12–6 months out
Terminal schedule prepared and, for landlords, usually served. Early service gives the tenant the chance to remedy, which either fixes the building or removes their argument that they were never given the opportunity.
At expiry
Inspection at or immediately after yielding up, with the condition recorded photographically on the day. Evidence gathered later is evidence somebody will attack later.
Afterwards
Quantified demand, response, negotiation. What the landlord then does with the building — refurbish, relet, redevelop — bears directly on what is recoverable, and it is better to know that early than to discover it in disclosure.

Called with three weeks to go? Ring anyway. We can usually get to a building quickly and record its condition, which is the part that cannot be recovered once the keys have changed hands.

04 — Practicalities

Fees, conflicts and who does the work.

Fees
Fixed fee for preparing or responding to a schedule, based on floor area and building type. Negotiation is charged at an hourly rate, agreed in advance, and we will tell you when we think further argument is costing more than it can recover.
Conflicts
Checked before anything about the matter is discussed. If we already act for the other party we will say only that, and nothing else.
Who does the work
One of the four chartered surveyors, named in the fee proposal, and the same person throughout the negotiation.
Coverage
Yorkshire and the North East. Multi-let and portfolio work across several sites is normal for us.
