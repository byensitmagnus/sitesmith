02 — Method

How an instruction runs.

The same shape whichever of the three it is. The point of setting it out is so you know what you are agreeing to before you agree to it.

  1. Enquiry

    Building type and deadline. Two minutes on the phone tells us whether we are the right firm, whether we have a conflict, and whether the date is realistic. If any of those is a no, we say so then.

  2. Scope and fee, in writing, on one page

    What is inspected and what is not. What is assumed. What the report will contain and who may rely on it. A fixed fee where the scope allows, and a clear rate with an estimated range where it genuinely does not — investigations into unknown causes sometimes do not.

  3. Inspection

    Carried out by one of the four chartered surveyors named on the instruction. Roofs, voids and plant areas are inspected where safe access exists; where it does not, we say what we could not see rather than implying we saw it.

  4. Anything further, agreed before it happens

    Opening up, drone survey, monitoring over weeks, materials testing, a structural engineer or an M&E consultant. We will tell you what it would add, what it would cost, and what our conclusion looks like without it. Nothing is commissioned without your say-so.

  5. Report

    Findings separated from opinion. Photographs and, where relevant, marked-up plans. Recommendations with indicative costs and a priority against each. Delivered on the date agreed.

  6. Afterwards

    A call to go through it is included — we would rather spend twenty minutes on the phone than have the report misread. If it becomes a dispute, we can act as expert witness in the same matter provided we were not previously acting as advocate for one party's position.

03 — Limits

Work we do not take.

A short list saves everybody a phone call, and it is the honest version of a capability statement.

Residential surveys
No RICS Home Surveys at any level, no mortgage valuations, no reports on houses or flats being bought to live in. This is the single most common call we take and the answer is always the same. We are happy to point you towards a residential practice.
Valuation
We are building surveyors, not registered valuers. Where a dilapidations claim needs a diminution valuation under section 18(1), that is a valuer's job and we will work alongside one rather than stray into it.
Agency and property management
We do not let, sell or manage buildings. Part of the reason managing agents instruct us is that we are not competing with them.
Asbestos and specialist testing
Asbestos management and refurbishment surveys are carried out by accredited specialists, as is intrusive materials testing. We commission and interpret them; we do not pretend to hold the accreditation.
Both sides of the same matter
We act for landlords and we act for tenants, but never both on the same building. Conflicts are checked at the enquiry stage, before anything is discussed in detail.

If the scope will not support a safe conclusion, we will say so. Occasionally that means declining the instruction. It is a better outcome than a report with a conclusion we would have to walk back later.
