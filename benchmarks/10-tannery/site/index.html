Vegetable tannery · Walsall · since 1878

Six leathers, drawn to scale.

We tan hides in oak and mimosa bark, in pits, over months rather than the day a chrome tannery takes. We sell by the hide to saddlers, bookbinders and two shoemakers. We do not sell to the public.

What separates these six is not colour. It is thickness in millimetres, temper and grain, so here they are: drawn edge on, all six standing on one baseline against rules a millimetre apart, thinnest first.

The rules
One millimetre apart. The height of every strip is its measured thickness at that scale, and the flat run is the part that is on the bench. This much is a measurement.
The overhang
Temper. How far the far end falls past the bench edge is the temper column turned into an ordering, very firm through to very soft. It is not a measurement.
The top edge
Grain. A rhythm cut into the grain side: close and even for a fine grain, long and open for a coarse one. A notation, not a picture of a hide.

The two most binders end up deciding between, a 1.2 mm goat and a 1.4 mm calf, are two tenths of a millimetre apart. Lay any two of the six over each other and the thinner is drawn as a dashed outline on the thicker one’s baseline, so that difference becomes a shape instead of a number to hold in your head.

Why the goat is eleven months

Nine to fourteen months. Nothing changes that.

A chrome tannery takes a day. Bark takes nine to fourteen months, and no amount of ordering earlier changes what happens in the pit. The bark is English oak, from one coppice in Shropshire, and mimosa, imported. There are twenty-eight pits in this yard and nineteen of them are down.

So a lead time here is not a statement about how busy we are. It is a statement about where a particular hide has got to.

How a hide is bought

  • By the hide

    Minimum order is one hide. There is no trade account to open and no minimum spend to reach first.

  • Quoted, not listed

    Every hide is priced individually, by size and by grade, because no two are the same size. That is why there is no price anywhere on this page.

  • Trade only

    We sell to saddlers, bookbinders and shoemakers. We do not sell to the public. If you want one skin for one project at home, this is not the right yard, and we would rather say so now than take the order.

  • Nobody is named

    Two shoemakers and a number of binders buy here regularly. None of them has agreed to be named, so you will find no list of customers on this page and no quotation from one.

  • Thursdays

    The pit yard can be visited by appointment, on Thursdays. It is the best way to settle a question about temper, because temper is a thing you decide with your hands.

  • Oak and mimosa

    Five of the six are tanned in English oak bark from one coppice in Shropshire. The sixth is tanned in mimosa, which is imported.
