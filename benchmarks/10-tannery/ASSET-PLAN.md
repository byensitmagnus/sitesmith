# ASSET-PLAN.md — Falkner & Vane

Written after `EVIDENCE.md` and `BRAND.md`, before anything was drawn.

Four assets, and the reason there are four rather than fourteen: there is no photography of
this subject and none can be made, so every asset here has to be *drawn from a number*. An
asset that cannot name the number it is drawn from does not get made. That rule removed the
yard gate, the bark store, the hides on the horse and the portrait of the tanner before any
of them was started, because each would have been a picture of an idea of a tannery rather
than a picture of this one.

The visitor's job, from the brief, has two halves and both are here: **choose a hide for a
specific binding**, and **know when it can ship**. One asset carries each half. The other two
are the mark and the yard.

## `plate-sections`

- kind: product
- carries: that the six leathers differ by tenths of a millimetre in substance and by a whole
  hand's worth in temper, and that a bookbinder can see the size of that difference instead of
  being told it
- job: choose between a 1.2 mm goat and a 1.4 mm calf for a binding whose spine has to survive
  twenty years
- use: six drawings in one plate, each on the same baseline against the same one-millimetre
  rules, stacked so the eye runs down a single left edge; it opens the page, directly under the
  headline, above the specification table and above everything about lead times
- comparative: yes, six subjects on one baseline at one scale, and any two of them can be
  superimposed on that baseline by the compare control that sits under the plate
- without-it: the page is reduced to a table of six numbers, which is the state the brief calls
  out by name, because two leathers described a screen apart is not a choice a binder can make
- evidence: EVIDENCE.md section 3, "Thickness is the material fact", and the six measured rows
  in BRIEF.md "What is true" (thickness, temper and grain for each of the six)

## `chart-pits`

- kind: proof
- carries: that the lead time on a hide is a fact about a pit rather than a service promise,
  by putting the nine-to-fourteen-month bark window and the six quoted lead times on one dated
  axis where they can be read against each other
- job: know when a chosen hide can ship, and understand why the goat is eleven months when the
  mimosa calf is three weeks
- use: one dated axis from August 2025 to July 2027 with a marked line at July 2026, six lead
  bars from that line, two hatched bars running back to the pit dates that are known, and the
  bark window drawn from each of those two dates; it sits inside the pit section, below the
  yard, and it is the last thing before the enquiry
- comparative: yes, six lead times and two pit windows share one time axis
- without-it: the eleven months on the goat reads as incompetence, which is the failure the
  brief names, because nothing on the page would show that it is the pit and not the office
- evidence: EVIDENCE.md section 8, and BRIEF.md "Lead times, measured from order, as at July
  2026", plus "Bark tanning takes 9 to 14 months and nothing changes that"

## `plan-yard`

- kind: editorial
- carries: how much capacity this actually is, by drawing twenty-eight pits and inking the
  nineteen that are down, so the reader sees that nine are empty and that a pit is a finite
  thing you have to wait for
- job: understand why a hide takes a year before reading a lead time that says eleven months
- use: a small orthogonal count of twenty-eight rectangles, four rows of seven, set beside the
  paragraph about oak and mimosa at the head of the pit section, at about a third of the
  measure so the paragraph still leads
- comparative: no
- without-it: "twenty-eight pits, of which nineteen are in use" stays a sentence, and the pit
  yard stays an abstraction on a page whose whole argument is that the pit yard is the reason
  for everything else
- evidence: BRIEF.md "Twenty-eight pits, of which nineteen are in use", recorded at
  EVIDENCE.md section 1

## `mark-fv`

- kind: brand
- carries: that this is one firm working one yard since 1878, by setting the two names as a
  wordmark over a tally of twenty-eight strokes with nineteen inked, which is the same count
  the yard drawing makes and is a thing no other tannery could wear
- job: recognise the page as Falkner & Vane on a second visit and in a browser tab
- use: in the masthead at the top left at about 26px cap height with the tally beneath it, and
  again at the foot of the page at the same construction and half the size
- comparative: no
- without-it: the header carries a name in the body face and the site has no identity of its
  own, which is the state three earlier builds shipped when the mark was treated as a detail
- evidence: none — brand asset

## What is deliberately not here

- **No hero image.** There is nothing true to put in one. The first screen is the plate.
- **No customer, partner or certification marks.** Nobody has agreed to be named and there are
  no certifications, so the page has no such row, and there is no substitute state for one.
- **No photographic texture, grain overlay or paper backdrop.** Every mark on this page is
  drawn from a measured value; a texture drawn from nothing would be the one decorative thing
  on a page that is otherwise entirely evidence.
- **No icons.** The page has four assets and no set of small pictures standing in for nouns.
