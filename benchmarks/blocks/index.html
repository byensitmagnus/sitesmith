Why the parapet goes first

In twelve years of surveys, the membrane over the open field of a flat roof has almost never been the thing that failed. It is the upstand at the parapet, and it fails for a reason that has nothing to do with the material.

A flat roof is not flat. It moves: thermally, twice a day, and structurally, slowly. The field has room to move. The upstand is pinned between a deck that expands and a wall that does not, and every cycle works the bond a little.

What a specification should say

Most specifications name a membrane and a warranty and stop. The three lines that actually decide whether the roof lasts are about the detail, not the sheet:

  • Upstand height above finished surface, and what happens where it cannot be met.
  • Termination method at the head, mechanical or adhered, and the fixing centres.
  • Whether the deck is checked for moisture before covering, and who signs that off.
The warranty covers the sheet. Nothing in it covers the junction between the sheet and the building, which is where the water gets in.

None of this is exotic. It is in every manufacturer's detail drawings, which is worth remembering when a quote does not mention any of it: the information was available and somebody chose not to price it.

Written from the survey record for 214 flat roofs inspected between 2014 and 2026. The failure distribution is our own and has not been published elsewhere.

Before you book

What if the survey finds more than I expected?

You get the finding and a price, and you decide. We have never started work on the day of a survey, because a quote you have not read is not a quote you agreed to.

Do you work on roofs you did not install?

Most of them. The exception is a roof still inside another contractor's warranty, where our work would void it. We will tell you if that is the case and what it covers.

How long does a flat roof actually last?

Single-ply membrane, laid well, on a dry deck: twenty to twenty-five years. The number in the manufacturer's literature assumes a detail standard that most installations do not meet, which is why we survey the junctions rather than the field.

Can you work in winter?

Yes, with two limits. Hot-air welding needs the deck above 5°C, and we will not lay on a wet substrate. In practice that costs a day or two of waiting in January, not a month.
