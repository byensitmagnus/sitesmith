title: Floor for a surface the visitor operates
read: at build step 4, when the visitor operates rather than decides
---

### The work shapes the screen, and the screen has already sorted

Argue the layout from work you watched, not a menu of dashboard conventions: a
dispatcher watching six things and a clerk working one record do not get the same
screen. Name the job's working unit, a shift's rows or a round's stops, and fit one whole
unit on screen; measurements follow from it. Within a second they know the object,
its state and the number deciding urgency. What needs deciding comes
first, because a screen showing everything equally has sorted nothing. The parts say
what they add up to, compared figures hold their position, and too much data is
reduced by filters they control, never a subset you curated. The rest are shaped
like this one.

Three ways:
- A listing of one whole shift, exceptions on top, totals at foot.
- One consignment full-bleed: what breached first, sums below.
- A map of the round, every stop carrying its own lateness in words.

### The system reports itself over time

Four answers stay live: what saved and when, what is pending and what happens if
they leave, what failed and why and what to do, whether a number is current and as of
when. Partial ranks with empty, loading and error: a half-succeeded bulk action says
which half. Past a tenth of a second a wait is acknowledged locally, never by
blanking the surface, and its finish reaches a screen reader too. Motion reports state
changes only, never scroll, and stops while anyone types or counts.

Three ways:
- Each row reports itself in place, the listing around it still.
- A log stream appending running and finished lines, naming partials.
- A standing panel naming what is saved, what is in flight, what failed,
  and when each figure was taken.

### A control sits at its object, tells the truth, and answers to a key

Row actions on the row, form actions at the end of the form, so nothing is held in
memory en route. Anything that reads as operable is operable. A disabled
control says why, or is not disabled. Destructive actions separate from constructive by
position rather than only colour, and are undoable or confirmed by a sentence naming
what will be lost. Every destination, scroll region and grid is traversable by key and
the key shows on screen. That is not visible focus: a region with no focusable child
passes a focus check and reaches nobody, and an unseen shortcut is unused.

Three ways:
- Inline row controls, arrow-key traversal, deletion apart at the row's end.
- A form ended by its commit, each region a landmark, deletion after it.
- Typed commands documenting themselves, deletion retyping the name.

### Entry is labelled, recoverable and never discarded

Every control carries a text label that stays visible while it is used; a hint that
vanishes on focus is not a label. Errors report beside the field that caused them and
again in one summary the keyboard reaches first, linked to those fields. Work already
done is never discarded to recover. Long entry runs one question group per step, with a
visible position and a review routing back to every answer.

Three ways:
- A grid whose column head is the label, bad cells editable and counted,
  faults listed at the head and linking down.
- One question per screen, position in words, a review linking back.
- A dictated exchange, each question named aloud, faults read back against
  the answer that caused them.

### A colour that carries meaning is spent on nothing else

Used decoratively it stops meaning anything and gets ignored. Verify each signal in
every context it renders in, naming the role of each treatment it needs.

Three ways:
- A near-monochrome console, signal colour only on breach.
- A wholly polychrome board, every colour a status, none spare.
- A printed sheet with no signal colour, state by word and position.
