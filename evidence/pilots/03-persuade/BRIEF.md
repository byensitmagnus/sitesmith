# Vestkystens Frøbank

> **Everything in this brief is fictional.** The seed bank, the people, the collections, the
> numbers and the addresses do not exist and were invented for this pilot. Nothing here may
> be presented as a fact about a real institution, and the built page must say on the page
> that the seed bank is a fictional example.

A page whose entire job is to persuade a landowner to let two people onto their dunes for
one morning in September. Nothing is bought. Nothing is booked. The page ends in a written
enquiry that a person reads.

## Who it is for

Someone who owns or manages a stretch of coastal dune between Nymindegab and Thyborøn:
a farmer with a marginal field behind the dyke, a golf club with rough nobody mows, a
parish council, a summerhouse association. They are not interested in botany. They are
mildly suspicious of anyone asking for access, and they have been asked before by people
who left gates open.

## The problem the page must solve

Every conservation body's page asks for the same thing in the same way: a mission statement,
three photographs of a landscape, and a contact form. The landowner cannot tell from any of
it what will actually happen on their land, who will be standing on it, for how long, or what
they get back. So they do not reply.

**This page must make one morning in September concrete enough to agree to.** Not the
mission. The morning.

## Facts (the only facts; nothing else may be stated as true)

### The seed bank

- Founded 2009. Holds seed from **214 wild plant populations** along 180 km of coast.
- Two collectors do the fieldwork: Aase Kirkeby and Halim Osman. They collect together and
  they are the two people who would come.
- Address: Havnegade 4, 6960 Hvide Sande. Telephone 97 31 44 08.
- The bank is not a public body and holds no right of access. Every collection happens
  because a landowner said yes.

### What one morning actually is

- **Two people, on foot, for three to four hours**, between 07:00 and 12:00, on one date
  agreed in advance. No vehicles past the last gate.
- They carry paper bags, a GPS, a notebook and a folding stool. Nothing is dug up: seed is
  taken from standing plants by hand.
- **Never more than one fifth of the seed on any one plant**, and never from a population of
  fewer than 50 plants. This is the rule the whole method rests on.
- They collect from a maximum of **12 marked spots**, each about 4 m across, chosen on the day.
- Gates are photographed on arrival and left exactly as found. Dogs, none. Drones, none.

### What the landowner gets back

- A one-page map of what grows where on their land, by name, within six weeks.
- Their choice: named as the source in the bank's public record, or not named at all.
- If a population on their land is one of the last three known in Denmark, they are told, in
  a letter, and told what that means for them, which in law is nothing.
- No payment either way. The bank does not pay for access and does not charge for the map.

### The season, which is why this is urgent and only this urgent

- Seed of the coastal species the bank still needs is ripe for about **five weeks**, from the
  last week of August to the first week of October.
- 2026's remaining gaps: **31 populations**, of which 19 are on land nobody has been asked
  about yet.
- A gap missed this year waits a full year. Nothing worse than that happens.

### What the seed bank will not do

- It will not collect without a signed line from the landowner.
- It will not sell seed, and it will not give seed to anyone who will.
- It will not name a landowner who asked not to be named, in any publication, ever.
- It does not claim to be saving anything on its own.

## The one journey the site exists for

**Understand the morning, then send an enquiry.** A visitor reads what would happen on their
land, decides it is acceptable, and sends a written enquiry naming roughly where their land
is and roughly when suits them. That is the whole conversion: an enquiry, read by Aase or
Halim, answered by a person.

No checkout, no payment, no account, no booking calendar, no instant confirmation. The
enquiry's success state must be honest about what happens next and how long it takes: **they
reply within five working days, by telephone if a number is given.**

## Required states

- **Empty.** The enquiry form before anything is typed: it says what it will ask for and what
  happens after, so a visitor can decide before they start typing.
- **Error, on the field that caused it.** A missing location, an unreadable telephone number,
  or a date outside the five-week season, each named on its own field with what would be
  acceptable.
- **Success.** What was sent, who will read it, and when they will answer. Not "thank you".

## What this brief will not accept

- A mission statement above the fold.
- Three cards with icons.
- Urgency that is not the season's. The season is five weeks; nothing else is scarce.
- A form that asks for more than the enquiry needs.
