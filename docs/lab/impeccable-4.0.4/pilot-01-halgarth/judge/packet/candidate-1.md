## Thesis

Halgarth's site is not a brochure with a form bolted to the bottom, it is the plate the rotor gets laid out on. Every fact on it (the machine's hard limits, the price schedule, the day-by-day turnaround, the booking form itself) is scribed and stamped onto one continuous sheet of engineer's-blue marking-out surface, so a visitor reads their own rotor's numbers against Halgarth's tolerances the way a fitter reads a layout table, not the way a customer reads a sales page. It refuses the generic "industrial services" template entire: no stock factory photo, no three-icon "why choose us" grid, no testimonial carousel, no blue-and-orange trust badges. The page owns exactly one idea, true versus not true, shown, not asserted, and never reaches for a second one.

## Subject grounding

The mechanism: Halgarth measures precisely how far a spinning rotor is out of true, removes exactly that much imbalance by hand-grinding, welding and machine correction, and hands back a certificate proving the number before and the number after. The audience: a maintenance engineer or works fitter standing next to a pulled impeller on a pallet in a loud plant room, works phone in hand, trying to answer three questions, in scope, what does it cost, when back, without ringing Kilnhurst and burning fifteen minutes on a refusal. The cultural home is UK light-industrial subcontract engineering: goods-in tags, calibration certificates, machine data plates, ISO tolerance charts, tail-lift dockets, and, closest of all to the actual business, the toolroom surface plate: a precision-ground table smeared with Prussian-blue marking compound, against which a straightedge or scriber shows true in bright contrast against not true. That contrast, true vs. not true, made visible rather than claimed, is the entire business in one material.
Because no photography of the unit, the machine, or either man exists, and none may be implied, this world is a genuine advantage rather than a workaround: everything on the page is flat scribed and stamped graphic material, lines, dots, plates, ink, so the photography ban is never fought, only built around from the start.

## Visual and material system

Colour strategy: Committed. Halgarth Blue is the one saturated colour and it owns 30-60% of every screen, hero, header, nav, and the dividers between sections, the way a real surface plate owns the bay it sits in. Dense reading surfaces (the form, the price schedule, the certificate-style success page) shift onto a pale neutral, the way real paperwork sits pale on top of a blued table, so small type stays legible on a works phone rather than fighting a saturated ground. Dark mode is not offered: the physical scene is a maintenance engineer reading a bright phone screen under harsh plant-room fluorescents or daylight, not a moody evening read, so the whole system is tuned for high-ambient-light legibility regardless of OS theme.
All values below are checked by WCAG relative-luminance formula against the grounds they actually appear on; browser-rendered figures should still be reconfirmed once built (see Honest risk).
Role: Halgarth Blue. Value: #12314A. Taken from: Engineer's/Prussian-blue marking compound, mixed dark. Verified use: Dominant ground: hero, header, section dividers, plate background.
Role: Halgarth Blue Light. Value: #1E4A6E. Taken from: Same compound, thinned. Verified use: Hover/active state on blue surfaces only.
Role: Plate Steel. Value: #EEF0EF. Taken from: Galvanised sheet / aged cartridge paper, kept cool not cream. Verified use: Panel background for form, tables, certificate, all dense reading.
Role: Scribe White. Value: #FFFFFF. Taken from: Bare metal exposed where a scriber cuts through the blue. Verified use: Primary text and rules on Halgarth Blue, ~13.4:1, clears AAA.
Role: Scribe Silver. Value: #C7CCCD. Taken from: Same scribed line, slightly toned. Verified use: Secondary hairlines/witness strokes on Blue, ~8.3:1, safe at any size.
Role: Brass Rivet. Value: #B5762A. Taken from: A data plate's brass rivets. Verified use: Large/bold buttons, icons, focus rings, decorative witness-dots. Never small body text, only ~3.6:1 on Blue, ~3.3:1 on Plate Steel; both fail AA for normal text.
Role: Brass Ink. Value: #8A5A1E. Taken from: Same brass, oxidised darker. Verified use: Any small inline link on Plate Steel, ~5.15:1, clears AA.
Role: Stamp Red. Value: #A1291F. Taken from: Rubber ink-stamp pad, aged. Verified use: Refusal/error states only, always on Plate Steel (~6.43:1). Never reused for a plain call to action, so "clickable" and "refused" never look alike.
Role: Ink. Value: #161B1E. Taken from: Sumi-toned near-black, not pure black. Verified use: Body text on Plate Steel, ~15.2:1.
Material grammar, all built in CSS/SVG, none photographic:
Scribed lines: 1px hairlines, Scribe White/Silver on Blue, Ink on Plate Steel, used for every rule, table row divider, and form-field baseline.
Witness marks: small filled circles (6-8px) at line intersections and field starts, echoing a centre-punch mark, Brass Rivet on Blue, Brass Ink on Plate Steel.
Milled-groove dividers: section boundaries use a paired inset shadow (box-shadow: inset 0 1px 0 rgba(0,0,0,.25), inset 0 -1px 0 rgba(255,255,255,.06);) to read as a shallow groove machined into the plate rather than a flat CSS border.
Stamp marks: the two error states and the "condemned" language use a rotated (−4°), Stamp Red, Big Shoulders wordmark with a slightly irregular edge (SVG feTurbulence-free, built as a hand-authored uneven-edge SVG mask, not a filter, so it stays crisp and reproducible), reading as an inked rejection stamp. Kept small and positioned beside the offending line, never full-width or full-bleed, so it stays as blunt as the copy it sits next to and no blunter.
No gradients, no glass, no drop-shadow halos, no glyph/emoji icons. Every icon (the ATEX prohibition mark, the witness dot, the stamp) is an authored SVG in the plate's own line-and-fill language, one consistent stroke weight throughout.

## Typography strategy

Three real faces, each earned from the subject's own world, not the trained-in defaults. All three are SIL Open Font License and must be self-hosted as local woff2 files, the brief bans third-party font fetches, so no fonts.googleapis.com link; download once, serve from the same origin.
Big Shoulders (Display cut for headings, Text cut for small caps labels), modelled on Chicago's own industrial and civic signage lettering, which is exactly the register a stamped data plate or a plate-side stencil uses. Used for: the hero headline (Display 700, clamp(2.25rem, 5vw + 1rem, 4.75rem), all-caps, letter-spacing 0.01em, line-height 1.05), section headings (Display 600, clamp(1.5rem, 3vw, 2.5rem)), and every stamped small label, field labels, nav, the rating-plate strip's line labels (Text 600, 0.8125rem/13px, all-caps, letter-spacing 0.08em). Fallback stack: "Big Shoulders Display", "Arial Narrow", sans-serif, the fallback is a flash-of-unstyled-text safety net only; the self-hosted face is required, per the craft floor's ban on a system face standing in as an own-world display voice.
Courier Prime, a clean revival of a typewriter face, genuinely rooted in typed official documents: the exact register of a certificate, a carbon-copy docket, or a works order banged out on an office typewriter. Used for every number on the site and nowhere else: prices, dates, kg/mm figures, the job reference HB-2617, the phonetic read-out line, table figures with tabular lining digits. 400 weight for inline figures, 700 for the job reference and any large certificate numeral. This is the craft floor's earned exception to "monospace as a costume", it is used strictly for data and measurement, never as a generic "technical" flourish elsewhere. Fallback: "Courier Prime", "Courier New", monospace.
Public Sans, the plain, humanist grotesque built for U.S. civic digital services, chosen for exactly that heritage: bureaucratic, factual, legible content that has to work for someone in a hurry. Used for all running body copy, form labels, and instructional text, 400/600 weight, 1rem, 1.125rem (16-18px), line-height 1.6, measure held to 65-75ch. Fallback: "Public Sans", -apple-system, "Segoe UI", sans-serif.
Discipline rule for the builder: the three faces never mix inside one text run, Big Shoulders is caps-only chrome and stamped labels, Courier Prime is numerals and dates only, Public Sans is every sentence. Breaking that boundary is what turns three purposeful faces into three unrelated ones (see Honest risk).

## Signature element

The Witness Rule. Beside each of the form's three numeric fields, rotor mass, diameter, journal spacing, sits a horizontal scale-ruler graphic with the field's real limit scribed onto it as a fixed stop-line: a single line for mass and diameter (0 to the maximum), two stop-lines for journal spacing (350mm and 1,900mm, since it's a band, not a ceiling). This is the page's central drama, does your number fit inside our number, turned into the actual input pattern instead of a decoration next to it.
Built as progressive enhancement, static-first:
With JavaScript off (a hard requirement of the brief), the ruler, its tick marks, and the stop-line(s) are plain HTML/CSS, always rendered from the real numbers, mass uses a native <meter min="0" max="900">, which already renders a visual bar with zero script; journal spacing's band uses a plain CSS ruler with two absolutely-positioned stop-line elements. Either way, the limit is "readable next to the field it constrains, before anyone types," exactly as the empty-state requirement asks, with no script required to satisfy it.
With the one permitted JavaScript file (/book/book.js, defer, scoped to this page only), an input listener updates the meter's value and moves a small Brass witness dot along the custom rulers to track what the visitor is typing live. Past the stop-line, the dot crosses into a lightly hatched zone, a preview, not a verdict; the actual pass/fail wording still comes only from the real static error pages, so the live dot is never the system telling the visitor a false yes.
Accessibility: the ruler graphic is aria-hidden; the real limit is present as ordinary associated text (aria-describedby on the input) so a screen-reader user gets "Maximum 900 kg" as text, never only as a moving dot. The witness dot's motion respects prefers-reduced-motion: reduce by snapping to position instantly rather than sliding, the one place in the whole design with any motion at all, which is also the one place required to remove it cleanly.

## Visitor path

Arrives at the homepage (search, a contractor's referral, a saved link) or directly at /book/ (a forwarded bookmark). Either entry point carries the hard limits before any typing or scrolling is required.
Reads the hero in under a few seconds: the mechanism line, the rating-plate strip, the ATEX refusal. A visitor whose rotor is already out of scope can leave here, correctly informed, without calling, the brief's stated success case for someone who never books.
Scrolls "Will we take it?" for the full refusal list, "What it costs" for the relevant price lines, "How long you'll wait" for the day-strip resolving to Mon 17 Aug (standard) or Wed 12 Aug (line-down) off the fixed reference date.
Follows "Book your rotor in" to /book/. Limits are restated inline. Fills all twelve fields; the three numeric fields carry the Witness Rule.
Submits. Four static outcomes, each its own URL, each keyboard-reachable and focus-correct:.
In scope → /book/success.html: job reference HB-2617, the phonetic read-out line, drop-off address and hours, "rotor off the shaft, rotor dry," assessment by Tue 11 Aug, return by Mon 17 Aug, payment on collection by bank transfer, rendered as a completed, stamped layout rather than a generic confirmation panel.
Over the mass limit (Booking A, 1,240 kg) → /book/error.html: all twelve answers still filled in, the mass line alone carries the Stamp Red mark and the exact required copy, and the mass field carries autofocus in the static markup, no JavaScript needed to land focus correctly, since this is a pre-rendered page, not a live validation event.
ATEX (Booking B) → /book/error-atex.html: same pattern, the ATEX field alone marked, mass and every other field left untouched.
Slots gone → /book/slots-full.html: the line-down option's chalk note is visibly scored through with the exact required copy, the radio disabled and unreachable by keyboard tab, and the rest of the form remains fully usable.
Every error page's own copy already names the recovery, ring 01709 000114, set as a live tel: link, one tap away on the same works phone the visitor is reading on. No chat, no account, no retry loop invented beyond what the brief specifies.
Footer repeats firm details, hours, and the fictional-business disclosure. No privacy policy or cookie link appears anywhere, because none exists to link to.

## Honest risk

Contrast pairings are formula-verified, not yet browser-verified. Every value above was checked by the WCAG relative-luminance formula against the specific ground it's used on, and one real finding came out of that check: a single brass accent fails AA for small text on both Blue and Plate Steel, which is why the palette carries two brass tones (Rivet for large/graphic use, Ink for small text) rather than one. That split is easy to get backwards under time pressure, a builder reaching for "the brass" without checking which one is on a small link is the concrete failure mode. Recheck all pairings with real font rendering before ship.
A blueprint/machinist register is a smaller step from typical industrial-B2B visual cliché than it first looks. It is genuinely distinct from the stock-photo- and-icon-grid template this brief explicitly rules out, but "technical drawing lines on an engineering company's site" is itself a familiar move to anyone who's seen a few B2B engineering sites. The scribed/stamped material and the Witness Rule earn their place by being load-bearing rather than decorative; if execution lets them become wallpaper, the distinction collapses.
The stamp motif can read as more punitive than the brief's own voice. The required error copy is calm and helpful, "Ray will tell you who can if you ring", while a rubber-stamp reject mark is a blunt, graphically loud device. If built at hero scale or in a shouty fill, it will out-shout copy that was deliberately written to be kind about a routine refusal. It has to stay small, sit beside the one offending line, and never spread across the whole form.
The Witness Rule's no-JS path is the sharpest technical risk in the build. The brief requires the form fully legible, fillable and submittable with JavaScript disabled, and the ruler's static version must be built first and correctly, with the live witness-dot strictly layered on top. Built in the other order, JS-dependent first, static fallback bolted on after, it will look identical in a normal browser and silently fail the one hard constraint the brief states most plainly.
