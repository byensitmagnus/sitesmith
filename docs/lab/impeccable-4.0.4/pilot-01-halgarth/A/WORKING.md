# Working notes: Halgarth Rotor Balancing

## Subject, pinned

A two-person workshop in Kilnhurst: Ray Halgarth balancing and welding,
Tomás Iriarte grinding, machining and driving, that takes worn industrial
fan impellers off site, regrinds and re-welds the blade tips, and
rebalances the rotor on their own horizontal soft-bearing machine,
returning it with a printed before/after certificate. The one thing the
page must do: let a maintenance engineer standing over a pulled impeller
find out, in under a minute and without a phone call, whether Halgarth
will take the job, what it costs, and when they'd get it back, and if it
qualifies, book it in on the spot.

the plant room the reader is standing in.

## Three theses (ranked)

1. **A weighbridge, not a shopfront.** The page's whole job is to weigh the
   caller's rotor against the machine's fixed limits before they ever pick
   up the phone, and only then walk them to the form. Everything on the
   page is in service of that one check.
2. **The certificate, run backwards.** The only authority object Halgarth
   actually has (no ISO 9001, no ATEX approval, no reviews) is the printed
   before/after certificate every job ends with. Build the whole page in
   the shape of that document rather than a sales page: a record, not a
   pitch.
3. **A job docket that starts filling itself in.** The page is the paper
   traveller tag that would clip to the rotor once it's on the workshop
   floor, and the visitor is watching that tag begin before the rotor has
   even left their site.

## Autopilot description (what I would produce without trying)

A dark charcoal hero with a generic gear or CNC-close-up graphic standing in
for a photograph, bold white sans headline reading something like "Precision
Rotor Balancing You Can Trust," an orange "Get a Quote" button. Oswald or
Poppins for headings, Inter for body, because they're the reach-for-first
pair for "industrial." A three-icon feature row (Balancing, Regrinding,
Certification) in rounded cards with drop shadows and a hover-lift. A
pricing section as three tiered cards instead of the plain price list the
brief actually gives. A "Why Choose Us" bullet list with green checkmarks,
tempted toward inventing a testimonial carousel even though none exist. A
sticky "Get a Quote" button following the scroll. Two-colour scheme, orange
accent on charcoal or navy, nothing in it could not belong to a driveshaft
garage, a boiler-repair firm or a locksmith with the labels swapped.

## Which I chose, and why

Chose thesis 1 (weighbridge). Argued thesis 2 (certificate) properly first,
as if 1 did not exist:

The certificate is the one earned authority object in this brief. Everything
else a trust section would normally lean on (ISO 9001, ATEX competence,
testimonials, star ratings, case studies) is explicitly forbidden or
explicitly absent. Building the page as that document's shape, a two-column
before/after record ruled like the actual certificate, numbers doing the
talking, would give the page an authority that is earned rather than
performed, because it would look like the object Halgarth actually hands
back rather than a website trying to look trustworthy. Typography would pull
from technical-certificate register throughout; the signature could be a
literal small rendering of a two-plane balance record with plane 1 and plane
2 marked along the real journal-spacing span.

Decided on the axis of **which moment in the visitor's actual sixty seconds
the design organises itself around**. The certificate thesis organises
around the moment of completion: proof that a finished job was done well.
The brief is explicit that the page's actual job sits upstream of that.
"Let the caller find the refusal themselves, before they ring" is a
gatekeeping problem, not a proof-of-completion problem, and half of every
call currently dies on exactly that check. The weighbridge thesis organises
around the moment of arrival, which is where this specific brief's stated
friction lives. Chose 1, on that axis, and folded 2's real strength (the
certificate and docket as legitimate material) into the plan as secondary
texture: the booking form reads as an intake docket, and the success state
(job reference, read-out line, dates) is the closest the page gets to the
certificate's own register.

## The two swaps

**Swap 1, neighbouring trade, same plan.** Ran the plan against a
driveshaft/propshaft balancing garage for cars and vans: same core shape
(numeric envelope, capability list, price list, day-count turnaround,
refusals, unknowns, docket form) fit almost unchanged, which meant the shape
was about the category (any tolerance-gated technical service) rather than
about impellers specifically. Changed: added one small recurring line-drawn
blade-wheel (backward-curved impeller) silhouette used sparingly as a glyph
near the H1 and as the end-caps on the plane-1/plane-2 diagram, so the page
is anchored to a fan wheel rather than "a rotating part in general", a mark
a driveshaft garage's page would have no honest use for.

**Swap 2, unrelated trade, same plan.** Handed the plan to a one-person
violin restoration workshop: eligibility plate, capability list, separate
price list, turnaround, refusals/unknowns pairing, docket form all survived
a plain relabel, which meant I had built a process rather than a design.
Changed: collapsed "what we do" and "price" into one merged spec-sheet
table, each capability and its ex-VAT price on the same row, instead of
two separate sections, and pulled the G6.3/G2.5 grade-as-tolerance language
up into the hero plate's own caption, so the page's whole currency reads as
a number checked against a tolerance from the first screen, which is a
metrology habit, not a generic "services, then pricing" template any trade
could wear.

Both changes are folded into `DIRECTION.md`, which reflects the plan after
both swaps, not before.
