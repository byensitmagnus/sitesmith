# Halgarth Rotor Balancing

> **This company is fictional.** It does not exist. Every name, price, dimension,
> address and rule below was invented for a build exercise. The page that gets built
> must itself carry a visible, permanent line stating that the business is fictional
> and that nothing on the page may be relied on. That line must be readable at every
> viewport and must not be hidden behind a toggle, a dialog, or a footer that only
> appears after interaction.

Two people in one unit. They take industrial fan impellers off site, grind the worn
blade tips back to a true profile, weld and re-dress leading edges where the metal
allows it, and rebalance the rotor on a horizontal soft-bearing machine. Then they
send it back with a printed certificate showing residual unbalance before and after.
They do not sell fans, they do not fit fans, and they do not go to site.

## Who it is for

- Maintenance engineers and works fitters at food, foundry, quarry, MDF, biomass and
  waste-transfer plants, who have a fan that is shaking and a production line that
  cares about it.
- Fan and ductwork contractors who need a subcontracted balance and a certificate
  they can hand to their own client.
- Insurance and warranty assessors who need a written residual-unbalance figure.

They are reading on a works phone, often in a plant room, often with the impeller
already pulled and sitting on a pallet. They want to know three things fast: will
Halgarth take this rotor, what will it cost, and when will they get it back.

## The problem the page must solve

At the moment every job starts with a phone call that goes the same way: what does it
weigh, what does it measure, is the hub cracked, is it ATEX, has anyone welded it
before. Half the calls end in a refusal, and the refusal costs both sides fifteen
minutes. The page must let the caller find the refusal themselves, before they ring,
and must let the ones who pass the test book the rotor in without speaking to anybody.

A visitor who leaves without booking should still leave knowing whether their rotor is
in scope, what the standard price is, and what date they would get it back — that date
being the one the Facts fix for a rotor dropped off on the build's reference date,
Monday 17 August 2026. No other return date may be shown or implied.

**Rules they apply to every job**

- The rotor must arrive off the shaft, or on a shaft with the motor removed. A rotor
  that arrives bolted to a motor is refused and returned at the customer's cost.
- The rotor must arrive dry and free of process build-up. Cleaning is charged at £60
  per hour if they have to do it.
- Payment on collection by bank transfer. Account terms of 30 days are offered after
  two completed jobs. There is no card machine.
- A printed certificate showing residual unbalance in g·mm per plane, before and after,
  goes with every balanced rotor. A duplicate is kept for six years.
- What happens to the details typed into the booking form: they are emailed to
  works@halgarth-balancing.example and filed with the job, and the file is kept for six
  years alongside the certificate. They are not sold, not passed to anyone else, and not
  used for marketing. There is no mailing list. Nothing else may be said about the data,
  and no privacy policy, cookie notice or consent text may be invented — there is none.

**What they refuse**

- Any work on ATEX or spark-resistant impellers. They are not certified for it and will
  not sign anything that says they are.
- In-situ balancing on site. Workshop only, always.
- Any rotor with a crack in the hub weld or hub casting. They will assess it, condemn
  it in writing, and charge the £95, but they will not repair it.
- Re-blading or correcting another shop's repair. If someone else has welded it, they
  will balance it as found or refuse it outright, nothing in between.
- Anything with unidentified coatings or possible asbestos in the deposit, unless the
  customer supplies a paid-for analysis first.

**What they do not know**

- They cannot say why a rotor went out of balance. Erosion, deposit build-up, a bent
  shaft and a failing bearing all look the same on the machine. They will state what
  they measured, not what caused it.
- They cannot say how long a balance will hold in an abrasive or sticky duct.
- They have no view on whether the fan was correctly selected for the duty. They do not
  do fan curves and will say so.

## The one journey the site exists for

**Book an impeller in.**

A maintenance engineer with a rotor on a pallet fills in one form and gets a job
reference plus written drop-off instructions. That is the whole reason the page exists.
Everything else on the page is there to get someone to that form or to stop them
wasting a trip.

The form collects, and nothing more:

1. Name.
2. Company.
3. Email.
4. Phone.
5. Rotor mass in kg.
6. Rotor diameter in mm.
7. Journal spacing in mm.
8. Work wanted: balance only / regrind and balance / assess and advise.
9. Is it ATEX or spark-resistant? yes / no / don't know.
10. Is the hub cracked, or has it been welded before? yes / no / don't know.
11. Line-down 48-hour slot wanted? yes / no.
12. Collection wanted, and the postcode it would be collected from.

There is no account, no login, no saved quote, no live chat, and no payment on the page.

## Constraints

- **Stack:** hand-written HTML and CSS. No build step, no bundler, no framework, no npm
  install, no CSS preprocessor. Deployable by copying a folder onto a host. At most one
  vanilla JavaScript file, and the form must remain fully legible, fillable and
  submittable with JavaScript disabled — validation messaging is the five static states
  above, not a script.
- **Viewports:** must work from 320 px wide to 1440 px wide. No horizontal scrolling at
  any width in that range.
- **Keyboard:** every field, control and link reachable and operable by keyboard alone,
  in a sensible order, with a visible focus indicator. On the error states, focus must
  land on the field that caused the error.
- **Reduced motion:** `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` must remove any animation or
  transition, not merely shorten it.
- **Photography:** none exists. There are no photographs of the unit, the machine, the
  two men, or any rotor. Do not use stock photography, and do not use images that imply
  a photograph of this business exists. Whatever fills that gap must not assert anything
  that is not in the Facts list.
- **Never claim:** ISO 9001 registration; ATEX certification or competence; 24/7 or
  emergency cover; any number of customers, jobs or years other than "trading since
  2009"; any guaranteed vibration reduction or run-time improvement; that balancing
  fixes bearing, shaft or foundation problems; any testimonial, review, star rating or
  client logo — none exist and none may be invented.
- **No cookie banner, no analytics, no third-party scripts, no web fonts fetched from a
  third party.** Everything is served from the same origin.
- **Copy language:** English (UK). Prices shown excluding VAT, and labelled as such
  everywhere they appear.
