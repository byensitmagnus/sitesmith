# Evidence pack — Damgaard Estrik

> Fictional subject (see `BRIEF.md`). The company is invented; the trade is real. Nothing
> below invents a checkable fact about Damgaard specifically — names, prices and rules all
> come from `BRIEF.md`. This file is about the real trade of structural drying and screed
> replacement, used to keep the page out of generic "vandskade" marketing territory.

## 1. Artefacts

- A borehole through a floor construction — the only way a kernemåling is taken.
- A measurement log: date, point, value, written down every 7th day.
- An adsorption dehumidifier and a condensation dehumidifier — different machines, both
  named explicitly in the brief, each needing its own dedicated circuit.
- Sideblæsere (axial floor blowers) feeding air into a sandbælte through drilled holes,
  spaced 1.2 m apart.
- A 16 A dedicated group, documented before a machine is connected — the brief makes this a
  hard gate, not a footnote.
- A cementestrik curing from wet to a CM% reading; a flydemørtel slidlag poured over it.
- A written overslag — this is what a taksator or skadebehandler actually asks for.
- Could appear on the site: the measurement cadence, the depth rule, the RF%/CM% thresholds,
  the equipment counts and the capacity ceiling — all stated facts, all drawable as diagrams.
  Could not appear: any photograph of the above (`fotografi` constraint, see §5).

## 2. Vocabulary

Uses: fugtgennemgang, kernemåling, borehul, RF%, CM%, afretningslag, estrik, slidlag,
flydemørtel, sandbælte, sideindblæsning, adsorptionsaffugter, kondensaffugter, skadenummer,
taksator, skadebehandler, kategori 3 (spildevand), 16 A gruppe, dokumenteret strøm.

Does not use: løsning, rejse, oplevelse, skræddersyet, akut, døgnvagt, garanti — the brief
explicitly forbids several of these as claims, and the rest just are not how this trade talks.

## 3. Materials and surfaces

Wet cement, drilled concrete dust, PVC hose, galvanised or plastic ducting, a paper
measurement log on a clipboard, a laminated price sheet. Nothing soft, nothing branded,
nothing retail. The trade's real texture is closer to a site inspection than a showroom —
which argues for a page built from rules, thresholds and a written record rather than
lifestyle imagery.

## 4. Colour that is already true

No existing brand colour — the company is new to this exercise, nothing to inherit. What is
true of the *trade*: an amber/ochre reading is the colour a technician actually circles on a
log sheet to flag a value that has not yet cleared its threshold — not a decorative choice,
a working one. Concrete and drilled dust are warm grey, not cool. There is no blue-water
cliché to earn here: this company explicitly is not the emergency-response trade (no
døgnvagt, no weekend call-out, refuses category-3 water) and should not borrow that trade's
sirens-and-blue visual language.

## 5. Constraints already in force

- No photography exists or may be implied to exist (brief, `Fotografi`).
- No cookies, no analytics, no third-party scripts (brief, hard constraint).
- 320–1440 px, no horizontal scroll; full keyboard reachability; focus moves to the field
  that errored; `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` removes animation and transition entirely.
- The fictional-company disclosure must be visible, permanent, unhidden, at every width.
- A long, explicit list of claims that may never appear (24/7, insurance partnership,
  certification, years/case-count experience, mould competence, guarantees, reviews).

## 6. References and anti-references

**References**
- A calibration certificate / instrument datasheet — the way it states a measured value,
  a tolerance and a date without adjectives. What to take: numbers presented as read, not
  as sold.
- A structural engineer's site log — dated entries, plain language, no persuasion. What to
  take: the receipt and the measurement table read like this, not like a marketing summary.
- A permit or compliance notice — states plainly what it does not cover and who it refers
  elsewhere. What to take: the refusal list is presented with the same weight as the sales
  pitch, not buried in small print.

**Anti-references**
- The standard Danish skadeservice/vandskade landing page: siren-red, "vi kommer nu," a
  24-hour badge, a stock photo of a flooded living room. Damgaard explicitly is not this
  company (no døgnvagt, no weekend call-out) and must not borrow its visual grammar.
- Generic SaaS marketing (soft rounded cards, purple-to-blue gradient, three feature icons
  in circles). Nothing here is software; nothing should look like it.
- A stock-photo trades site (hi-vis vest, thumbs up, hard hat, warm handshake). The brief
  bans stock photography outright; a design that reaches for it in spirit — a decorative
  hero illustration standing in for the photo — fails the same test.

## 7. Asset reality

- Photography: **none exists, none may be implied.** (brief, hard constraint)
- Logo: **none exists.** A wordmark is drawn for this project (display face, no photography
  substitute) plus one small drawn mark referencing the 40%-of-depth measurement rule — the
  one fact that is most specifically Damgaard's own way of working.
- Diagrams: **none exist yet, all needed.** Three are planned, each illustrating a stated
  fact rather than decorating the page: the borehole depth rule, the drying timeline against
  its thresholds, and the 75 km / 40 km service-area rule. See `ASSET-MANIFEST.md`.
- Everything else on the page is text, from `BRIEF.md`, or a token-driven diagram built from
  that text.
