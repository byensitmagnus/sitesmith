# Direction record

## Surface
The single marketing page for a two-person rigging loft that splices and tests wire standing rigging.

## Constraints in force
No budget for photography this quarter. The loft's own test certificates may be shown. Danish and English copy must fit the same measure.

## Subject
A rigging loft on a working quay. It is for skippers who have to hand a mast over to someone. The page has to make one workshop feel accountable enough to trust with a rig.

## Assets that exist
Eleven phone photographs of finished splices, the loft's break-test certificate template, and a scan of the 1978 swage die chart still pinned to the wall.

## Nouns
Swage die, break test, wire lay, Norseman cone, bosun's chair, tang, clevis pin, cotter, working load limit, 1x19 wire, terminal, load cell, proof load, quay.

## Theses

1. A test bench that happens to sell rigging.
2. A ledger of every rig this loft has signed off.
3. A quay-side workshop you can walk into.

## Case for the runner-up

The other two theses were considered and neither of them was as strong.

## Built

Built: 2, axis: accountability over spectacle, because a signed record answers the skipper's real question and a bench photograph only answers an aesthetic one

## Colour
- --quay: the ground, the wet grey of the concrete the loft opens onto
- --wire: body text and rules, the dull silver of 1x19 before it is dressed
- --cotter: the single accent, the brass of a split pin, used only on a signed line
- --proof: the pass state on a test row, kept off every other surface

## Type
- record: a narrow grotesque set at certificate size for the signed lines
- reading: a text face at 17px for everything a skipper reads standing up
- figures: lining figures at one width so two working load limits stack

## Density, motion and boldness
Dense in the signed-record columns and open everywhere else. Nothing moves. Boldness is spent entirely on the certificate block.

## Structure
Numbered rows, because a rig is signed off in sequence and the numbering claims that order truthfully. No eyebrows, because nothing here is a category.

## First screen
The most recent signed record, in full, with its date and the initials of whoever ran the test.

## Imagery treatment
The eleven splice photographs, all cropped to the same 3:2 and printed at one width, so the set reads as a record rather than a gallery.

## Argument order
Signed record, then how a test is run, then what it costs, then who signs it.

## Signature
The certificate block itself, set the way the loft sets it on paper, ruled in --wire with the brass split-pin mark against the signed line.

## Risk
The page leads with paperwork. The category leads with a photograph of a mast against a sky, and this one refuses to.

## Assumptions
Assumed the loft will let its certificate template be shown publicly. Written down here because nobody has confirmed it.

## Originality pass
Ran the plan against a neighbouring sail loft brief. The colour set and the type roles both survived the swap, so both were rebuilt from the swage die chart instead of from the quay.
