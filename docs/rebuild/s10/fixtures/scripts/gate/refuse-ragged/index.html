Trelfall & Son
Work Terms

Six pits, ninety days, no shortcut

We tan with bark liquor in open pits. A hide enters at pit one and leaves at pit six, and the ledger records the day it moved.

The six pits and what each liquor does

Pit one is the weakest liquor and pit six the strongest, and a hide moves only forward. The ledger at the pit side records the day it moved and the name of whoever moved it.

A hide that misses its move is written down as missed, with the day and the reason, in the same book and in the same pencil.

Terms of the pit ledger

The ninety days are counted from the day a hide enters pit one, and the count is kept by hand at the pit side rather than by anything that could be behind.

Nothing skips a pit and nothing goes back, so the ledger reads forward and a reader can follow one hide from the first entry to the last without turning back.
