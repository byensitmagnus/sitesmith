# Direction record

## Surface
shop

## Subject
a two-person vegetable tannery that runs six pits on a ninety day cycle

## Constraints in force
No photography exists. The pit numbers are the only fixed vocabulary the trade uses.

## Assets that exist
The sizing drum chart, photographed once, and the pit ledger itself.

## Nouns
pit, liquor, bark, sizing drum, hide, ninety day cycle

## Theses
1. the ledger, the page is the book the pits are recorded in
2. the drum, the page is the machine and the copy is its label
3. the cycle, the page is ninety days laid end to end

## Case for the runner-up
For: 2. The drum is the one object a customer would recognise, and leading with it would
have made the trade legible in a second. It lost because the ledger is what the customer
actually receives, and the page should look like the thing they get.

## Built
Built: 1, axis: document over showcase, because the buyer is handed a ledger page and not a
brochure.

## Colour
- ground: `#e6ecf2`, the paper the ledger is kept on
- drum: `#dccfb8`, the sizing drum seen through the doorway
- bark: `#b9a487`, bark before it is ground

## Type
- display: "Courier New", set at the pit labels because the trade writes them on a machine
- body: "Georgia", the ledger's own reading face

## Density, motion and boldness
Dense, still, quiet. Nothing moves. The only emphasis is a rule.

## Structure
One column the width of a ledger page, with the pit rows running the full measure.

## First screen
The pit number, the date, and the hide count. No headline above them.

## Imagery treatment
The one photograph is set as a plate with a caption, never as a background.

## Argument order
What is in the pit now, then what comes out, then what it costs.

## Signature
the wet line that runs under every heading (`.wetline`)

## Risk
A page that looks like a document can read as unfinished to a buyer expecting a shop.

## Answer to the risk
The risk above is answered by `.wetline`, the signature that runs under every heading and keeps the page reading as one object rather than as a document with sections.

## Second reading
`.ledgerpage` renders the pit book itself, hides in pit one and the oldest entry, below the first screen and from facts the signature does not carry.

## The shell
Who: `Trelfall & Son` in the header, on every screen. Where: Marstal, named in the footer. Do: the enquiry link in the footer, which is the one action this surface carries.

## Assumptions
Assumed the pit numbering is public. Written down because nobody confirmed it.

## Originality pass
Ran the plan against a neighbouring cooperage brief. The colour set survived the swap and
was rebuilt from the drum chart instead of the yard.

## One-offs
- `0.5px` the wet line is a hairline and a hairline is not a scale step

## Deliberate
- `gradient-text` the drum label bleeds like liquor into leather, and the bleed is the mark
