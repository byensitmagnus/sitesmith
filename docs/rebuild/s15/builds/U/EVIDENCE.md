**Words the brief's own facts hand over, and which must be used as written:** pouchlæder,

**Words the category uses that this workshop should not:** løsninger, skræddersyet, passion
for musik, oplevelse, univers, totalrenovering.

Why it matters: a person who has inherited an instrument does not know the word *pouch*. The
page has to teach five words and then use them, because those five words are what the
customer will repeat on the phone.

## 3. Materials and surfaces

Buff-to-manila paper, punched, and stiff enough to run over a bar without tearing
`(inferred)`. Thin leather. Rubber tubing that hardens. Cloth-covered bellows. Spruce and
poplar in the chests, cast iron in the plate, felt on the hammers `(inferred)`. Black or
walnut lacquer on the case `(inferred)`.

Implication for the page: this is a paper-and-leather world with a hard machined element
(the brass bar, the iron plate) inside it. Warm, matte, absorbent grounds; edges that read as
cut or punched rather than rounded; nothing glossy and nothing that floats.

## 5. Constraints already in force

- **Told by the brief:** pneumatic instruments only, not electronic and not ordinary pianos —
  except that they will tune an ordinary piano if they are already there. No quote before the
  assessment. Four instruments at a time and usually a wait. Workshop hours Tuesday to
  Thursday 09:00–16:00, which means the phone is not answered five days a week.
- **Told by the run:** Danish copy, one self-contained HTML file, no external JS, no
  photography, works at 375 / 768 / 1440, accessible.
- **Told by the brief, negatively, and this is the sharpest constraint:** no customer names,
  no testimonials, no review scores, no counts of instruments restored. The four strongest
  ordinary proof devices are all unavailable, so proof has to come from method and price
  instead.

## 6. References and anti-references

**References**

1. **The music roll itself.** Take the punch grid: rectangular slots with rounded ends, in a
   regular rhythm down a strip of buff paper. It is the one geometry that belongs to this
   trade and to nothing else.
2. **Workshop parts drawings** — the labelled line cross-section with leader lines and
   numerals, as printed in a service manual. Take the numbering convention: the drawing is
   numbered and the list beside it uses the same numbers.
3. **A written condition report.** Take the register: short declarative lines, a price on its
   own line with a rule under it, no adjectives.

**Anti-references**

1. **The standard local-trade landing page** — centred hero, a stock photograph of hands on
   keys, three equal cards, "Kontakt os i dag". Must not resemble it, because every competitor
   already does and because there is no photograph to centre anything on.
2. **Antique-shop pastiche** — sepia, ornament, a ribbon reading "siden 1981", copperplate
   script. Must not resemble it, because it turns a technical trade with a published price
   into nostalgia, and because it makes a customer expect a collector's markup.
3. **Auction-house minimalism** — an acre of white, 11px type, the object floating alone.
   Must not resemble it, because the visitor has a broken machine and a practical question,
   and this treatment answers a different one.
