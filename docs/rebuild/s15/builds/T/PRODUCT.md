## Users

The person who has **inherited a player piano** and does not know whether it can
be saved. They are not a collector and not a technician; they have an object in a
room that they cannot evaluate, may feel obliged to, and may be slightly afraid of
the answer. **[inferred]** Secondary: owners who already know the instrument works
and want music rolls for it.

## Product Purpose

Klinke & Datter repair and re-tune pneumatic player pianos and pianolas, and cut
perforated music rolls from original masters. The surface's job: a person who
inherited such an instrument should understand what these two people do, roughly
what it costs to find out, and how to start.

## Positioning

Two named people, father and daughter, doing pneumatic work only — Verner Klinke
since 1981, Liv Klinke since 2011. The entry point is not a quote but a paid,
bounded diagnosis: 1.850 kr., half a day in the customer's own home, ending in a
written condition report whether or not the job goes ahead. They will not quote a
restoration before that assessment.

## Capabilities and Constraints

**They do:** pneumatic player pianos and pianolas; full pneumatic restoration;
tuning of an ordinary piano *if they are already there*; cutting new music rolls
from original masters — 38 titles in stock, 340 kr. per roll.

**They do not:** electronic instruments; ordinary pianos as a job of their own;
quoting a restoration before the assessment.

**Named common failures:** perished pouch leather, split tracker bar tubing, worn
striker pneumatics, a cracked wind trunk, drive belts gone hard.

**Undecided / absent:** no email address, no booking form endpoint, no opening
hours for phone calls as distinct from workshop hours, no price for tuning, no
list of the 38 roll titles, no delivery or shipping terms. None of these may be
invented; the page must work with phone and address only.

## Product Principles

1. **The diagnosis is the offer.** The thing being sold on this page is 1.850 kr.
   of certainty, not a restoration. Everything routes to that.
2. **Refusing to quote is the trust signal.** State it plainly; do not apologise
   for it.
3. **Name the mechanism, not the feeling.** Pouch leather, tracker bar tubing,
   wind trunk. The specifics are the only proof available, and they are enough.
4. **Two people, finite capacity.** Four at a time and a wait is honest scarcity,
   stated as fact, never as pressure.
5. **Nothing gets rounded up.** No claim, count, name or guarantee beyond the
   brief, in any form.
