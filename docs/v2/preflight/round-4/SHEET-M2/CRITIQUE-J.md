notes:
- assets — Desktop screen 1: the H1 breaks as "Rope, cut to the metre while you / wait", leaving a one-word last line in the hero card; and in every cut panel (desktop, all five rows) the black button wraps to a second line landing 34px right of the price above it, so it aligns to neither the price nor the stepper and leaves an L-shaped void beside "Cut me".
- production-readiness — Desktop screen 1: the white hero card overhangs the photo by about 215px, and the beige band it drops into is empty for roughly 800px to its right; the footer also still carries "No such counter exists… written for a design exercise", which would have to come off before a customer sees it.
- direction — Legible and singular in the first screen: a paper counter ticket you can operate before you get to the counter. Warning band, then the knife-and-rule photo, then a row you can price. Not a list of components.
- specificity — Structure, not just copy, does the work: a per-metre stepper that prices live, a batch number under every construction, a "ticket" rather than a cart, and a dashed "Not photographed. Polypropylene is sold in the trade's blue and orange" box where a stock image would go (desktop screen 3). The photography is bespoke — five rope ends on the same plank in the same light, matching the hero.
- type — Two faces used to a rule that holds on every row: old-style serif for names, headings and money; humanist sans for body and controls; figures bolded inline inside running sentences ("12 mm diameter, breaking load 3.1 t"). The £ set in serif and coloured is the one moment the type is used hard, and it is the right moment.
- colour — Cream page, warmer cream for the operable panel, near-black text, one warm brown reserved for money and the pressed button, one pink reserved for the safety band and the refusal message. The accent never appears where it does not mean money; the panel tint is what tells you which part of the row you can touch.
- hierarchy — The five rows are deliberately identical because the visitor came to compare, and within each row the order is name → use → spec → price → action without exception. The refusal (desktop state 1) is well judged: at 1m the price becomes an em dash rather than £0.00 and the button visibly greys out.
- Mobile note: in the "two cuts on the ticket" frame the Kernmantle button renders brown where every other Add button on both views is black — readable as a retained tap/focus state, but it is the only place the accent lands on a full button.
