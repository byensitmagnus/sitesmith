notes:
- hierarchy — Desktop screen 2 (scrolled 900px) shows three construction names at once but their spec lines are ~330px apart with a stepper, a price and a black button wedged between each, so no two sets of numbers are ever in the eye at the same time; the comparison the rigger came for has to be done from memory.
- assets — The five rope thumbnails are one treatment on one plank and correctly cropped at both widths, and the "Not photographed / we will not show you white polyester in its place" box is the best single decision on the sheet, but the mobile "two cuts on the ticket" frame renders the Kernmantle "Add to the ticket" button in rust-brown while every other instance of that button, including the equivalent desktop frame, is black.
- type — The serif/sans split is deliberate and consistent (serif for names and measured facts, sans for advice), yet £103.75 appears twice in the same mobile frame in two treatments: serif rust in the product row, sans black in the ticket bar; the desktop headline also drops "wait" onto a line of its own.
- production-readiness — Shippable but not apology-free: the footer prints "No such counter exists… written for a design exercise", the mobile button colour anomaly above, and the ticket bar's right-aligned footnote ends on the orphan "knife.".
- direction — Legible in one screen and unmistakably a counter rather than a shop: the safety band above the fold, the brass rule and knife on dock timber, and a price list you operate rather than browse. The composition is the weak part — below the hero photo, right of the white card, roughly 450×125px of flat warm grey is doing nothing, and the body measure stops ~290px short of the right margin down the whole document while the header alone runs to the full width.
- colour — Warm off-white ground, a deeper warm grey for every control block, black for actions, one rust for money and a pale pink band for the safety notice. It comes from the subject (timber, brass, manila, hazard card) and the accent is restricted to price — the mobile button being the single leak.
- specificity — Strip the wordmark and this cannot be another business: minimum breaking loads distinguished from safe working loads, batch numbers per coil, "Cut me __ metres", a refusal at 1 m because the minimum cut is 3 m, whipping charged at £1.80 a cut, and "Take this to the counter" instead of a checkout.
