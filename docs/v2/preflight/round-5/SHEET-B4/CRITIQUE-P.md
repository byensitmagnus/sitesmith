notes:
- assets — desktop screens 1 and 3: the four rope photographs do not read as one shoot, the three-strand and kernmantle sit on cool grey-brown timber while the double braid and eight-plait sit on a distinctly warmer orange board; and directly under "Every construction we cut" the left column gets a heavy black rule while the right column gets a pale pink hairline at the same y, which reads as a mismatch rather than a distinction.
- hierarchy — desktop screens 3 and 4: the page ends with an orphan fifth card in a two-column grid, its photo slot empty, then a footer set to about 45% of the width, so the final two screens are majority empty ground with no closing move except the sticky ticket.
- production-readiness — desktop screen 4, footer: the last paragraph tells the reader "No such counter exists… written for a design exercise", which is a literal apology printed under the client's name, and the empty dashed image slot on screen 3 would have to be explained before this went live.

what is working, named:
- The price is a word in a sentence, not a badge. "12 mm diameter, breaking load 3.1 t, stretches 14 %, £2.40 the metre." set in the serif with the figures bold and the price in a warm brown is the strongest idea on the sheet, and it is held on all five products at both widths.
- There is an actual typographic rule, not a pairing: the serif carries anything the shop has measured (spec lines, batch numbers, the address and hours, the £158.65 total) and the sans carries description and interface. That would be recognisable on a second page.
- The refusal is designed, not bolted on. Desktop screen 3, left card: quantity 1 puts an em-dash where the price was, greys the "Add to the ticket" button out, and prints "The minimum cut is 3 m, we cannot sell a shorter length off the coil." The price does not go to £0 and the button does not stay live.
- "Not photographed. Polypropylene is sold in the trade's blue and orange, and we will not show you white polyester in its place." is a merchant refusing to fake a photo. The idea is right even though the box it sits in is the page's biggest hole.
- The ticket bar reads back what a counter would read back — quantity, construction, diameter, batch, two cuts, total, and the whipping charge as a footnote beside the button. Mobile keeps all of it and stacks it correctly at 390.
- The accent is disciplined. Warm brown appears only on prices, the dark red only on the safety band and refusals, and the near-black only on buttons. The pale pink safety strip above the hero puts the one thing a rigger could be hurt by above the fold.

what is holding it back, beyond the scores:
- Desktop screen 1: the hero photograph — brass rule, knife, coil on dock timber, the best asset on the page — is cut to a 220px letterbox in a 900px viewport and the intro card covers its lower half. At 390 the same photograph gets proportionally more room and works better, which is the wrong way round.
- Desktop, every product card: the cut widget's sentence breaks as "Cut me [10] metres, which comes to" / "£24.00 … Add to the ticket", so the clause ends a line with about 250px of empty box after it and the price it points at sits below and to the left. The sentence is a good idea fighting its own line break.
- Type scale is timid in one place: the section head "Every construction we cut" is set at the same size as the individual card titles beneath it, so the section does not outrank its own children.
- Mobile screen 1: the hero card's text is indented about 44px further than "Every construction we cut" below it, so the headline aligns to nothing on the page.
