notes:
- type — the serif is chosen and given real work (every measurement, every price, the "Cut me … metres" control), but all the prose is set in the Windows system UI sans, so half the page's type is inherited rather than picked; and the scale is a narrow ramp — on desktop screen 1 the card price £24.00, the product name "Three-strand polyester" and the section head "Every construction we cut" are within a couple of points of each other, so nothing on a page about price per metre is ever set large.
- assets — desktop screen 3, right column: the "Not photographed" panel is a dashed box roughly 480px tall holding two lines of grey text and nothing else, so one of the five products shows a void where a photograph goes; desktop screen 1, under "Every construction we cut", the left column is capped by a heavy black 2px rule and the right column by a pale hairline at the identical baseline, which reads as a bug rather than a device; mobile ticket frame, the primary button label "Take this to the counter" sits hard left in a full-bleed black bar with ~400px of empty black to its right; the placeholder copy uses a straight apostrophe in "trade's" on a page that otherwise sets "Mon–Sat" with a correct en dash.
- hierarchy — the loudest thing on the first screen, desktop and mobile, is the pink safety band, which fires before the visitor has been told what is sold; and below the hero the page is one card repeated five times with identical internal spacing, so the section rhythm does no work at all — on mobile that is six near-identical scrolled screens.
- production-readiness — I would have to explain the empty "Not photographed" panel and the off-centre CTA label in the mobile ticket before showing this, and the mobile ticket itself takes about 390px of an 844px viewport with only two cuts on it, growing with every further cut.

what is working, named: the hero photograph is the argument rather than decoration — a folding brass rule and a knife on the bench, which is exactly what "cut to the metre while you wait" means, and it re-crops correctly at 390px keeping rule, knife and coil. The product photographs are one treatment throughout: the same weathered board, the same light, the same shallow letterbox. Colour is disciplined — a warm ochre used only on prices, a dark red used only for the safety band and the minimum-cut refusal, black reserved for the two ticket actions. The states are complete and in the client's voice: the refusal replaces the price with an em dash, greys the button and says "The minimum cut is 3 m, we cannot sell a shorter length off the coil"; "Out of stock. The next coil lands 12 August" gives a date; and the ticket footer names the whipping charge before you commit. This is not the category default — a rope shop rendered as a counter with a paper ticket is a real idea, and it is carried consistently.
