notes:
- assets — desktop screen 3, right column: "INSIDE THE SOUNDBOW, AFTER CUTTING" sits about 20px above the "TOWER" field label with the section hairline squeezed between them, and the caption is left-aligned to the image while the label sits roughly 100px further left, so three elements pile into one gap on two different margins. Separately, the header wordmark and hero headline sit on a left margin about 50px inside the one every section below them uses.
- hierarchy — desktop screens 1 and 2, partials table: the cents figure is right-aligned to the end of the track regardless of sign, so "−38" sits to the right of the centre tick while its bar runs to the left, and a "0" sits at what reads as the top of the scale. The number stops agreeing with its own bar.
- production-readiness — desktop screen 3 and mobile screen 5: native user-agent controls (the number spinner in "Bells in the ring", the select caret in "Faculty position", the resize handle on the textarea) are unstyled against a dark maroon error fill; and at 390 the outlined "TELL US ABOUT THE RING" in the header and the filled one in the hero are the same words twice within about 250px.

what is working, named:
- The headline argues rather than announces, and the colour break carries the argument: "A BELL IS TUNED BY" in white, "TAKING METAL AWAY" in brass. One sentence, two weights of meaning, no subhead needed.
- The partials table is a real instrument, not a stat strip. Partial / cents / metal off, a BEFORE–AFTER toggle, a zero-centred deviation track per row, kilograms in a mono, an em-dash where the nominal lost nothing, and a summary line "After: nothing further than 3 cents from true, and 9.4 kg of bronze gone for good." No other trade could reuse this.
- "WHAT WE WILL NOT DO" is three refusals given a section of their own with gold rules over each: cracked bells, no faculty, quoting from a photograph. Turning the refusals into a headline is the page's second-best idea after the toggle.
- "NINE KILOGRAMS CAME OFF A BELL THAT WEIGHS SEVEN HUNDRED AND THIRTY-EIGHT" set in condensed caps with the number spelled out, beside a macro of the soundbow where the bright bands are the cut, with the sentence "The bright bands are the cut." bolded inside the body copy. The picture and the words are doing the same job.
- Three roles for three faces, each held: condensed grotesque caps for headings, humanist sans for body, mono for figures and letterspaced captions. Brass appears on the active nav frame, the CTA, the active toggle half, the deviation bars, the rules and the captions — always as the foundry's own voice, never as decoration.
- The refusal copy is in the owner's voice and each error names a consequence: "Above twelve we would rather ring you, put the tower in and we will call", "Say where the faculty stands, even if that is 'I do not know'", "We need an address that works, this is the only way we answer."
- The ENQUIRY SENT receipt reads the parish's own words back — Tower, Bells, Faculty, Reply to, and "You said: The third sounds sour." That is exactly the reassurance an enquiry form usually withholds.

what is holding it back, beyond the scores:
- Mobile screen 1: two lines of the hero paragraph ("publish both sets, because a foundry that will not / show you the before is asking you to take the") run across the brightest bronze in the photograph and are the least legible text on either sheet.
- Mobile screen 1, header: the wordmark is left-aligned, the nav row beneath it is right-aligned, and the button beneath that is left-aligned again — three rows, three alignments, no scrim between them.
- Desktop screen 3: "Bells in the ring" shows the persistent hint "Between 1 and 16…" and then an error that opens with the same six words in red, so the reader is told the range twice in two colours.
- Desktop screens 3 and the state frames: beside the form the left column runs out of copy after four lines and leaves roughly 700px of empty ground while the form continues down the right.
- The footer prints "There is no such foundry and there is no such tenor" under the client's name — honest, but it is a printed apology.
