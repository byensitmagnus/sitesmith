- production-readiness — mobile, screen 4 (scrolled 2532px): the whole Specification block runs past the right edge, clipping body prose as well as two table columns, and there is no visible scroll affordance; on mobile screen 1 the nav also wraps, orphaning "BUYING" onto a second line while the active underline sits alone under "THE LEATHERS".
- direction — "Six leathers, drawn to scale" is the argument and every later section is the same argument extended: the rules, the overhang, the top edge, the lead-time chart, the pit count. One sentence, and the first screen delivers it.
- specificity — bark pits, one Shropshire coppice, twenty-eight pits with nineteen down, "we do not sell to the public", nine to fourteen months no matter who asks. Remove the wordmark and the drawings alone still say vegetable tannery.
- type — an old-style serif carrying display and prose against a mono for eyebrows and figures, with each thickness set large in mono and its unit dropped small beside it; the bold lead-in word ("One millimetre apart.", "Temper.", "Grain.") is used the same way every time it appears. It would be recognisable on a second page.
- colour — bark-cream ground, one oxide red reserved for the enquiry, and a full inversion to near-black for the pit section, so the ground changes exactly where the subject does.
- assets — every drawing finishes the sentence. The six strips hold a consistent scale (about 39 px per millimetre across 1.4, 2.4 and 3.2 mm), the far-end fall encodes temper and the notched top edge encodes grain, both captioned as notation rather than picture. The lead-time chart puts all six on one dated rule with a today marker, hatched bars for the two already in the pit and dotted nine-to-fourteen-month windows, with a legend. The twenty-eight-square pit count is captioned "as a count, not as a survey of the yard". Borrowed proof is refused in writing: no customer list, no quotation, and a sentence saying why. The mark is a millimetre rule under the name.
- assets — the one gap: the copy promises "all six standing on one baseline against rules a millimetre apart", but the six are drawn in six banded rows on six baselines, so the cross-row read depends on faint tick rules; the LAY/OVER control below is what actually makes the overlay, one scroll later than the claim.
- hierarchy — headline, six rows, mechanics, pits, buying, enquiry: the order a trade buyer needs, and the enquiry form is the only red on a cream page.
