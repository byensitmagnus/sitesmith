notes:
- colour — desktop screen 1, STATE column: the salmon OVERDUE pill is lighter and less saturated against the near-black ground than the amber DUE TODAY pills either side of it, so the colour ranks "due" above "late"; the same status is then spent a second time as a coloured bar on the row's left edge, so the palette says one fact twice instead of two facts once.
- hierarchy — desktop screen 1, row order: the sequence runs DUE TODAY, OVERDUE, ON TRADE, DUE TODAY — not sorted by state, not by due date, not by gyle (214, 212, 214, 209) and not alphabetically; squint and the four identical "Book in" buttons and four equal-weight pills form two flat vertical stripes with no first thing in them.
- production-readiness — desktop screen 1, DUE BACK column: three notations in one column ("Today", "3 d late", "+4 d") with no stated sign convention, so "3 d late" and "+4 d" are the same quantity written two ways in adjacent rows; that plus the default sort is what the cellarman would complain about on the first morning.

The direction is a cellar slate at 06:40, and it is legible in the first screen: near-black ground,
one list, monospace for anything measured, and a status line reading "06:40 · dray in at 07:15 · 15
on trade, 2 late". It reads as back-of-house rather than as a SaaS dashboard — no cards, no shadows,
no widget grid. It is a competent console. What it is not is ordered by the urgency it is measuring.

The best decision on the page is the cask glyph: it is drawn to scale, so a kilderkin row's barrel
is visibly larger than a firkin's, and the SIZE column therefore carries a comparative quantity
rather than a repeated icon. That is a genuine, subject-specific device and it would survive having
the copy stripped off. It is undercut by its own labelling — the header says SIZE, but the words
"kilderkin" and "firkin" are printed one column over, in the WHERE column beneath the pub name, so
the named value and the column that claims it are in different places.

Specificity beyond that glyph lives mostly in the nouns. Gyle numbers, dray, on trade, consignments,
casks — the vocabulary is a cellar's. The chassis under it is not: header strip, filter chips,
column headers, repeated row of glyph / name + mono sub-line / large numeral / status pill / action
button. A tool-hire desk or a courier depot could take that structure unchanged. The trade is in the
copy and in one drawing, not yet in the structure.

Type is two families with the same clean split as the rest of this work — a grotesque for names and
the large cask counts, a monospace for gyles, times, due-backs and headers. The large cask numerals
are the one strong typographic move and they are the right thing to have made large. Few sizes,
used consistently.

Every row gets an identically weighted "Book in" button, including the Kell & Anchor row that is not
due back for four days. The action has no priority gradient, the list has no sort, and the state
colour ranks the wrong state first. Individually each is small; together they are the whole job the
visitor came to do. The page is also very short — four consignments and one empty "Booked in this
week" panel over 1,302px — so there is nothing else drawing the eye that might have compensated.
