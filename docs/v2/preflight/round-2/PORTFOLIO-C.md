## 1. One studio applying one template, or three pieces of work?

One studio, one method, three surfaces — and the method is close enough to a recipe that it should
be named.

Evidence of one hand, in order of how hard it is to explain away:

- **All three end with the same confession, in the same place, in the same small grey type.**
  "No such counter exists: the shop, the lines and the batch numbers were written for a design
  exercise." / "There is no such foundry and there is no such tenor. Weights and notes shown are
  invented." / "Invented brewery, invented cellar, invented week." Same position, same tone, same
  size. This is a signature, not a coincidence.
- **All three reserve a monospace for measured values and use a proportional face for names.**
  Prices and breaking loads on T6, partial labels on R9, gyles and times on M2. The rule never
  breaks on any of the three.
- **All three refuse photography entirely** and substitute exactly one family of line-drawn
  technical assets: rope cross-sections, a bell section, scaled cask glyphs. One stroke weight per
  page, no illustration anywhere else.
- **All three lead with a limitation rather than a benefit.** "They are not safe working loads." /
  "There is no putting it back." / "15 on trade, 2 late." None of the three has a benefit statement,
  a testimonial, or a call to action above the fold.
- **All three carry a nearly empty state panel near the foot** — "Your order: nothing cut yet",
  "Booked in this week: nothing booked in yet". R9 substitutes the enquiry form in the same slot.
- **All three put a small mark plus wordmark top-left and a status or utility line top-right**, above
  a single hairline rule.

Evidence that they are three pieces and not three skins: the grounds are genuinely different and
each is argued for (a cream ticket, a warm foundry near-black, a cellar blue-black); the type systems
differ in kind, not just in choice (condensed grotesque + mono / garalde serif + humanist sans +
mono / grotesque + mono); the layouts differ structurally (a single measured column, a two-column
page with a held illustration, a full-bleed row console); and the accent logic differs in what it is
*for* (oxblood used once for a warning, gold meaning metal, amber/salmon meaning status).

So: not one template. But one very identifiable set of convictions, applied three times — which at
portfolio scale will read as a voice on the second page and as a formula by the fourth.

## 2. Which two are closest, and on what?

**T6 and M2, on layout and row anatomy.** Both are a header strip with a mark and a utility line, a
filter or eyebrow row, a small monospace caps column-header line, then a repeated record built the
same way left to right: drawn object glyph, name with a monospace sub-line under it, a large numeral,
a monospace status value, a bordered action button at the right edge. The row rhythm, the hairline
between rows and the column order are the same object. Change T6's cream to M2's blue-black and add
a left status bar and you have M2's row. They also share the same weakness in the same place — the
action button is the lightest thing in the row on both.

R9 is the outlier on layout, spacing and imagery: two columns, a held illustration, a serif display
voice, a form instead of a list. It joins the other two on ground treatment (flat, single, no
gradients), on the mono-for-measurement rule, on the drawn asset, and on the closing confession.

## 3. What all three share

A refusal to sell, expressed as a preference for publishing the awkward number.

Concretely, and in a form you could check on a fourth page: one hand-drawn technical asset per page
and no photographs; a monospace reserved for anything that was measured; a flat single-tone ground
with exactly one accent hue that means exactly one thing; a table as the centre of gravity; a stated
limitation before any benefit; and a small grey admission at the foot that the business is invented.

The shared blind spot follows from the same conviction: on all three pages the thing the visitor
came to *do* — order a cut length, send an enquiry, book a consignment in — is the visually lightest
element on the page, and on two of the three it feeds a panel that is empty. These pages are very
good at telling you the truth and noticeably reluctant to let you act on it.
