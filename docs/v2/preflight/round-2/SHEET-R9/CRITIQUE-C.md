notes:
- assets — desktop screens 1–4, left column: one drawing, held static for the full 2,600px of scroll, captioned "TENOR, 14 CWT 2 QR, ST ÆTHELBURGA'S — BEFORE RE-TUNING"; half the viewport is inert for the entire page, and the after that the table publishes is never drawn.
- hierarchy — mobile screen 1: the fold is the small bell drawing plus its four-line caption; the headline "A BELL IS TUNED BY TAKING METAL AWAY" does not begin until roughly 600px down an 844px screen and its supporting paragraph is cut off, so the phone's first screen carries the illustration and not the argument.

The direction is the clearest of anything I have looked at today and it is stated in six words at
the top right of the first screen. A bell is tuned by taking metal away; there is no putting it
back; therefore we publish the before as well as the after. Everything on the page is that sentence
— the section drawing of where the five partials sit, the table of cents before and after with the
kilos removed alongside, the three refusals, the enquiry form that asks what is wrong with it. A
parish reads the whole argument without being sold anything.

Specificity is near the ceiling. Partials named hum, prime, tierce, quint and nominal; weights in
hundredweight and quarters; cents from equal temperament; faculty jurisdiction; Dove's Guide; "we
will not lift a bell out of a frame on a verbal from a churchwarden". Strip the wordmark and the
page could not be anything but a bell foundry, and the drawing alone would carry that.

Type runs three families with clean role assignment — a high-contrast garalde serif for display and
section headings, a humanist sans for body, a monospace for the drawing's partial labels. The
assignment is defensible (voice, body, measurement), but it is one family more than the page needs,
and the body sans is the least chosen thing on it: a default humanist face doing the work under a
deliberately period serif. At hero size the serif caps also sit at loose default tracking and thin
out against the dark ground.

Colour is a single warm gold on a warm near-black, and it is used to mean one thing: metal. It fills
the bell, draws the section lines, sets the wordmark, marks the AFTER column in the table so the
changed figures read as the changed figures, and fills the one submit button. Five placements, one
meaning, no second accent anywhere. The ground is warm rather than neutral, which is the difference
between a foundry and a template.

Craft in the form is better than the page needed it to be: fields with a visible fill and border on
dark, letterspaced small-cap labels, helper text under the two fields that need it ("Between 1 and
16. A ring of more than twelve is rare enough that we will ring you rather than email"), and a
placeholder answer offered in the helper text for the textarea. The disclosure at the foot is
handled the same way. Small misalignment: on mobile the drawing's caption and its hairline rule are
inset from the column's left edge by an arbitrary amount, where on desktop they sit flush to it.

The gap against the brief is the drawing. A parish that came to understand what re-tuning does is
given one static section of a bell before it was cut. Everything the page knows about the after is
in a table of numbers; the picture, which is the thing a churchwarden will actually look at, never
participates.
