## What is on the sheet

Dark, warm-black ground; brass. A half-section of a bell drawn about a dashed centreline, with
dotted leaders out to NOMINAL, QUINT, TIERCE, PRIME, HUM, held in a sticky left column. The right
column carries the argument: the claim, what re-tuning moves, a before/after table in cents with
the metal removed per partial, three things the foundry will not do, then the enquiry form.

**Direction (9).** One sentence: a foundry that argues by publishing its own numbers, on a ground
the colour of the inside of a tower. The headline states the irreversibility ("A bell is tuned by
taking metal away") and everything after it is evidence for why you would trust someone with an
irreversible job. Legible in the first screen and sustained to the last.

**Specificity (9).** Cents from equal temperament, hundredweight and quarters, faculty
jurisdiction, Dove's Guide, "we will not lift a bell out of a frame on a verbal from a
churchwarden". Nothing here transfers to another trade, and little of it transfers to another
foundry. The "WHAT WE WILL NOT DO" section is the strongest piece of writing across everything I
looked at, and it is doing design work as well as copy work — three refusals are how the page
earns the enquiry.

**Type (8).** A serif in caps for the headline, section heads and all letterspaced labels; a
humanist sans for body prose and the bold subheads; a monospace for the diagram annotations only.
Three families, but each has a single job and holds it. The scale is used with intent: one very
large size (the headline), one medium serif caps for sections, one bold sans for the refusal
subheads, one small letterspaced caps for labels. Marked down slightly because the page ends up
with two competing label registers — letterspaced serif caps for captions and form labels,
monospace caps on the drawing — which is one more than the system needs.

**Colour (9).** The accent is doing semantic work, not decoration: in the partials table the
BEFORE figures are cream and the AFTER figures are brass, so the column that represents the
foundry's work is the column that is lit. The same brass then appears once more as the solid
"Send the enquiry" button and nowhere else. The ground is not "dark mode" — it is a specific warm
near-black that makes the drawn bell read as metal.

**Assets and craft (6).** The drawing is real and correctly drafted: half-section about a dashed
centreline, leaders landing on the profile at the right heights, and it survives the mobile width
with its labels still legible, which is not trivial. The table also survives 390px with four
columns, right-aligned, no horizontal scroll. Against that, the form-field contrast, the stock
select and the generic bell glyph named above.

**Hierarchy and rhythm (6).** Desktop is ordered correctly and the section rhythm is doing
something — claim, mechanism, evidence table, refusals, form — rather than alternating bands.
Mobile inverts the opening, as noted. A second, smaller cost of the sticky column: because the
diagram never changes, screens 2, 3 and 4 spend roughly a third of their width on a picture the
reader has already finished with.

**Production-readiness (6).** The content is complete and nothing is broken; the two defects are
both fixable in an afternoon, but both are visible to the customer and one of them is the first
thing a phone shows.
