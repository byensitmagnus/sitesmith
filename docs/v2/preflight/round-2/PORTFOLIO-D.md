## 1. One studio applying one template, or different pieces of work?

On the evidence of T6 and R9, different pieces of work sharing a method rather than a template.

The visual evidence for *different*: the two share no ground (warm manila paper vs warm near-black),
no palette (near-black ink + one oxblood vs cream + brass), no type pairing (grotesk + monospace
vs serif caps + humanist sans + monospace), and no page architecture (a single full-width table
strip vs a persistent two-column split with a sticky illustration). Nothing in R9's layout could
be produced by re-skinning T6.

The evidence for *one hand*: both build the page around a drawn technical asset made specifically
for the subject rather than around photography; both reduce their whole offer to one dense table
of real quantities with units (Ø / MBL / stretch / £ per metre; cents before / cents after / kg
removed); both use a monospace as a signal for "measured" content; both write copy that names what
the business will *not* do or will not guarantee (T6's "nothing sold here is rated for lifting
people", R9's "we will not lift a bell out of a frame on a verbal from a churchwarden"); and both
carry a plain footer disclaiming that the business is invented. That is a shared method and a
shared editorial voice, not a shared stylesheet.

The shared method also produces a shared *failure*, which is the more useful finding: on both
pages the signature drawn asset is the weakest link at the point of conversion. T6's bench rule is
illegible at mobile width; R9's bell is inert next to the form. Both pages spend their craft on
the explanation and let the transaction end thinly — T6's order panel is an empty state with no
length input, R9's form fields are the lowest-contrast elements on the page.

## 2. Which two are closest, and on what?

Not answerable as asked without M2. Between the two I did see, the closest correspondences are
**the table and the monospace**: both pages settle their argument in a right-aligned numeric table
with hairline rules and no fills, both hold that table to four or five columns, and both keep it

ground, type, layout and spacing rhythm are unrelated.

## 3. What do all three share?

Again, two rather than three. What T6 and R9 share is a *proof-first* argument: each leads with a
quantity the trade would recognise and treats persuasion as a by-product of publishing it, with a
drawn diagram doing the explaining that a photograph would normally do. The corollary they also
share is that the drawing is treated as the finished deliverable and the interactive end of the
page — cart, form, empty state — is finished to a visibly lower standard.
