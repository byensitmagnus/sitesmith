notes:
- hierarchy — desktop screen 2: the "Your order" panel is a heading, a two-thirds-width rule and one sentence with roughly 110px of dead cream below it before the footer, while the five "Cut a length" buttons on screens 1–2 are outlined boxes lighter in weight than the prices beside them, so the page's one action sits visually beneath its own data.
- production-readiness — mobile screen 1: the bench rule, the device the whole argument rests on, renders as a ~4px illegible hairline with no numerals and is inset from the left margin so it aligns with neither the text column nor the caption; I would have to apologise for it before showing this to a rigger on a phone.

The direction is legible in the first screen and it is a real one: this is a counter ledger, not a
marketing page. "Every rope on this page is cut from the coil while you wait, and the breaking load
beside it is the batch we will cut from" is the argument, the drawn bench rule under it is the unit
of sale made physical, and the table below is the whole range with nothing withheld. Five
constructions, five prices, one page. That is a sentence, not a list of components.

Specificity is the strongest thing here. Take the logo and the copy away and you still have five
rope cross-sections drawn to distinguish three-strand from double braid from kernmantle from
eight-plait, a column set of Ø / MBL / stretch / per metre, and a ruler. No other trade uses that
set. The copy reinforces it rather than carrying it alone: minimum breaking loads not safe working
loads, cut lengths cannot be returned, "Next coil lands 12 August" on the one out-of-stock line.

Type is two families with clean roles — a condensed grotesque for names and statements, a monospace
for every measured value, the legal block and the eyebrows. Few sizes, used hard. The setting is
recognisable; the faces themselves are workmanlike rather than chosen, and neither would be
identified as this shop's on a second page without the mono columns to carry it.

Colour is disciplined and it is the page's quietest success. One flat cream ground, near-black ink,
and a single oxblood that appears exactly once — on "They are not safe working loads" — and nowhere
else. The accent means one thing. The cost of that restraint is that the ground never does any
second job: the "Your order" panel is not grounded differently from the table, which is part of why
it reads as more list instead of a place.

Craft is consistent. One asset treatment throughout — line cross-sections at a single stroke weight,
correctly cropped at both widths, with the out-of-stock row greyed as a set rather than piecemeal.
The mobile table is genuinely re-thought rather than squashed: each row becomes a labelled Ø / MBL /
stretch / per metre stack with values right-aligned. The one unexplained inconsistency is the dotted
versus solid outer circle on the diagrams (dotted on three-strand, eight-plait and polypropylene;
solid on double braid and kernmantle) — it looks like it means something and nothing tells you what.

Squinting, the order is right for comparing and wrong for buying: statement, rule, table, then the
action almost last and almost weightless. A visitor who came to order a cut length is shown, in
descending order of prominence, the philosophy, the unit, the specifications, the price, and only
then a small outlined box — and never a field in which to type a number of metres.
