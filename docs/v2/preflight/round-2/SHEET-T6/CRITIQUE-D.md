## What is on the sheet

A merchant's cut-list, and it commits to that idea from the first screen. The lede states the
deal in one sentence ("Every rope on this page is cut from the coil while you wait, and the
breaking load beside it is the batch we will cut from"), a drawn bench rule establishes the unit,
and then five rope constructions are listed as a single table with drawn cross-sections, Ø, MBL,
stretch and price per metre. There is no hero image, no testimonial band, no feature grid. That
restraint is a decision, not an absence.

**Direction (8).** Describable in a sentence that is not a list of components: it is a counter
ticket — the thing you would be handed at the shop, printed on the shop's own paper. Legible in
the first screen, and the page does not deviate from it afterwards.

**Specificity (9).** Strip the logo and the copy and this is still a cordage merchant and could
not be anything else. The five section drawings are genuinely different from one another —
three circles for three-strand, a braid ring over a core for double braid, sheath-over-seven-core
for kernmantle, eight circles for eight-plait — and the columns are the ones a rigger actually
compares. The out-of-stock row on desktop screen 2 dims the drawing, replaces the button with
"Out of stock" and adds "Next coil lands 12 August"; that is the behaviour of a business that
sells off coils, not a generic disabled state.

**Type (8).** Two families with a rule between them: a neo-grotesk for names and prose, a
monospace for every measured quantity, every meta line and all the legal text. That split is
applied hard — the price, the diameter, the "£0.00" in the header and the liability paragraph in
the footer are all mono; the rope names and descriptions are all grotesk. It would be recognisable
on a second page. The weakness is that the grotesk itself has no display voice: the largest type
on the page is the 3-line lede at roughly twice body size, so the family is never asked to do
anything a UI default could not.

**Colour (8).** Warm manila ground, near-black ink, and one oxblood red that appears exactly once
in the whole document — on "They are not safe working loads" in the header note (and again in the
same sentence on mobile). One accent, used on the one sentence that carries legal risk, and
nowhere else. That is the most disciplined thing on the sheet. The ground reads as paper rather
than as a light theme.

**Assets and craft (6).** The five sections and the wordmark are one drawing system — the mark is
three interlocking loops built from the same vocabulary as the three-strand section, which is a
real piece of identity work rather than a logotype with a bought icon. Against that: the dashed
"nominal diameter" ring appears on two of the five sections and not the others, and all five
drawings are the same physical size regardless of the 11/12/14mm figures beside them, so the
dashed ring implies a scale the drawings do not keep. Plus the mobile rule, the doubled row rules
and the stray indent named above.

**Hierarchy and rhythm (7).** Squinting, the lede and the rule bar are first, then the table, and
the eye moves in the order the argument needs. The five rows are deliberately identical in weight,
which is right for a comparison. What breaks the rhythm is the bottom: "Your order" is a
large-type heading over an empty paragraph and a rule, followed by a long empty gap before the
footer, so the page's last full section is a hole. On mobile screen 1 the eyebrow "EVERY
CONSTRUCTION WE CUT" wraps onto three lines inside about a quarter of the available column width
— the desktop table-header column width has survived the breakpoint.

**Production-readiness (6).** Everything except the mobile rule and the missing length input is
shippable, and the copy is better than most trade sites will ever have. But those two are in the
first screen and the conversion path respectively.
