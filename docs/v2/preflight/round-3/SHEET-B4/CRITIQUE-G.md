notes:
- colour — the ground never changes: from the end of the hero band to the footer the page is one flat cream, and the cream fill of each row's pricing box is so close in value to the page cream (desktop, screens 2 and 3) that the boxes read as smudges rather than as a plane; the blush safety band is the only tonal event after the photograph.
- assets — the desktop hero is a hard letterbox roughly 1280x220 in which the rope is a half-cropped sliver at the extreme right edge and the brass rule and knife take the frame (desktop, screen 1); on mobile the spec strip flex-wraps so "PER METRE" sits about four pixels under "12 mm" and "BATCH" fails to align to the column above it (mobile, screen 2, Three-strand polyester); and in the ticket both line items orphan "mm" onto a line of its own (desktop and mobile, "two cuts on the ticket" frame).
- hierarchy — past the hero the page is one module repeated five times with identical internal proportions, and the only rhythm event in three screens is the out-of-stock row losing its photograph; combined with the empty right column the desktop composition has nothing to move the eye except more rows (desktop, screens 2 and 3).
- production-readiness — a counter shop that says "Cut at the counter, Mon-Sat" gives no address, no telephone and no email anywhere on the page (desktop and mobile, header and footer), and the ticket dead-ends: after "TICKET TOTAL £158.65" there is no control of any kind, no print, save or send (desktop, "two cuts on the ticket" frame).

What is right, and worth saying plainly. The five product photographs are real, one treatment, one light, one distance, one diagonal, on the same weathered boards, and they are cropped correctly at both 1440 and 375. The spec strip holds identical column positions down all five rows, so the page reads as a comparison table without ever drawing one — that is the single best decision on the sheet. The out-of-stock line refuses to substitute a photograph and says why ("Polypropylene is sold in the trade's blue and orange, and we will not show you white polyester in its place"), and the refused length gives a reason rather than a validation stub ("The minimum cut is 3 m, we cannot sell a shorter length off the coil", desktop, "a length priced, and one refused"). The serif carries the wordmark, the product names, the prices and the ticket total and nothing else; the letterspaced small-caps sans carries every label; the ochre appears on prices and on the button of the row just added and nowhere else. That is a settled system, not a rotated accent.
