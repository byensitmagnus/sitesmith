notes:
- assets — desktop and mobile, state frame "a length priced, and one refused", first row: where the cut price should be there is a single orphaned comma in the price colour, the residue of an empty number, sitting directly under the "THIS CUT" label.
- production-readiness — same frame, both views: I would not put a price field that renders as a comma in front of a rigger, and the disabled "Add to the ticket" beside it drops to grey-on-beige that reads as an unstyled leftover rather than a considered off state.

The page's argument, in a sentence: this is a counter, you say how many metres and we cut it off a
named coil, and the breaking load and the batch stand next to the price so you can check what you
carried out. That reads off the first screen without help — masthead, then a pink band that says
these are minimum breaking loads and nothing here is rated for lifting people, then a photograph of
a boxwood rule, a knife and a coil on planks, then immediately the first construction with its
stepper and a live £24.00. Nothing in the first screen is decoration.

Specificity is the strongest thing here. Strip the wordmark and this still cannot be another trade:
the spec strip is DIAMETER / BREAKING LOAD / STRETCH / PER METRE / BATCH, the ticket carries the
batch number down into every line item, the refusal is "the minimum cut is 3 m, we cannot sell a
shorter length off the coil", and the out-of-stock row refuses to substitute a photograph —
"Polypropylene is sold in the trade's blue and orange, and we will not show you white polyester in
its place." That last one is a design decision, not copy: a dashed empty box where a stock image
would have gone.

Type is a serif for the wordmark, headings and every measured value, a humanist sans for prose, and
letterspaced small caps for the micro-labels. Few sizes, used hard. The signature that would be
recognisable on a second page is the label-over-value spec strip and the serif numerals — £103.75,
12 mm, 3.8 t all in the same face. One wobble: on desktop the section head "Every construction we
cut" and the row titles sit at almost the same size, so the section does not clearly outrank its
contents.

Colour comes from the photographs — cream ground, warm beige for the control strips so they read as
a counter surface, black, and a single ochre reserved for money and nothing else. That reservation
is held everywhere. The pale pink band with dark red text is the one loud thing and it is loud on
purpose. The dark green bar on the "added" confirmation is the only hue outside the family; it
appears once.

Photography is real, one treatment, one plank table, one daylight, each rope shot at the same
distance and cropped correctly at 1440 and 375. The ticket panel is well made: line item, batch,
Remove, rule, TICKET TOTAL in spaced small caps against a serif £158.65. Its line items wrap so
"mm" orphans onto a second line beside a top-aligned price, which is ragged but minor.

Rhythm is the honest weakness. After the hero the page is five identical rows and then a footer;
the only compositional events in the whole scroll are the hero card and the out-of-stock row. That
is defensible for a price list and it is not alternating bands for the sake of it, but there is no
second idea. On mobile the masthead wraps so "Cut at the counter, Mon–Sat" lands right-aligned on
its own line under a left-aligned wordmark, which reads as a flex artefact rather than a composed
head.
