- type — mobile screen 1: the H1 renders as "TUNED BY / TAKING METAL / AWAY" with "A BELL IS" clipped by the header block above it, and on desktop screens 2 and 3 the section heads break to leave single-word orphans, "WHAT WE WILL NOT / DO" and "TELL US ABOUT THE / RING".
- assets — desktop and mobile, partials table, Nominal row, in both the before and after states: the METAL OFF cell contains a lone comma instead of a figure, so the last line of the table that is the page's whole argument has no number in it.
- hierarchy — desktop state frame "the enquiry sent": the receipt sits in the left column while a reset, live form with a full-strength gold "SEND THE ENQUIRY" occupies the right, and the button is louder than the confirmation it is meant to follow.
- production-readiness — mobile screen 1: the gold hero CTA overflows its container and covers the first line of "WHAT RE-TUNING ACTUALLY MOVES", and its trailing ", we answer within a week" sits on its own baseline starting with a floating comma.

Desktop, this is the best-argued page of the three. The claim is stated in the hero — we publish the
before as well as the after, because a foundry that will not show you the before is asking you to
take the after on trust — and then the page makes that claim operable with a BEFORE / AFTER toggle
over a five-partial table in cents and kilograms. The visual argument is one sentence: metal is
removed and never put back, so here is exactly how much and from where. Gold is the cut metal, black
is the patina, and the macro photograph captioned "inside the soundbow, after cutting" makes the
palette literal — the bright bands in the image are the same gold as the type.

Specificity is not in doubt. Faculty jurisdiction, headstocks, "we will not lift a bell out of a
frame on a verbal from a churchwarden", a form field that asks how many bells are in the ring and
tells you that above twelve they would rather telephone. Strip the wordmark and the copy and the
photography alone still says bell foundry.

Type: a heavy condensed grotesque for display, a humanist sans for prose, and what looks like a
tabular mono for the cents and the kilograms — the right decision, and the numeric columns line up
because of it. The scale is few sizes used hard. But the setting is not finished. The display face
is being asked to wrap inside a narrow left column and it orphans single words at large size, and
on mobile the H1 has lost its first line entirely.

The error state is the best-made thing on the sheet: fields tinted, messages in the trade's voice,
"Say where the faculty stands, even if that is 'I do not know'." Two flaws in it. The bells field
stacks a permanent hint and an error message that both open "Between 1 and 16", so the visitor reads
the same instruction twice in two colours. And the number input shows the browser's default spinner
chevrons, an unstyled OS control sitting inside an otherwise carefully built dark form. In the
neutral state the fields have no fill at all — a hairline outline on near-black — so the form is
barely visible until you focus it.

Craft elsewhere is strong: four photographs, one treatment, raking light on bronze, correctly
cropped at both widths, each with a caption that carries a rule of the trade. The lapse is the
Nominal row's missing figure, which is not a detail — it is the last row of the table the page is
built around, and it is empty in both states and at both widths. On desktop the "inside the
soundbow" caption is jammed against the section divider so it reads as belonging to the form below
it rather than the image above.

Rhythm on desktop is real and not band-alternation: hero, data table, a three-up of refusals, a
full-width statement set against a macro image, then the form. Mobile is where it comes apart, and
mobile is where a churchwarden will read it.
