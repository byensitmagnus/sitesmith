notes:
- direction — desktop and mobile screen 1: the ordering argument is asserted in a sentence, "Late first, then due today", but the only thing the design does to carry it is a 6px colour bar at the far left of each row, while the four white buttons at the right pull harder than the bar does.
- specificity — with the wordmark and the copy removed, what remains on desktop screen 1 is a slate card list with red/amber/blue severity bars, spaced small-caps field labels, a select, a number input and a white primary button; that shell would serve any returns or inventory desk in any trade.
- type — desktop screen 1, rows two and three: the count numeral touches its unit label with no space, "4KILDERKINS", and the rest of the string wraps so "GAL" drops to a second line left-aligned under the numeral, orphaned from the "72" it belongs to.
- colour — desktop and mobile state frame "a book-in refused": the primary button turns saturated mint green, a hue used nowhere else, at the exact moment the field beside it is ringed in amber with a red error underneath.
- assets — the page carries no working imagery; the single photographic element is a brick texture in the masthead knocked back so far it reads as sensor noise rather than a cellar wall, and the cask line-glyph is the only mark on the page.
- hierarchy — desktop and mobile state frame "the consignment booked back in": the same event is printed twice in two stacked green-barred blocks, the record line and then a sentence repeating it, and the duplicate is set larger and brighter than the record it duplicates.
- production-readiness — the doubled confirmation and the green button on a refusal are both things I would have to explain to a cellarman before letting him use it.

Read plainly: this is the category default, built competently. The parts that make it the default
are the slate near-black ground, the card-per-row list, the coloured left rail for severity, the
label-above-control field pattern, the three KPI figures top right, and a white primary button
repeated once per row. None of that was chosen for a brewery; it is what a dark operations screen
looks like.

The parts that are genuinely the trade's are in the words and the units, not in the design: firkins
and kilderkins with their gallonages, gyle numbers set in mono so they read as record identifiers,
"ULLAGE, PT" as a field, and the line that does the real work — "a cask coming back is a duty entry
and not just a count". The masthead line "Cellar, Thursday · 06:40 · dray in at 07:15" is a good
piece of writing. But if a reviewer has to quote the copy to prove the design belongs to this trade,
the design is not doing it.

What works. The severity order is real and the header counters move honestly with it — booking the
late consignment takes LATE from 2 to 0 and CASKS OUT from 15 to 13, and the row leaves the list.
The refusal message is specific and in voice: "Say what condition it came back in, it goes on the
duty record." The mobile reflow is the cleanest of the three sheets — the row folds into a stacked
card with the condition and ullage side by side and a full-width action, and nothing is clipped.

What does not. On mobile the booked record wraps around a right-floated Undo, so the meta line reads
"gyle 212 · ullage short · ullage" with Undo beside it and "11 pt" stranded on the next line. The
mono meta string also says "ullage" twice in one breath. Squint at either width and the page is a
ladder of white bars; the red chip that is the whole point is smaller and less bright than the
button under it. There is no second treatment anywhere in the scroll — one card, repeated, then a
log, then a footer. It is short, legible and undemanding, and it would work. It has not been
designed so much as assembled correctly.
