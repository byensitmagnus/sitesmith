## 1. One studio and one template, or three pieces of work?

One studio, one template, with real per-subject art direction laid over it on two of the three.

The template is visible in the parts a visitor does not consciously read:

- **The same label-over-value unit on all three.** Letterspaced small caps in a muted grey above a
  bold value, in a horizontal strip: `DIAMETER / BREAKING LOAD / STRETCH / PER METRE / BATCH` on B4,
  `PARTIAL / CENTS / METAL OFF` on K7, `CONDITION / ULLAGE, PT` and `LATE / DUE TODAY / CASKS OUT`
  on R9. Same size relationship, same tracking, same colour relationship every time.
- **The same body face.** The prose sans is, as far as I can tell at these sizes, identical across
  all three sheets, including the same grey for secondary text and the same generous line height.
  Only the display face changes: serif on B4, condensed grotesque on K7, and on R9 there is none.
- **The same coloured-left-bar callout.** B4's "18 m of Kernmantle added" note, K7's "ENQUIRY SENT"
  receipt, R9's "Booked in this week" entries. One component, recoloured green, gold, green.
- **The same action row.** A control, an adjacent value, and a primary button at the right end:
  stepper + price + "Add to the ticket"; select + number + "Book in". Same proportions.
- **The same rhetorical move, three times.** Every page has a state in which it refuses the visitor
  in one specific sentence of trade voice, and every page has a footer that admits the business is
  invented. Three different studios do not independently arrive at that.
- **The same defect.** An empty numeric value renders as a lone comma — B4's "THIS CUT" price in the
  refused frame, K7's Nominal metal-off cell in both the before and the after states. Two sheets,
  two subjects, one identical failure mode. That is shared code, not coincidence.

Against that, B4 and K7 are not a recolour of each other. The photography on each was directed for
its subject and captioned in its subject's voice, the display faces are genuinely different choices,
and the page shapes differ — a priced list with a persistent ticket versus an editorial argument
ending in a form. That is a studio with a kit, not a studio with a skin.

## 2. Which two are closest, and on what?

**B4 and K7**, on type and on imagery — despite having opposite grounds.

- **Type.** Both run a chosen display face against the shared humanist sans, and both use the
  letterspaced small-caps label as the connective tissue of a data block. Both set their measured
  values in a face distinct from the prose. R9 has no display face at all; its wordmark "Cask desk"
  is the body sans in bold.
- **Imagery.** Both are built on photographs of the trade's own objects, one treatment per page,
  correctly cropped at both widths, each carrying a caption that states a rule — "not photographed,
  polypropylene is sold in the trade's blue and orange"; "mouth up on the boring machine, we do not
  lift a bell out of a frame on a verbal". R9 has one invisible texture and a line glyph.
- **Accent discipline.** Both reserve a single warm accent for the measured thing and hold it there:
  ochre for money on B4, gold for cut metal on K7. R9 lets white — the loudest value on the page —
  sit on every button, then introduces a mint green that appears once.

K7 and R9 are closer only on ground value, and that similarity is superficial: K7's black is warm
and brown-shifted and comes out of the bell photography, R9's is a cool slate that comes from
nowhere in particular.

## 3. What do all three share?

A refusal. Each page is organised around telling the visitor what it will not do, and each earns its
credibility by publishing the number that makes the refusal checkable — the batch the cut came off,
the before-tuning cents as well as the after, the duty entry rather than the count. Visually that
shows up the same way three times: a small-caps label, a hard value under it, and one accent colour
allowed to touch only that value. It is a real, transferable house idea, and it is the reason B4 and
K7 read as designed rather than assembled. R9 inherited the idea in its copy but not in its
composition.
