## What the sheet is doing well, named

The type is the strongest thing here and it is a system, not a ramp. Three faces, each with a rule it never breaks: a letterspaced condensed sans in caps for structure (TRELFALL & SON, ON THE RACK, THE TICKET, BREAKING LOAD IS NOT A WORKING LOAD); an old-style serif with text figures for every sentence of prose; and a monospace that only ever carries something measured — £2.40, 3.1 t, TS12-2431, 184 m, 20 m, 07:00 to 16:30, and the en-dash that stands in for the missing batch on the uncertified line. Two pages into this site you would know it from the mono alone.

The ground is a kraft sand rather than an off-white, the spec strips are a second, darker sand, and the footer inverts to a warm near-black with the accent lightened to a rose so the two contact links still carry (sampled around 6:1). The brick red appears on the cut button, the safety rule and links, and nowhere else.

The photographs are one shoot: five rope ends on the same bench, same light, same distance, cropped correctly at 1280 and at 390 where they become a full-width band. The mark is a three-strand rope cross-section seen end-on — an actual mark, not a monogram.

The ticket is sticky on desktop (it re-pins at the top of screen 2 at 900 px of scroll and releases when the rack ends), and on mobile it becomes a persistent bottom bar carrying cuts and total. That is the correct pair of solutions, and the empty right column beneath the pinned card on desktop is a consequence of a sticky summary, not a hole.

Direction is legible before you scroll: eyebrow, headline, safety caveat, ON THE RACK, then a row with a photograph, a construction description, a spec strip and a cut control. The argument is "this is the counter's price list and you can order a length off it", and it is stated, not implied.
