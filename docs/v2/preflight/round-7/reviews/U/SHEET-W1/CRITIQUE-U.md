## What the sheet is doing well, named

The direction is stated in the first screen and it is a real argument rather than a component list: what is overdue is printed on slate, what is in hand is printed on paper. LATE BACK is white type on near-black with red day counts; DUE TODAY flips the whole band to cream with black type and black BOOK IN chips. The inversion carries the meaning, and it needs no legend.

The specificity is the highest of anything on this sheet. Gyle numbers on every consignment, firkin / kilderkin / barrel with their gallonages given in the footnote, ullage recorded in gallons before a wet cask is tipped, "Board as at Thu 06:40, dray 07:15" in the masthead, and a "last booked in" ticker line under it. Strip the wordmark and this is still unmistakably a brewery cellar desk and not a generic operations dashboard.

The cask pictogram is the best craft idea here: a small drawn barrel repeated once per cask, so six kilderkins is six glyphs and you read the size of the problem before you read the number. It is also the page's mark, reused at the head of the masthead. That is a mark doing two jobs.

The type is committed — one condensed grotesque for pub names and section heads, monospace for every figure and every sentence — and it is recognisable. It is scored 8 rather than 9 because mono is asked to carry two long prose blocks at the foot and the THIS WEEK intro, where it is doing legibility no favours and where there is no third voice to hand the work to.

Colour is 8 for the same reason as hierarchy: slate and paper and one red, with the red correctly rationed to lateness alone, but the inversion used exactly once across a three-screen page.

The responsive transformation is genuinely worked, not merely survived: at 390 px each cask becomes a stacked card — name, gyle line, pictograms, casks-back and ullage side by side, the four condition cells across, BOOK IN full width — and the record table drops its GALLONS column rather than squeezing six into the width, which is why the desktop collision does not reappear on mobile.
