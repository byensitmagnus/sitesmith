## 1. One studio applying one template, or three pieces of work?

One studio, one template, applied with real care to three different grounds. The evidence is
specific and it repeats:

- **The same micro-label device on all three.** Letterspaced monospace caps at roughly 11 px,
  used for every field label, column head and eyebrow: PER METRE / ON THE COIL (M2), BELL TUNING
  AND FRAME WORK / CROWN AT THE TOP, LIP AT THE BOTTOM (R9), CASKS BACK / ULLAGE, GALLONS (W1).
  Same size, same tracking, same colour relationship to the ground.
- **The same display face.** A flat-terminal, slightly condensed grotesque: "A TUNED BELL IS A
  LIGHTER BELL", "LATE BACK", "Rope off the coil". R9 and W1 use it in caps; M2 uses it in
  sentence case for the headline and in caps for section heads. The A, B and E shapes match.
- **The same mark construction.** A single trade-derived line glyph at the same weight, in the
  same masthead position, left of a two-line lockup (name over category): a three-strand rope
  seen end-on, a bell section with five ticks at the five partial heights, a cask end with a
  shive hole. Three different ideas, one drawing hand.
- **The same table.** Hairline rule under a monospace column head, generous row padding, a
  secondary line of grey sub-label beneath the bold cell: "WHICH LINE / WHAT WE WOULD HAND YOU",
  "PARTIAL / CENTS / METAL OFF", "WHEN / PUB AND GYLE / CASKS".
- **The same accent discipline.** Exactly one hue per page, used three or four times and nowhere
  else: oxide red on ADD THE CUT, the warning rule and the footer links; amber on ENQUIRE, the
  hero CTA, "23 lb" and SEND THE ENQUIRY; signal red on the days-late numerals only.
- **The same closing move.** A dark band of small print that states a limit rather than a claim —
  "We publish no ratings, no reviews and no delivery promises", "A cracked bell is not a tuning
  job", "A cask goes from this screen to the wash and nowhere else."
- **The same copy device mid-page.** A bordered aside naming what the business will *not* do:
  "BREAKING LOAD IS NOT A WORKING LOAD", "THREE THINGS WE WILL NOT DO", "We will not print a
  working load".

None of that is disqualifying — it is a house style, and a good one. But it is a recipe, and a
second page from any of these three would be predictable from the other two.

## 2. Which two are closest, and on what?

**M2 and W1**, on layout and ground.

Both are built out of a repeated transaction row with the same skeleton: subject name in
grotesque caps on the left, an identifying line of monospace metadata beneath it, a mono numeric
block in the middle, an editable field or two, then a single filled action button flush right
(ADD THE CUT / BOOK IN). Both sit on a warm paper ground in the same family (ecru at roughly
#EDE7D5 on M2, greige at #EAE6DD and #E6E1DB on W1), both invert to a near-black band, both use
that inversion for the same job — the thing that matters most or the thing said last. Both also
share the same input rule: the field fill is one step lighter than whatever ground it sits on.

R9 is the outlier on ground and imagery — near-black is the page rather than a band, and it
carries two full-bleed photographs where the other two carry none — but it shares W1's all-caps
grotesque display and shares M2's editorial column rhythm. W1 is the hinge: it has M2's row
layout and R9's dark.

## 3. What all three share

A documentary posture. Each page is composed as a *record* rather than a pitch: figures set in
monospace as though transcribed from a ledger (batch TS12-2431, gyle 214, −31 cents), a named
refusal placed early and given real estate, an empty or unavailable state shown honestly and
explained rather than hidden (the polypropylene with no coil to cut from, the tierce they did not
bring to true, the cask condemned to scrap), and one accent colour held back for the single
number or the single action that carries the consequence. All three trust the reader with trade
vocabulary and none of them explain it apologetically. That posture, not the typeface, is the
signature.
