# Visual source plan — Marrow & Kell

> Written before anything is generated. Media strategy: **cinematic craft, material and
> process**. A foundry is heat, weight and sound. The old page had one line drawing holding half
> of every screen and a reviewer called the bell glyph itself generic. This page is carried by
> the metal and the work, at long lens and low light.

## The strategy in one line

Three photographs of the process in the order it happens — the swarf coming off, the bell on the
machine, the bronze after — lit like a workshop at night, not like a product catalogue.

## What is deliberately **not** here

- No line drawing as the primary asset, and no drawing holding a full-height column. That was
  the old page and it failed twice.
- No polished bell against black with a rim light. That is the luxury costume the brief forbids.
- No gold, no serif-and-gold heritage styling, no cathedral interior at golden hour.
- No hands in hero position, no faces.

---

## `swarf`

- role: the opening picture, immediately under the headline — the argument in one image
- why: the page's whole claim is that tuning is subtractive and irreversible. Metal coming off a bell is that claim, photographed
- strategy: generate
- medium: photograph, close process study
- subject: a fresh curl of bronze swarf lifting off a cutting tool inside a bell, bright metal against the dark oxidised surface around it, cutting fluid catching the light
- materials: bell bronze, roughly four parts copper to one tin; cutting fluid; steel tooling
- composition: very close, shallow depth, the curl at the left third with the cut band running away into darkness to the right
- lighting: one hard working lamp from the upper left, everything else falling to near-black, no fill
- aspect-ratios: 3:2; 4:5
- crops: 1200x800; 390x488
- focal: 34% 46%
- anti-references: a polished bell against black with a rim light; sparks flying dramatically; a golden-hour cathedral
- must-not-change: the swarf must read as bronze, not steel, and the cut surface must be brighter than the surrounding patina
- factual-risk: showing sparks or a grinding wheel would depict a process this foundry does not use -- tuning is cut on a lathe, not ground
- max-attempts: 2
- candidates: 4
- capability: text-to-image

## `bell-on-the-machine`

- role: the picture that carries the middle of the page, where the before-and-after figures are
- why: the visitor has to see that a bell is a workpiece that goes on a machine, not an object handled reverently
- strategy: generate
- medium: photograph, wider workshop study
- subject: a church bell mouth-up on a vertical boring machine in a working foundry, the inside of the bell visible, tooling above it, the shop dark behind
- materials: bell bronze with old patina; cast-iron machine bed; oil
- composition: from slightly above and to one side, the bell filling the lower two thirds, the machine head entering from the top, deep shadow behind
- lighting: one overhead workshop lamp, hard, plus a dim cool spill from a window far behind; everything unlit falls to near-black
- aspect-ratios: 3:2; 1:1
- crops: 1200x800; 390x390
- focal: 48% 58%
- anti-references: a bell hanging in a tower; a bell on a plinth in a museum; anything lit evenly
- must-not-change: the bell is mouth-up on a machine bed, and the metal is dull patinated bronze rather than polished
- factual-risk: a bell shown hanging in a frame would contradict the foundry's stated refusal to work on a bell in situ
- max-attempts: 2
- candidates: 4
- capability: text-to-image

## `bronze-after`

- role: the closing picture, beside the enquiry, and the "after" half of the page's argument
- why: the page publishes what was removed. The visitor should see what the cut surface actually looks like when the work is done
- strategy: generate
- medium: photograph, macro material study
- subject: the inside wall of a bell after tuning — concentric machined bands in bright bronze crossing the older dark patina, tool marks visible, no text and no markings
- materials: freshly cut bell bronze; old patina
- composition: filling the frame, bands running diagonally, no edges of the bell in shot, abstract enough to read as material rather than object
- lighting: raking light from the right so the tool marks read as texture, falling off sharply
- aspect-ratios: 16:9; 1:1
- crops: 1200x675; 390x390
- focal: 50% 50%
- anti-references: a gold-graded metal texture; a generic bronze background; anything with a lens flare
- must-not-change: machined concentric bands, and bronze colour rather than gold or brass
- factual-risk: a gold-looking surface would misrepresent bell metal, which is a pale grey-gold bronze, and would pull the page toward the luxury costume the brief forbids
- max-attempts: 2
- candidates: 3
- capability: text-to-image
