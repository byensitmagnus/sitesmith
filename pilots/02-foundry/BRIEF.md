# BRIEF — Marrow & Kell, bellfounders

> Verified input. Survives the rebuild. Nothing here describes a design.

## The subject

A bell foundry that re-tunes church bells: cutting metal from the inside of a bell at the
heights where each partial sounds, so a ring comes back into tune with itself. The subject is
fictional; the craft is not, and every figure in [`EVIDENCE.md`](EVIDENCE.md) comes from it.

## What the site is

**Marketing and portfolio with character** — a place with heat, weight, sound and handwork. Not
a technical paper, and not a luxury brand site.

## What a visitor came to do

A parish — a churchwarden, a tower captain, an architect — needs to understand what re-tuning
does to their ring, decide whether to commission it, and send an enquiry that gets a useful
answer.

They arrive not knowing the vocabulary. They must be able to:

1. understand that tuning is **subtractive** — metal comes off and does not go back;
2. see the before and the after as a real change, not only as a static table;
3. see that the foundry publishes the unflattering figure as well as the flattering one;
4. send an enquiry with the facts a foundry actually needs.

## Constraints that are not negotiable

- **A bell that is cracked is not a tuning job.** The site says so before it sells anything.
- **Bells in a church are under faculty jurisdiction.** The foundry can advise and wait; it will
  not lift a bell out of a frame on a verbal from a churchwarden.
- **The foundry will not quote from a photograph.** Weights and notes are on record; the frame,
  the fittings and the state of the headstocks need somebody in the tower.
- The published cents figures and metal-off weights are the St Æthelburga's tenor and appear in
  `EVIDENCE.md`. They are invented for this exercise and are not those of any real ring.

## Success

A parish that has never commissioned tuning understands what it buys, believes the foundry, and
can start the enquiry **without scrolling and without guessing**.
