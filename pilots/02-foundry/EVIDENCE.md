# EVIDENCE — Marrow & Kell, bellfounders

> Pilot 2, mode **M**. Subject is fictional; the trade is not. A working bell foundry casting
> and tuning change-ringing bells, and restoring hung frames.

## 1. Artefacts

- The **profile** — a bell's section drawing, from crown through shoulder, waist, soundbow to
  lip. Every foundry has its own profile and it is the closest thing the trade has to a
  signature.
- The **strickle board**: the wooden template swept round to shape the mould.
- **Cope and core** — the two halves of the loam mould.
- The **tuning fork test** and the vertical boring machine that shaves the inside.
- The **inscription** cast into the waist: founder, date, dedication, sometimes a rhyme.
- The **headstock, wheel, stay and slider** of a hung bell.
- The tuning book: five partials per bell, in cents, before and after.

## 2. Vocabulary

Uses: profile, soundbow, waist, shoulder, crown, lip, canons, hum, prime, tierce, quint,
nominal, partials, cents, true-harmonic, strickle, cope, core, loam, headstock, stay, slider,
peal, ring of eight, tenor, treble, hung dead, chimed.

Does not use: artisan, heritage-inspired, timeless craftsmanship, journey, passion.
A founder says "the tenor is 14 cwt, nominal C sharp, tuned true-harmonic in 1904 and
re-tuned by us in 2019".

## 3. Materials and surfaces

Bell metal: roughly 78 % copper, 22 % tin. Cast surface is matte and slightly pitted; the
turned inside is bright. Loam is grey-brown and damp. The foundry floor is sand. Oak
headstocks, iron fittings, hemp bell ropes with woollen sallies.

A bell is heavy, and everything in the trade is about mass: weights are quoted in
hundredweight, and the tenor's weight is how a ring is described.

## 4. Colour that is already true

- Fresh bell metal: pale gold, `#c8a96a`-ish, going to a mid brown-green with age.
- Old bells: dark verdigris-brown, near `#4a4034` in shadow.
- Loam and sand: warm grey.
- The casting pit at pour: white-orange, the only bright thing in the building.
- Ringing chamber walls: limewash white.

The truest pairing is **bell metal against near-black** — which is exactly what a bell looks
like in a tower with one window.

## 5. Constraints already in force

- **Weights and notes are matters of record.** A bell's weight, note and founder are
  published in Dove's Guide and can be checked. Nothing about a specific bell may be invented.
- Tuning claims are measurable in cents. "Perfectly tuned" is not a claim; "+2 cents on the
  nominal" is.
- Faculty jurisdiction: work on a church bell needs permission from the diocese. That changes
  the enquiry — the first question is not "when can you start".
- A ring of bells cannot be photographed usefully from the ground; the interesting views are
  the profile and the frame.

## 6. References and anti-references

**References**

1. The **bell profile drawing** as printed in tuning books — a half-section with the partials
   marked at the heights they sound from.
2. **Cast inscription lettering**: wide, unhurried capitals, the letterforms of the founder's
   own stamps.
3. **Tuning tables**: five partials, in cents, before and after, set as figures.

**Anti-references**

1. **The heritage-craft brochure site**: sepia photograph of hands, a serif quote, "four
   generations". Every foundry, distillery and forge site is this.
2. **The dark luxury landing page** — black ground, thin gold serif, centred. This is the
   nearest miss and the most tempting, because bell metal *is* gold-coloured.
3. **A hero photograph of a church exterior.** It is a photograph of somebody else's building.

## 8. The published tuning book — St Æthelburga's tenor

> Restored to this file during the rebuild. `BRIEF.md` states that the published cents figures
> and metal-off weights are the St Æthelburga's tenor and appear here, and that they are
> invented for this exercise and are not those of any real ring. The section itself was missing
> from the pack, so it is written back in full and every figure the page publishes is drawn from
> it and from sections 2 to 5 above.

The bell: the tenor of a ring of eight. **14 cwt**, nominal **C sharp**, tuned true-harmonic in
**1904**, back on our machine in **2019**. 14 cwt is **1,568 lb** (112 lb to the hundredweight).

Every value is **cents away from true-harmonic**: nought is where that partial should sit, a
minus is flat of it and a plus is sharp of it. Each partial is cut from a different height of
the inside wall.

| Partial | Cut at | 1904, as it came in | 2019, as it went out | Metal off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hum | the crown | −31 | −4 | 2 lb |
| Prime | the upper waist | +18 | +3 | 3 lb |
| Tierce | the lower waist | −22 | −5 | 4 lb |
| Quint | above the soundbow | +14 | +2 | 5 lb |
| Nominal | the lip | −9 | +2 | 9 lb |

**Total metal off: 23 lb**, off a bell of 1,568 lb.

The nominal's after figure is the `+2 cents on the nominal` given as the exemplar in section 5.

**The unflattering figure.** The tierce came in 22 cents flat and went out 5 cents flat, and it
stays there. Bringing it the rest of the way meant cutting the lower waist thinner than the
foundry is willing to leave a bell that has to keep swinging. The foundry publishes the five
rather than take the metal.

**The bound on an enquiry.** From `JOURNEY-INTENT.md`: a ring of more than **twelve** bells is
rare enough that the foundry rings rather than emails, so an enquiry form is bounded at one to
twelve and says why.
