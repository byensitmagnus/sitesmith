# EVIDENCE — Trelfall & Son, rope and cordage

> Pilot 1, mode **E**. Gathered before any visual decision. Nothing here picks a colour.
> Subject is fictional; the trade is not, and every fact below comes from the trade.

## 1. Artefacts

- Coils on a horizontal rack, cut to order from the end.
- The **cut ticket**: length, diameter, construction, price per metre, the running total.
- A **cross-section** — the thing a buyer actually compares. Three-strand shows a visible lay;
  double-braid shows a core inside a cover; kernmantle shows a sheath over parallel fibres.
- A **breaking-load certificate** for a specific batch, with a batch number.
- An **eye splice**, tucked and tapered, sometimes with a thimble.
- **Whipping twine** and a hot knife; every cut end is sealed or whipped.
- A **rope tag** at the coil end: material, diameter, MBL, batch.

## 2. Vocabulary

Uses: lay, strand, core, cover, sheath, kernmantle, double braid, three-strand, eight-plait,
MBL, SWL, breaking load, splice, whipping, chafe, creep, stretch, elongation, spliceable,
soft eye, thimble, snatch load, cut to the metre, off the coil.

Does not use: premium, curated, solutions, engineered for excellence, journey, bespoke.
A chandler says "12 mm three-strand polyester, 3.1 tonnes, £2.40 a metre".

## 3. Materials and surfaces

Polyester (workhorse, low stretch, UV-stable), nylon (stretches, absorbs shock, weakens wet),
polypropylene (floats, cheap, degrades in sun), HMPE/Dyneema (as strong as steel by weight,
creeps under sustained load), manila and sisal (natural, still used for decorative and
agricultural work, rots).

The surfaces are fibrous and matte. Nothing in this trade is glossy. Rope is sold by the
metre from a coil, so the unit of the whole business is **a length**, not an object.

## 4. Colour that is already true

- Manila: pale straw, `#c9a678`-ish, darkening with age.
- Tarred hemp: near-black brown.
- Polypropylene: the trade's safety blue and hazard orange, chosen for visibility.
- Dyneema: bright white, sometimes with a single coloured tracer yarn.
- Galvanised hardware: cool grey.
- The certificate and the ticket: white paper, black type, one red stamp.

No brand colour exists yet. The strongest true colour in this world is the **straw of manila
against the black of tar** — and the buff of a paper catalogue behind both.

## 5. Constraints already in force

- **Breaking loads must be stated accurately and attributed to a batch.** A stated MBL that
  cannot be evidenced is a safety claim, not marketing copy.
- Safe working load is a ratio applied to MBL, and the ratio depends on use. The site must not
  publish an SWL as if it were a property of the rope.
- **Nothing here is rated for lifting persons.** That must be visible, not buried.
- Rope is cut to order, so the buying control is a **length**, not a quantity of units, and a
  cut length cannot be returned.
- Prices move with the polymer market; a price is per metre and dated.

## 6. References and anti-references

**References**

1. A printed chandler's catalogue — the specification table is the page, not an illustration
   sitting beside prose.
2. Wire-rope manufacturers' data sheets: figures set tabular, units in a fixed column, the
   diameter driving the row order.
3. Admiralty chart lettering — condensed, confident, purely functional.

**Anti-references**

1. **The generic direct-to-consumer store** — lifestyle photograph, three benefit cards,
   testimonial band. Every competitor's site is this, and it hides the only figures the buyer
   came for.
2. **"Premium" black-and-gold heritage styling.** This is a trade counter, not a distillery.
3. **A hero photograph of a harbour at sunset.** It says nothing about rope and would be the
   easiest thing to reach for.
