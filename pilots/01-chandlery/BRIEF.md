# BRIEF — Trelfall & Son, rope and cordage

> Verified input. Survives the rebuild. Nothing here describes a design.

## The subject

A rope and cordage merchant on a fishing dock in Grimsby. Rope is sold **by the metre**, cut
from a coil on the counter while the buyer waits. The subject is fictional; the trade is not,
and every fact in [`EVIDENCE.md`](EVIDENCE.md) comes from the trade.

## What the site is

A **complete e-commerce experience**. Not a catalogue, not a specification table with a button
on the end. A buyer must be able to choose a construction, choose a length, see the price move,
and place the cut in an order — and see that order build.

## What a visitor came to do

A rigger needs a working line. They arrive knowing the job, not the product. They must be able
to:

1. tell the constructions apart — three-strand from double braid from kernmantle — because that
   choice *is* the purchase decision in this trade;
2. read the figure that decides safety, the minimum breaking load, against the batch it belongs
   to;
3. set a length, in metres, and see what it costs;
4. put it in an order and see the order total.

## Constraints that are not negotiable

- **Breaking loads are batch figures.** A stated MBL that cannot be pointed at is a safety
  claim, not marketing copy. Every figure on the page appears in `EVIDENCE.md` §8.
- **Safe working load is not a property of the rope.** It is a ratio applied to MBL and it
  depends on the use. The site must not publish one as if it were.
- **Nothing sold is rated for lifting people.** Visible, not buried.
- A cut length cannot be returned. The buying control is a **length**, not a quantity of units.
- Minimum cut is 3 m. Whipping both ends is £1.80 per cut.

## Success

A visitor understands what they are buying and can start the purchase **without scrolling and
without guessing**, and the order they build shows them what they have chosen and what it costs.
