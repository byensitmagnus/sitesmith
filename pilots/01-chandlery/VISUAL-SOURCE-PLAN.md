# Visual source plan — Trelfall & Son

> Written before anything is generated. Media strategy: **tactile product and material
> photography**. This shop sells a physical material by the metre and the buyer's whole decision
> is which construction to use, so the pictures have to be close enough to see fibre, lay and
> braid. Not a harbour, not a boat, not a lifestyle.

## The strategy in one line

Close, matter-of-fact material photography on a working bench, under one north window. The
pictures do the job a line drawing was doing badly: they let a rigger tell three-strand from
double braid from kernmantle at a glance.

## What is deliberately **not** here

- No diagrammatic cross-sections as the primary asset. That was the old page's whole idea and
  two blind rounds found it read as one studio method shared with two other sites.
- No wide harbour, dock, boat or sunset. Anti-reference 3 in `EVIDENCE.md`.
- No hands holding a product toward camera in the stock-photo posture.

## `rope-three-strand`

- role: the product picture for the three-strand polyester line, at the size a buyer compares at
- why: choosing between three-strand and double braid is the purchase decision in this trade, and a buyer cannot make it from a name and a number
- strategy: generate
- medium: photograph, close product study
- subject: a cut end of 12 mm three-strand polyester rope lying on a worn oak bench, the three strands and the twist of the lay clearly readable, the cut end heat-sealed
- materials: white polyester fibre; worn oak; a little dust
- composition: close, from directly above, rope running left to right across the frame, cut end at the right third, nothing else in shot
- lighting: soft north window light from the left, overcast, no fill, gentle shadow under the rope
- aspect-ratios: 4:3; 1:1
- crops: 640x480; 390x390
- focal: 62% 50%
- anti-references: a harbour at sunset; a coiled rope styled as nautical decor; a hand holding rope toward the camera
- must-not-change: three strands, right-hand lay, a sealed cut end, white polyester
- factual-risk: a wrong number of strands or a braided cover would show the buyer a different product from the one the row sells
- max-attempts: 2
- candidates: 4
- capability: text-to-image

## `rope-double-braid`

- role: the product picture for the double braid polyester line
- why: double braid is the line that runs over a winch without hockling, and the cover-over-core construction is exactly what a photograph can show and a word cannot
- strategy: generate
- medium: photograph, close product study
- subject: a cut end of 12 mm double braid polyester rope on the same worn oak bench, the braided cover visible along the length and the parallel core just showing at the cut end
- materials: white polyester braid; polyester core; worn oak
- composition: close, from directly above, rope running left to right, cut end at the right third, matching the three-strand frame exactly
- lighting: soft north window light from the left, overcast, no fill — identical to the three-strand study
- aspect-ratios: 4:3; 1:1
- crops: 640x480; 390x390
- focal: 62% 50%
- anti-references: a harbour at sunset; rope styled as decor; a different light or bench from the other studies
- must-not-change: a braided cover over a separate core, and the same bench and light as every other line
- factual-risk: showing a laid rope here would misrepresent the construction the row is selling
- max-attempts: 2
- candidates: 4
- capability: text-to-image

## `rope-kernmantle`

- role: the product picture for the 11 mm low-stretch kernmantle line
- why: kernmantle cannot be hand-spliced and must be terminated with a knot, which is a consequence of the sheath-over-parallel-core construction the picture shows
- strategy: generate
- medium: photograph, close product study
- subject: a cut end of 11 mm low-stretch kernmantle rope on the same worn oak bench, the tight woven sheath along the length and the parallel white core fibres just visible at the taped cut end
- materials: woven polyester sheath; parallel nylon core; worn oak
- composition: close, from directly above, rope running left to right, cut end at the right third, matching the other studies exactly
- lighting: soft north window light from the left, overcast, no fill — identical to the other studies
- aspect-ratios: 4:3; 1:1
- crops: 640x480; 390x390
- focal: 62% 50%
- anti-references: climbing or outdoor-sport styling; a harbour; a different bench or light from the other studies
- must-not-change: a smooth woven sheath with parallel core fibres at the cut, and the same bench and light
- factual-risk: showing a laid or braided end would contradict the row's own note that it cannot be hand-spliced
- max-attempts: 2
- candidates: 3
- capability: text-to-image

## `bench-measure`

- role: the one wide picture on the page, at the top, establishing that this is a counter where rope is cut to length
- why: the whole shop is a length. A visitor has to understand in one look that the unit is a metre off a coil, not a packaged item
- strategy: generate
- medium: photograph, wider bench scene
- subject: a chandler's cutting bench seen from just above and to one side — a boxwood folding rule open along the bench, a coil of pale rope at the far end, a hot knife resting, a pencilled ticket
- materials: boxwood and brass rule; manila-coloured rope; worn oak bench; brass hot knife
- composition: three-quarter view from above, bench filling the frame edge to edge, rule running into the frame from the left, coil top right, no horizon and no room behind
- lighting: soft north window light from the left, overcast, one working lamp warm at the right edge
- aspect-ratios: 16:9; 4:5
- crops: 1280x720; 390x488
- focal: 40% 55%
- anti-references: a styled flat-lay with props arranged in a grid; a harbour or boat visible; a person in shot
- must-not-change: the rule must read as a graduated measuring rule, since it is what the page's argument rests on
- factual-risk: a decorative bench with no measuring instrument would make the page's central claim -- that rope is sold by the metre and cut while you wait -- unsupported by its own picture
- max-attempts: 2
- candidates: 4
- capability: text-to-image
