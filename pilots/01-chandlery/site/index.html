    Which line for which job

    Riggers arrive knowing the job rather than the rope. This is what we would hand across the counter, and why.
    The jobWhat we would hand you
    A mooring line that takes a snatch load 14 mm eight-plait nylon Nylon stretches, so the surge goes into the rope instead of into the cleat. It plaits flat, which is why it feeds out of a locker without kinking.
    A sheet or a halyard running over a winch 12 mm double braid polyester The cover turns on a drum without hockling and it keeps its round section under load, so it grips in the hand and in a clutch.
    A working line you intend to splice yourself 12 mm three-strand polyester The lay opens to a fid and closes again. An eye splice is an evening on the bench and it does not need a machine.
    A line that must not stretch under load 11 mm low stretch kernmantle The core runs parallel inside the sheath, so the length you cut is near enough the length you get. It will not take a splice, so plan for a knot.
    A heaving line or a pot marker that has to float 14 mm three-strand polypropylene Nothing else on this rack floats. It is off the rack at the moment and there is no coil to cut from.

    How a cut is made

    Worth knowing before you give us a number, because a cut length is yours the moment the knife goes through it. The coil lengths on the rack are what was left after this morning's count, so they move during the day.

    The minimum is 3 m
    Below that the twine and the bench time cost more than the rope, so the counter will not take the order. Offcuts under 3 m sit in the box by the door and they are free.
    Whipping is £1.80 a cut
    Both ends, twine and a hot knife. A polyester end left raw will unlay itself inside a week of real use, and a braided cover will milk back off its core.
    The batch goes on the ticket
    Every length comes off a coil with a tag on it. The batch number printed on your ticket is the batch on the breaking-load certificate, and the certificate is on the counter if you want to read it before you pay.
    A cut length cannot come back
    We cannot sell 17 m of rope to the next person through the door. Measure the job twice, add for the splice and the knots, then give us the number.
    We will not print a working load
    The breaking load is what a sample of that batch broke at on a machine. What you may safely load is a fraction of it, and the fraction depends on the job, the knots, the angle and the weather. Ask at the counter and we will work through it with you.
    Nothing here lifts people
    None of these five lines is rated for lifting or holding a person, including the kernmantle, which is the one that looks as though it should be. That is a different certificate and a different shop.
