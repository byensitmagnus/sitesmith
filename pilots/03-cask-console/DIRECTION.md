# DIRECTION — Stalbridge cask desk

**Chosen: C, the board.** Built from empty. No palette, class name, row anatomy or table
carried over from the version that failed two blind rounds.

## The signature

**Severity is the only hierarchy, and it is said three ways at once.** What is late is first in
the list, carries a coral spine down the left of its card, and puts the loudest word in the row.
A cellarman crossing the room reads the order and the colour before reading a single figure.

- signature-selector: .spine, .state
- signature-min-share: 2

## Why the previous direction was abandoned rather than improved

The old board scored a median production-readiness of 2 with four criteria below the floor. The
findings were specific and all of them are structural:

- Every mobile row printed its data on top of its own illustration. The list was unreadable.
- The state that was **not yet a problem** was the loudest thing in the row, and the only real
  control was the dimmest.
- One accent marked both a pressed filter and a cask state.
- The rows sat in **no order at all** — not state, not due date, not gyle, not alphabetical.
- A reviewer said that with the copy removed it was the stock dark operations table, and named
  the same row anatomy the rope counter used.

## What the rebuild does instead

**Ordered by operational severity, in code.** `rank()` sorts late, then due today, then out on
trade, and within a band the latest first. It is asserted in the journey, not left to taste.

**Cards, not table rows.** The rope counter's anatomy — a drawing, a name, right-aligned
figures, a button at the far right — appears nowhere here. A consignment is a card with a
severity spine, a size block, an identity block and its own book-in row.

**The controls are on the page.** Condition and ullage sit in every card at all times. Nothing
opens. A cellarman in gloves gets controls, not a button that produces controls.

**The size glyph carries a quantity.** Firkin, kilderkin and barrel are drawn at heights in the
cube-root ratio of 9, 18 and 36 gallons, so the shape of a row says how much beer is at that pub
before a digit is read.

**It remembers.** A booking is written to local storage and survives a reload. A cellar desk
that forgets what was booked when the screen is refreshed is not a tool, and the journey asserts
the reload.

## Axis record

- composition: a standing header with the counts that matter, then a severity-ordered stack of
  cards; no hero anywhere
- type: one system sans throughout, with tabular figures for counts, gyles and ullage and
  nowhere else
- colour: cool near-black panels; coral for late, amber for due today, an outline for on trade,
  green only for what has come back
- imagery: functional — drawn glyphs proportioned to real volume, and one cellar texture behind
  the header at 18 % that carries no claim
- rhythm: one continuous stack of equal panels divided by ground rather than by rules

## What the page refuses to do

- No hero, no marketing photograph, no pint.
- No line-drawn technical illustration doing page-carrying work.
- Amber never marks both a control and a state. Controls are ink; states are the severity
  colours; the two vocabularies do not mix.
