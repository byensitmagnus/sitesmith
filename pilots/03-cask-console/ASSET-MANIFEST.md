## The glyphs carry data, which is why they are drawn and not generated

A firkin is 9 gallons, a kilderkin 18, a barrel 36. The three glyphs are drawn at heights in the
**cube-root ratio of those volumes** — 0.63, 0.79, 1.00 — so the shape of a row says how much
beer is at that pub before a digit is read.

Equal-sized glyphs would tell a cellarman that four firkins and four barrels are the same amount
of beer, which is wrong by a factor of four. That is the factual risk the plan names, and it is
the reason this is `drawn` rather than `generate`: a model cannot be relied on to hold a
numerical ratio, and the ratio is the whole point.

## The one photograph, and where it is not allowed

`cellar-ground` sits behind the standing header and nowhere else. It is at 18 % opacity under a
gradient, it carries no claim, and no figure is ever set on top of it. One concession to the
room, made quietly.

Accepted on the first pass — three candidates, no iterations against a ceiling of two.

## What is deliberately not here

No hero. No photograph of a cellar, a pub, a pint or a dray. No line-drawn technical
illustration doing page-carrying work: that was the chandlery's old failure and the foundry's
old failure, and both were called one shared studio method by blind reviewers.
